Family Office Lists Vacancy for Next-Generation Principal; Job Description Strategically Omits the Word 'Born'

The position, the office confirmed, had always been filled, but the paperwork had simply never been told.

EM Elena Marsh
Head of Research 2026-11-13 · 2 min read

Maya Rivas has been an executive assistant at the Voss Family Office for eleven years. Her job this week: post an internal job listing for the role of next-generation principal. The listing requires no resume, no references, and one qualification Maya is not allowed to put in writing, which is that the candidate has to share DNA with the person who signs her paycheck.

Maya typed up the description. She listed the duties as "strategic vision setting" and "capital stewardship." The actual duty, she knows, is being born to the right people and remaining alive. She printed the listing and taped it to a door that only three people walk through, one of whom is the heir, whose job interview consisted of his mother asking him if he wanted lunch.

The heir's name is Connor. He is twenty-four. Maya orders his lunch most days. He has never asked her what she makes. She has never told him, because if he knew, he might feel bad, and then one of the other assistants would have to comfort him, and then nobody would get the lunch order right.

Connor showed up for his "interview" in sweatpants. Maya was asked to sit in the room and take notes. The questions were: "Do you want the job?" and "Are you going to do anything weird with the money?" Connor said yes to the first and no to the second. The second was not true, but the office felt it was the right kind of not true.

“Connor has been around the office since he was twelve years old. He knows the culture. The paperwork is just formalities. The health insurance started immediately. That's how a family office should work.”

— a family office employee who respects the succession

Maya is responsible for onboarding Connor. She printed his employee handbook and left it on his desk. He asked her if there was a cafeteria. She told him he can order whatever he wants. He said no, I mean like, a place to sit. Maya said yes, there are six of them, all empty. Connor said sweet.
