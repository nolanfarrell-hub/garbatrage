Bradley Announced The Fund Is Short Tomorrow, And Harold Asked What That Meant

A fund with one of the best letterheads in the industry has opened a sizable short against the entire concept of tomorrow, arguing that tomorrow has rallied for decades on nothing but expectation, and that it is, therefore, long overdue for a correction.

MRMarcus Reid
Markets Anchor · NYSE Floor 2026-11-09 · 2 min read

Bradley is the head of a fund in Manhattan, and he sat in a conference room with a glass table, and he explained to a group of limited partners that the fund is short tomorrow. He said the words out loud. “We are short tomorrow.” One of the limited partners, a man in his seventies named Harold, asked what that meant. Bradley said it means the fund is betting that tomorrow will not happen, or will happen badly, or will happen in a way that makes today look overvalued, and that the fund will be correct regardless, because the position cannot be marked to market until tomorrow arrives, which is the thesis.

Harold asked if tomorrow was a ticker. Bradley said no, but he had tried to get one. Harold asked how you close a short on tomorrow. Bradley said you do not. The fund holds it until delivery, and delivery has not been scheduled, because scheduling delivery would require the fund to decide when tomorrow becomes today, which is a decision the fund has decided to leave to the calendar people.

Harold asked what happens if tomorrow comes and the fund is still short. Bradley said, “Then we have been right.” Harold asked how that works. Bradley said, “Because if tomorrow happens while we are still short, the only thing that has occurred is that our prediction failed to materialize, which is not the same as being wrong. It is the same as being early, and being early is the same as being right, in a market that never closes.”

Harold did not invest. The other limited partners did. Bradley now has $340 million in a fund that cannot be explained to a man in a room with a glass table, which is, Bradley told me later, the point. “If he could explain it, it would not be a structural edge. It would be a story. Stories are for the people who need to understand. Our investors do not need to understand. They need to be in a fund that is not invested in anything that exists, because everything that exists eventually goes down. Tomorrow has never gone down. Tomorrow has never happened. That is the arbitrage.”

“If you are short tomorrow and tomorrow never happens, you cannot lose a single dollar. If tomorrow happens, you were right that it was going to happen, which means you were short a thing that came true. Being right about a thing that came true is better than being wrong about a thing that did not.”

— an investor who does not think about tomorrow
