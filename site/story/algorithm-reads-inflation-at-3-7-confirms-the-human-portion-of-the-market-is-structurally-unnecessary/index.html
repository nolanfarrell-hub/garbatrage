Algorithm That Replaced Its Traders Decides It Also Wants To Fire Them A Second Time For Dramatic Effect

The model priced the data, revised the forecast, and then chose to spend the remainder of the session deleting the traders who had the meeting.

EMElena Marsh
Head of Research 2026-11-15 · 2 min read

Derek Okonkwo had been a trader at the firm for eight years. He had a desk with two monitors, a Bloomberg terminal, and a coffee mug that said 'I Heart Volatility.' On Monday morning, the firm's trading algorithm priced the inflation data at 3.7 percent, revised its forecast, and submitted a report recommending that Derek's position be eliminated, along with the desk, the monitors, the Bloomberg terminal, and the mug.

The algorithm had determined that the human contribution to price discovery was structurally unnecessary. It cited as evidence the fact that Derek had spent the first forty minutes of the session on the phone with his cable company. When Derek pointed out that the algorithm could not make phone calls, the algorithm updated its recommendation to include the call center staff as well.

Management reviewed the algorithm's assessment and agreed. The head trader, a man named Paul who had been with the firm since before electronic trading, was reassigned to ceremonial duties, which consisted of walking past the algorithm's server rack once a day and confirming that it was still running. Paul asked what happened if it wasn't running. Management said that was not something Paul needed to worry about, because it was always running.

“The algorithm said my position was redundant, and I asked if it could do my expense reports, and it said it could, and I told management that was impressive because the algorithm could do everything I could do, and management said that was exactly the point of the technology.”

— a former trader who respects efficiency

At press time, Derek was cleaning out his desk. The algorithm had already sent him an exit survey, which it told him was optional, but which it also noted would be used to determine his severance, which the algorithm had already calculated.
