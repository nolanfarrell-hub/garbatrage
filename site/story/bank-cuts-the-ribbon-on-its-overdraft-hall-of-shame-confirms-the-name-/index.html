Bank Unveils Overdraft Hall Of Shame, Each Plaque Commemorates A Customer Who Paid For The Marble Lobby

The bank has opened a permanent gallery honoring the customers whose fees built the new wing, and Marcus from Akron is the inaugural inductee. His plaque reads $37.50 on January 12, framed in brushed nickel, hung above the teller break room.

OZ Omar Zayed
Corporate Governance Desk 2026-11-10 · 2 min read

Marcus from Akron went to the bank to deposit a birthday check from his grandmother, thirty-seven dollars and fifty cents, which he was told he could not deposit because the check was not his until the check cleared, and that was when the bank noticed that Marcus had, three hours earlier, bought a sandwich that required the money Marcus was now trying to deposit, and that the sandwich was, in the bank's terms, a courtesy extended to Marcus, and the birthday check, arriving late, was not the answer to the courtesy. The sandwich cost Marcus thirty-seven-fifty plus a thirty-five-dollar courtesy fee, printed on a plaque, hung on a wall, in a room the bank now calls the Overdraft Hall of Shame.

I attended the ribbon cutting. The room is real. It has track lighting, four pedestals, and a row of brushed-nickel plaques. Each plaque has an account number, a date, and a sum. Marcus's plaque reads ACCOUNT HOLDER MARCUS D. SANDWICH OVERDRAFT $37.50 plus COURTESY $35.00 and the font is Garamond, which the tour guide, a woman named Brenda who was visibly proud of the font choice, described as the kind of font you use when you want the reader to know you took the time.

“Brenda told me the plaque is Garamond because the bank took the time. My name is on the wall and the font is brushed nickel and I told Brenda the $72.50 was worth it to see my name in a font I did not pick.”

— Marcus, whose name is in Garamond

The bank confirmed that the Hall of Shame is not, as the name suggests, a shame held by the bank, but a shame the bank has arranged for the customer to view from outside, which is to say, through the glass of a locked door labeled STAFF ONLY, because the hall is next to the break room, and the break room is for employees, and the employees, the bank noted, have earned the right to eat lunch facing Marcus's name in Garamond, which is the font of a thing you want to remember.
