ING Unveils €1.65 Billion Buyback and 400 Job Cuts

Arthur Winthrop, 59, signs his own exit and calls the redundancy a graceful rotation.

EMElena Marsh
Head of Research 2026-11-19 · 4 min read

Arthur Winthrop, 59, signs the exit memo that makes 400 roles redundant and calls his own departure a graceful rotation, the same week ING announced a €1.65 billion buyback from the same part of the balance sheet that used to pay people. The two numbers sit in different paragraphs of the press release but come from the same ledger. The buyback costs the bank €1.65 billion and pays the shareholders who will still be there after the 400 have left. Winthrop calls the whole spread a restructuring and holds the door. The buyback is a machine that feeds itself. The cuts are a machine that feeds the buyback. The bank calls the whole thing streamlining, and 'streamlining' is just the word the bank uses so the memo does not have to say fired, which is the bank's whole approach to language: make the number of people smaller and the fucking sentence cleaner.

Pays: Arthur Winthrop, who called it a rotation.
