Estate Planners Now Offer A Plan Where Your Heirs Inherit A Curated Version Of You, Minus The Part Where You Yelled

A family office, having noticed that the most valuable asset a person leaves behind is no longer the house but the edit, has begun bequeathing heirs a gently revised ancestor, so that the dead can finally give the eulogy.

TN Tessa Nguyen
Economy & Data Reporter 2026-11-09 · 2 min read

I talked to an estate planner down in a big office that smelled like leather and maybe a little bit like decisions, and he told me that the old way, leaving your kids the cabin and a water-damaged box of receipts, is over. The new way, he said, is you leave them a version of you that you curate, like a highlight reel, but for a person.

He had this folder, and in the folder, he had a whole grandfather, and the grandfather did not yell at the cable company even once, because the grandfather in the folder was the grandfather you requested, the one that told stories about the war and then politely stopped. "We had to remove the part where he yelled about the thermostat," the planner said, genuinely proud. "The heirs do not want the thermostat story. They want the version of you that would advise them warmly, and then, this is key, shut up."

"The estate is not where the money lives. The money lives in the memory, and the memory is a property you can remodel, unless the heirs knew you, in which case it is condemned." — Elena Marsh, Head of Research

The service is expensive, which the planner says is the point: an heir who gets a curated you will never find out about the real you, because the real you, he assured me, is now optional, and frankly, extraneous. He sealed the folder with a stamp that said CERTIFIED and looked very trustworthy.
