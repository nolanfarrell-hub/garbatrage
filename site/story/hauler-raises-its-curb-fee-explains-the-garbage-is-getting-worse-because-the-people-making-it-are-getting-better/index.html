Hauler Raises Its Curb Fee, Explains The Garbage Is Getting Worse Because The People Making It Are Getting Better

A sanitation firm, citing record contamination, said rates must rise to handle a stream so degraded it no longer resembles recycling, and urged households to consider that the waste, in a very real sense, is them.

EM Elena Marsh
Head of Research 2026-11-11 · 2 min read

Terrence McDade stood at the curb in his bathrobe at 6:14 in the morning and watched the truck skip his can for the third Tuesday in a row. He had put a pizza box in the recycling again. He knew better. He had known better the first time. The pizza box is not recyclable. The pizza box is, in a very specific way, Terrence.

Route supervisor Maurice Litt has now flagged 11 houses on Terrence's block with a notation the company calls “the bloom.” The bloom is not a flower. The bloom is what the garbage does to the garbage that is already inside the garbage when the garbage sits too long waiting for the truck that will not take it because of the pizza box.

“I look at what they put out and I see who they are,” Litt said. “Terrence McDade is a pizza-box-in-the-recycling guy. That is his personality now. His wife knows. His kids know. The guy who drives behind me on the route knows. You cannot be a different person next week. The pizza box is who you are.”

The new rate, which the company calls a “sort-incentive adjustment” and Terrence calls “another $9 a month to feel worse,” is designed to fund interpretation: what the hauler now calls the part of the job where it figures out what you meant. “We are translation services for your denial,” Litt said.

Terrence is now considering composting. He is also considering moving. He is also considering, for the first time in 40 years, buying a vegetable he does not have to peel from a box.

“I told my wife the recycling is a promise to the planet. She said the planet can take a promise. The pizza box cannot. The pizza box is going in the trash from now on, and I am going to therapy.” – Terrence McDade, Former Curbside Optimist
