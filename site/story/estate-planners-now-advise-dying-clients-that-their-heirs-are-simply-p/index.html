Estate Planner Calls Dead Man's Children 'Pre-Earners,' Family Feels Better About Not Getting the Money Until They Die Themselves

Linda Bowen sat in a conference room and was told that her late father's estate would not be hers to inherit. It would be hers to "vest in." She asked what that meant. The estate planner said it meant she could think about the money, as long as she did not touch it.

Linda Bowen's father died on a Tuesday. On Wednesday, Linda sat in a conference room at a law firm that smelled like old paper and newer grief. Across the table sat a man named Richard, who had the kind of smile that makes you think he is about to sell you something you do not want, which is exactly what he was about to do.

"Your father was a grantor," Richard said. "You are not a beneficiary. You are a pre-earner."

Linda asked what that meant. Richard explained that the money was not hers yet. It would vest over time. She asked how much time. Richard said that depended on several factors, including her age, her father's age at the time of his death, and the market's performance over the next thirty years, which nobody could predict, which was why the trust was structured to not let her have any of it until they could.

“I am a pre-earner — that means I have been working toward my inheritance my whole life by staying alive. It sounds way better than beneficiary, which just sounds like somebody died.”

— Linda, who is vesting

Linda's brother Mark, sitting next to her, nodded. Mark had always been good at nodding at things he did not understand. "So I am a pre-earner," Mark said. "What does that make me, exactly?" Richard smiled. "You are a senior pre-earner," Richard said. "You are closer to the money than your sister, because you are older, and closer to the age at which your grantor passed, which means you are closer to becoming a grantor yourself."

Linda looked at Mark. Mark looked at Linda. Neither of them was closer to any money. They were both in a conference room, being told that their father's money would remain their father's money, even though their father was dead, because the trust had a schedule, and the schedule did not include them for at least another decade, provided they kept pre-earning, which is what the estate planners call staying alive.

"Congratulations," Richard said. "You are now pre-earners. The money is not yours. But the opportunity to wait for it is. And that, in a way, is the real inheritance."
