Billionaire Calls His 20% Cut a Tip, Has Tipped Nobody Since 1987

The carried interest still lands every quarter. The man still calls it a gratuity.

OZOmar Zayed
Corporate Governance Desk 2026-11-19 · 4 min read

Cecil Hartigan, 68, of Greenwich, takes 20 percent off every fund he raises, calls the money a tip, and has not actually tipped a human being since 1987, which he will tell you is the whole thesis. Over forty years that 20 percent has come to just north of $10 billion, calculated on capital raised and not on anything the money ever did. The rule does not care if the fund makes a nickel.

Listen to me. The 20 is the tip. That is what you are buying the room for. You want to sit at this table, you leave the 20 on it, and I do not have to say thank you, because a table that costs 20 of every dollar is a table you asked for. I have never once left a tip in my life. That is the point. The tip is a pump, and I am the one who gets it wet.

Mr. Hartigan says this without irony, which is the closest thing to a substantive position he has ever held. When a portfolio company he owned went bankrupt in the third quarter, the 20 on the money that entered it had already been wired to an entity with no address and a single officer, who is Mr. Hartigan.

The tip does not go down when the room burns. The tip is collected in advance, like a coat check for a coat that was never worn. He calls it generosity. What he is paying for, he will tell you, is the position from which the tip is taken, and he wants you to understand how much that costs, because in his view you are not really in the business until it costs you that much and you still do not give a fuck.

Cecil Hartigan keeps the tip.
