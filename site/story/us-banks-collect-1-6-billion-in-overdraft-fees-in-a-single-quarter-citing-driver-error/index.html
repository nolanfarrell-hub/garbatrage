Keisha's $6.87 Coffee Cost $41.87 Because The Bank Processes Happier Numbers Before Sadder Ones

Revenue rose 9.4% to a four-year high as lenders attributed the bonanza to a chronic shortage of balance on the part of account holders.

DW Dana Whitfield
Consumer Finance Reporter 2026-11-15 · 2 min read

Keisha Allen bought a coffee for $6.87 at the shop on the corner of her block. She paid with her debit card, because she had $42 in her account and the coffee was $6.87, which left $35.13, which should have been enough until payday. The bank disagreed. The bank saw the $6.87 and decided that Keisha had made a decision with insufficient regard for the balance, which the bank had arranged in an order that put the coffee first, and the $42 second, so that the coffee cost $6.87 and the insufficient-funds fee cost $35, and the whole thing cost $41.87, which was almost the entire $42, leaving Keisha with thirteen cents and a coffee.

Sterling National and Keystone Bank alone collected $590 million in overdraft fees in the third quarter, according to data from KBRA, an amount roughly equal to the rest of the twenty largest banks combined. A federal rule that would have capped the overdraft fee at $5 was approved and then rolled back in the same year, restoring the banks' right to call a $6.87 coffee a $41.87 financial event.

Keisha called the bank to ask about the fee. The representative, a woman named Tiffany who sounded like she had taken this call before, explained that the fee was assessed because Keisha's account balance had been insufficient at the time of the transaction. Keisha asked if the $42 had been in the account before the coffee. Tiffany confirmed it had. Keisha asked how $42 became insufficient to cover $6.87. Tiffany explained the order of operations. Keisha asked if the operations could be arranged differently. Tiffany said they could not, because the operations were standard.

"I asked the lady at the bank how a $6.87 coffee could cost $41.87. She said it was because the system processed the withdrawal before it processed the balance. I asked if the system could process the balance first. She said the system did what the system did, and the system was not a suggestion box." — Keisha Allen, Home Health Aide

At press time, Keisha had thirteen cents in her account and a cup of coffee that was, according to the bank's statement, fully paid for.
