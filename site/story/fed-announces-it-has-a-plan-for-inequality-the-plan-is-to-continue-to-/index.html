Fed Announces It Has a Plan for Inequality; the Plan Is to Continue to Have a Plan

The board, responding to sustained criticism, clarified that it remains 'very engaged' with the topic, and that its engagement consists primarily of being very engaged with the topic, which it feels is the appropriate next step.

PS Priya Sharma
Federal Reserve Desk · Washington 2026-11-05 · 2 min read

The Federal Reserve has a plan for inequality. I asked. It does. The plan, as articulated by the vice chair, is to remain, in her words, 'very, very focused' on the issue, which is precisely the plan you'd expect from an institution whose power over the problem is roughly equivalent to its power over the weather, minus the ability to at least be blamed for the weather.

Minutes from the last meeting show the word 'inclusive' used seventeen times and the word 'wage' used zero times in the context of anything that could be converted into it.

"We are monitoring the gap closely," said Elena Marsh. "And when I say monitoring, I mean we have a very nice chart of it."

Pressed on whether any tool exists to actually narrow the gap, the board noted that it has many tools, and that the tools' primary function is to be tools, which is what having a plan means to an institution that has never once had to live within one of its own projections.

The board then adjourned. The gap did not. The meeting, by all accounts, could not have gone better.
