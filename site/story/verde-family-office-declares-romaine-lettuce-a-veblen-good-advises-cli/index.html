Verde Family Office Declares Romaine Lettuce a Veblen Good, Advises Clients to Serve It on Velvet

A family office reclassifies lettuce from commodity to luxury good, publishes sommelier tasting notes, and commissions a fork that costs more than the produce it lifts.

MR Marcus Reid
Markets Reporter 2026-11-16 · 2 min read

Romaine lettuce reached $3.49 per pound in October, up 7.3% in a single month, surpassing the per-pound cost of chicken — a pricing inversion that Verde Family Office is treating not as an anomaly but as a permanent reclassification of leafy greens into the luxury-goods category.

In a client memo titled "The Romaine Revaluation," Verde managing partner Julian Croft argues that lettuce has transitioned from commodity to Veblen good, meaning demand should theoretically rise as the price increases. The firm recommends clients display romaine on a velvet pillow rather than eat it, and has published sommelier-style tasting notes describing a "2026 California romaine with notes of soil and regret." The memo advises clients to "stop eating salad in front of the help" and compares conspicuous lettuce consumption to "wearing a Rolex in a soup kitchen."

Verde has begun assembling a lettuce-futures desk and is exploring a partnership with a Manhattan cutlery maker to produce a fork that retails for $3.95 — forty-six cents more than the head of romaine it is designed to lift. At a tasting event held in a private dining room with no natural light, three romaine leaves were served on a bed of crushed ice with a printed provenance card.

"We have moved beyond the era of salad as sustenance. A client who serves romaine without ceremony is signaling that they do not understand where we are as a society." — Julian Croft, Managing Partner, Verde Family Office

At press time, Verde was developing a romaine-aging cellar in Chelsea, despite the fact that lettuce spoils in roughly five days and the cellar's climate-control system alone costs more than the entire annual romaine harvest of California.
