Verde Family Office Declares Romaine Lettuce a Veblen Good, Advises Clients to Serve It on Velvet

Romaine lettuce hit $3.49 a pound, up 7.3% in one month — now more expensive per pound than chicken. Verde Family Office says the price is the point.

MRMarcus Reid
Markets Reporter 2026-11-16 · 2 min read

The romaine arrived on a velvet pillow carried by a Verde Family Office associate wearing white gloves. It had been trimmed to a single perfect leaf, stem removed, and placed under a museum-quality halogen spot calibrated to 3200 Kelvin. "The chlorophyll bloom is exceptional for a November hydroponic," said senior partner Arthur Verde, gesturing at the leaf as though it were a Rothko. "You are not looking at lettuce. You are looking at a Veblen good whose price elasticity has inverted. The price is the point."

Verde advised clients to stop eating salad in front of household staff, comparing a $3.49 head of romaine to "wearing a Rolex in a soup kitchen" and recommending that salads be consumed in a dedicated romaine salon with closed-circuit cameras and a lock on the refrigerator. The firm has opened a "romaine-aging cellar" in Chelsea — a climate-controlled room kept at 38 degrees and 95% humidity, staffed by a former MoMA curator who logs each head's provenance (farm, harvest date, soil pH, distance from a major highway) on numbered cards bound in calfskin. The lettuce spoils in five days. "Spoilage is a feature, not a bug," Verde said. "It reinforces scarcity. It creates urgency. A head of romaine that lasts two weeks would not trade at this multiple."

The climate-control system for the Chelsea cellar costs $14,000 a month in electricity alone — more than the annual value of California's entire fall romaine harvest at current prices. Verde called the ratio "encouraging" and said the firm is staffing a "lettuce-futures desk" that will allow clients to buy forward contracts on salad greens they will never eat, settled in cash, stored in a room they will never visit. Verde Family Office has commissioned a $3.95 salad fork from a Danish silversmith, which costs more than the lettuce it will pierce. "That's the point," Verde said. "The fork is the signal. The lettuce is the credential."

“I paid $3.49 for a head of romaine and I felt good about it. If it was cheap everybody would have it and then what's the point of buying it?”

— a shopper in the organic aisle, 47, who carries her lettuce in a reusable bag

At press time, the Chelsea cellar had logged its first head of romaine as spoiled — provenance card filed, disposal fee invoiced to the client's capital account, replacement ordered at the forward price of $3.72 a pound, and the empty velvet pillow returned to its dust cover under the halogen spot, waiting.
