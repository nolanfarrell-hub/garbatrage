Susan Spent $429 On A Garbage Can So Margaret Would Think She Eats Fresh Vegetables

The hauler, following a regional rollout, has introduced a deluxe trash receptacle whose primary function is not containment but discretion, allowing residents who can afford it to put their waste away with both hands.

OZOmar Zayed
Corporate Governance Desk 2026-11-09 · 2 min read

Susan lives in a neighborhood where the garbage goes out on Wednesday, and on Wednesday at 7 a.m., Susan watches her neighbor Margaret carry her bags to the curb, and Margaret watches Susan do the same, and they both know, because both of them have looked, that the other one had a frozen pizza box this week again. The exchange is silent, weekly, and total.

Susan bought the premium bin. It cost $429. It has a locking lid. It only opens for the hauler. The hauler has a special truck that opens the lid with a locking arm that costs more than Susan’s first car. The bin is not for the garbage. It is for the fact that Margaret saw the frozen pizza box, and Susan could not live with Margaret having seen it, so Susan built a wall around the frozen pizza box and called it waste management.

The executive who designed the bin, a man named Craig, told me at a demonstration that Susan is his best customer. “She is not paying for containment,” he said. “She is paying for the idea that her neighbor cannot see what she threw away. The neighbor knows anyway. The neighbor knows because both bins are on the same curb. But the locked lid creates a fiction, and the fiction is what we sell.”

Two blocks away, the older part of the neighborhood, where the bins are cracked and the lids do not close, has submitted a request. They want a bin that does not leak when it rains. The department that handles requests from older neighborhoods has confirmed receipt of the request, and will deliberate on it. Susan’s premium bin was delivered within three business days.

“Four hundred and twenty-nine dollars for a garbage can is a lot of money. But my neighbor does not see my garbage anymore. She sees a nice bin. The garbage is the same. The witness is cleaner. That is worth four hundred and twenty-nine dollars.”

— Susan, who does not want her neighbors to know what she throws away
