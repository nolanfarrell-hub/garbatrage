Wealth Manager Tells Client She Cannot Enjoy Her Money Because It Would Violate Wash-Sale Rules

Patricia Holloway wanted to buy a sandwich with some of her own money. Her wealth manager, a man named Derek, told her that would require a 30-day cooling-off period and a signed form. Patricia asked if the form tasted as good as the sandwich. Derek did not laugh.

Corporate Governance Desk 2026-11-06 · 2 min read

Patricia Holloway has a lot of money. She has enough money that she does not need to think about money, which is the whole point of having money. But Patricia made a mistake. Patricia wanted to enjoy some of it.

She called her wealth manager, Derek. Derek works at a firm that charges Patricia a fee to keep her money from doing anything that would make Patricia happy. "I want to buy a sandwich," Patricia said. "A good sandwich. With good bread and good meat and the kind of cheese that costs extra."

"I am sorry," Derek said. "That would be a wash-sale violation."

Patricia asked what that meant. Derek explained that deriving pleasure from one's own gains constitutes taking a position in the self. Opening a position in the self without declaring it is improper. The thirty-day rule applies. The cooling-off period is mandatory. The location, if a location is required, is the firm's.

Patricia asked if she could at least look at her money. Derek said she could look at it, but she could not feel anything about it. "Looking is passive," Derek said. "Feeling is active. Active management is not permitted in this account. You agreed to the terms in the subscription agreement, page forty-seven, subsection C, which you signed without reading, which is your right, but which also binds you to the position that your money is not for enjoying."

Patricia asked what the money was for, if not for enjoying. Derek said the money was for growing, which is different from enjoying, and that the growth itself was the enjoyment, which is a thing that people who have never enjoyed anything say to people who have never grown anything.

Patricia hung up. She did not buy the sandwich. She sat in her kitchen, looking at her money on a screen, feeling nothing, which is what Derek wanted, which is what the firm wanted, which is what the rules wanted. The money grew. Patricia did not enjoy it. The system worked.

"We are not saying a client cannot enjoy their wealth," Derek said, in a memo distributed the next day. "We are saying they should enjoy it the way we do: quarterly, after approval, in a spreadsheet, with the totals checked twice, silently, alone."
