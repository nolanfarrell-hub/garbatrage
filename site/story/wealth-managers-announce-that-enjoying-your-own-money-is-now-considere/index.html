Wealth Managers Announce That Enjoying Your Own Money Is Now Considered A Wash-Sale Violation

The revised guidance clarifies that deriving pleasure from one's own gains, however modest, constitutes taking a position in the self, and that the self, per current rules, must be allowed a thirty-day cooling-off period before the position can be recognized, ideally in a location of the firm's choosing.

OZ Omar Zayed
Corporate Governance Desk 2026-11-06 · 2 min read

Now, I am not a financial advisor, and I am not a tax attorney, but I do have a strong opinion, which is the same thing as long as nobody asks. And my strong opinion is this: enjoying your own money, even a little bit, has officially become an accounting problem.

The new guidance, issued with the firm's signature blend of apology and margin call, clarifies that deriving pleasure from one's own gains now counts as a position in oneself, and that opening a position in oneself without declaring it is, at minimum, improper, and, at market close, a wash sale. The thirty-day rule applies. The cooling-off period is mandatory. The location is, as ever, the firm's.

Clients, informed that their enjoyment must now be held, netted, offset, and, ideally, deferred, have asked where, exactly, in the balance sheet one books the happiness. The firm declined to answer, citing the possibility that the happiness has not yet vested, and that vesting, in the current environment, is a word we increasingly use to mean maybe.

“We are not saying a client cannot enjoy their wealth. We are saying they should enjoy it the way we do: quarterly, after approval, in a spreadsheet, with the totals checked twice, silently, alone, and in a font that is primarily for viewing.”

The firm has proposed a subscription tier under which clients may, for a fee, enjoy their own money on a precise schedule, and projected that this tier, the first to sell people back feelings they already own, will outperform the market, which it is now required to.
