CEO Says Company's Best Customers Are People It Fired, Who Think Their Severance Was a Dividend

Brad Kelleher stood at a podium and told the room that his most loyal, most consistent buyers are the ones he laid off. They believe their severance checks were dividend payments. Nobody has corrected them. The room applauded.

TN Tessa Nguyen
Economy & Data Reporter 2026-11-06 · 2 min read

Brad Kelleher is the CEO of a company that sells a thing. The thing does not matter. What matters is who buys the thing. Brad stood at a podium during an investor day and told the room the truth. The truth is that the company's best customers are the people it fired.

"They keep buying," Brad said. "They buy quarter after quarter. They believe their severance checks were dividend payments. We have not corrected this impression. We will not correct this impression. It is the deepest loyalty metric we have ever measured."

"A laid-off worker who thinks her severance is income," Brad continued, "is a customer who will never complain. She will never ask for a discount. She will never wonder why the product costs what it costs, because to her, the product was free. She already paid herself for it, using money we gave her to leave."

A guy in the back raised his hand. His name was Tom. Tom had been laid off six months ago. Tom had been buying the product every month since. Tom had been telling his friends about the product. Tom had been using his severance check, which he thought was a dividend.

"Is the severance check a dividend?" Tom asked.

Brad looked at Tom. The room looked at Tom. Brad smiled.

"Yes," Brad said.

Tom nodded. Tom sat down. Tom was satisfied. Tom bought more of the product that afternoon. Tom will buy the product until his severance runs out, and then Tom will buy the product using money he does not have, because Brad has figured out something that should be illegal but is not: a customer who thinks she is being paid to buy will never stop buying, even after the payments stop.

"We are not exploiting a misunderstanding," Brad said, closing the presentation. "We are honoring a conviction. Tom believes in us. We have given Tom no reason not to believe in us. That is a sustainable relationship. That is the kind of customer we want. That is the kind of customer we make."
