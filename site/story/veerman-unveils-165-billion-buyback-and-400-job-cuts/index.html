Veerman Unveils €1.65 Billion Buyback and 400 Job Cuts

Arthur Winthrop, 59, signs his own exit and calls the redundancy a graceful rotation.

EMElena Marsh
Head of Research 2026-11-19 · 4 min read

Arthur Winthrop, 59, signed the 'streamlined' exit from Veerman's wholesale unit the same week the bank announced a 1.65 billion euro buyback, and he could not sign fast enough, because a bank telling you that you are redundant while handing 400 of you the door in the same breath is the closest the profession gets to a compliment. He called his own redundancy a graceful rotation, which is how a man names the thing that carried him out on a gurney of his own making.

The 399 who were not Arthur did not get a handshake as generous, and Arthur does not ask. The buyback is the bank buying itself back to a shape that no longer has a line for them, and the structure is simpler in exactly the way a family is simpler after the kitchen catches fire. He reads the numbers on the way to the airport and does not once look back at the building.

'Veerman is simplifying the bank,' the statement said, and Arthur felt the simplification personally, the way you feel a knife at the right distance.

The buyback paid for his retirement and the 399 can bill the difference to the streamlining in general, because streamlining is just the word you use when you want gore to sound clean. Shit, the bank gutted the floor and called the wound a diet.

Pays: Arthur Winthrop, who called it a rotation.
