Thornwood Partners Frames Negative Real Wages as a Loyalty Discount, Advises Employees to Consider It a Character-Building Opportunity

A wealth firm's internal memo reframes seven consecutive months of negative real wages as a loyalty discount, recommends charging employees an oxygen fee, and concludes that the paycheck now arrives with an invoice attached.

CR Claire Reyes
Consumer Finance Reporter 2026-11-16 · 2 min read

Real wages fell for the seventh consecutive month in October, with nominal wage growth of 3.1% trailing inflation of roughly 4%, a gap that Thornwood Partners has rebranded as a "loyalty discount" in an internal memo circulated to the wealth firm's high-net-worth clients.

The memo, obtained by Garbatrage, advises clients to reframe shrinking purchasing power as "character-building fiscal discipline" and suggests that employees paying more for less are effectively "paying your employer for the privilege of working there." One passage notes that a worker earning $50,000 in nominal terms who faces $52,000 in effective costs has "contributed $2,000 to the firm's rent fund, which is both generous and tax-inefficient on their part." The memo recommends clients consider charging employees a "break-room oxygen fee" to align the workplace experience with market realities.

The firm's reasoning extends to housing: a landlord raising rent is described as implementing a "neighborhood appreciation fee," and the memo advises property owners to frame each increase as "an investment in the renter's character." The document concludes with the observation that "the ladder is also more expensive now" — a line Thornwood managing partner Richard Langley reportedly delivered at a client dinner to sustained applause.

"Your employees are not underpaid. They are making a voluntary contribution to your margin, and you should thank them for it — preferably in writing, so they feel recognized." — Richard Langley, Managing Partner, Thornwood Partners

At press time, Langley was drafting a follow-up memo titled "Why Your Paycheck Now Comes With an Invoice Attached," which he described as "the logical next step in wage transparency."
