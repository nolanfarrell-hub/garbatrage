Founder Calls Stock Lockup a 'Pool' He Forgot He Built

Rafferty cannot sell his shares for five years, says his best years are in there.

MRMarcus Reid
Markets Anchor · NYSE Floor 2026-11-19 · 4 min read

Rafferty O'Dell, 44, called the five-year lockup on his own company's shares a pool when the deal was signed, and has spent three of those years in it, unable to sell, describing the water as cold. The pool was his own idea. He proposed the lockup to the investors as a sign of confidence, and has since confirmed that the confidence was real at the time and that he would like out of the pool now.

The company's stock listed at $38 and trades at $14. Mr. O'Dell's shares are worth roughly $41 million on paper and cannot be sold until the fourth quarter of next year, which he has described as the quarter the pool empties. He has asked the board twice whether the pool could be drained early. The board said no.

Mr. O'Dell told a conference he had built a good pool. The conference was about AI, not liquidity, which he noted after he had finished speaking. He told reporters that the pool is cold because he built it in the winter of the deal, and that he has spent the time since trying to build a heater.

The heater is a second offering the company filed for, which would dilute his shares further but raise the cash he cannot access any other way. Mr. O'Dell has described the trade as heating the pool with the water in the pool, which is a bastard of a plan. When it was pointed out that this would make the pool colder, he said that was exactly the kind of cold the pool was already, and that he had gotten used to it, which he described as the entire problem.

Rafferty pays the price.
