Landlord Cheers Tax Increase That His Tenants Will Pay

The man who owns forty units on Reading Road has never been happier about a piece of paper that will cost him money.

OZOmar Zayed
Corporate Governance Desk 2026-11-16 · 2 min read

Gary Pemberton is sitting in the members' lounge of the Cincinnati Athletic Club with a county auditor's notice spread across the mahogany table like a winning paddle at Bancroft's. His coffee has gone cold. He does not care. Hamilton County has just completed its triennial reassessment, and residential property values in his zip code are up twenty-seven to twenty-eight percent — ninety-nine percent of single-family homeowners affected — and Gary is grinning at the number like it is a personal check from the county commission. He owns forty units on Reading Road. He has owned them since 2003. None of them have new roofs. None of them have updated kitchens. The parking lot at the Avondale property is held together by patch asphalt and hope. But the county says they are worth more — a third more, in some cases — and Gary believes this is the single best piece of news he has received since the city announced the streetcar.

“My landlord raised the rent because his taxes went up. The county says the building is worth a third more than last year so why shouldn't I pay a third more? That's just math.”

— a tenant on Reading Road, 36, who received the rent-increase notice

He has not calculated what his 2027 property tax payment will be. He will do that later, when his accountant calls, and he will be briefly annoyed, and then he will remember that he can pass it through to his renters, who cannot frame a county notice and smile because they are not at the Cincinnati Athletic Club having a good morning. The school levy will pass. The roads will maybe get paved. Gary's portfolio will be worth more on paper, and that is the only paper he reads. At press time, Gary was on hold with the county auditor's office to request a printed certificate he could laminate, because 'digital reassessment notices do not look good on a wall.'
