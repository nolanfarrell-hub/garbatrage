Fed Worried the Labor Market Has Gotten Too Settled to Count Anyone As Working

Federal Reserve officials are worried the labor market has gotten so settled that a person can no longer be counted as hired, laid off, or doing anything at all — the state the desk calls 'a peace we did not ask for.'

TN Tessa Nguyen
Economy & Data Reporter 2026-11-14 · 2 min read

Greg Furnish has not been fired in eleven months, and the Fed has noticed. Hiring is low, layoffs are low, and the number of unemployed people actually finding work has fallen to the kind of number that used to mean something was on fire. Officials convened to ask whether this calm was a victory or a mistake, and settled on describing it as unsettling, which is their word for a situation that does not require them to do anything.

The problem, as the desk explains it, is that nobody is moving. A worker stays at his job because every other job looks like the same job, and every other job looks like the same job because nobody leaves, and nobody leaves because the jobs are the same. Economists have a name for this. They call it the problem, and they have asked if anyone else knows what to do about it.

Greg, for his part, said he liked not getting fired. He works in a warehouse, stacks the same boxes, and drives home to the same apartment. He has stopped googling the going rate for warehouse work because the going rate is his rate and his rate is not changing. The Fed, he figured, could worry about the calm; he would just work.

The market has achieved full settlement. Nobody leaves, nobody starts, and nobody is hired or let go, which is exactly how you get a headline that says everything is fine. — Economist who declined to name the number that would make him say otherwise.

Officials say the state is likely temporary. Greg says he has a birthday coming up and intends to spend it at work, where he has now been for a while.
