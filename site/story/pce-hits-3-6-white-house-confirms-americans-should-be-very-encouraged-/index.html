PCE Hits 3.6%, White House Confirms Americans Should Be Very Encouraged By A Number They Cannot Eat

As inflation stays above target, officials note the figure is down only if you look at the goods people used to buy.

TN Tessa Nguyen
Economy & Data Reporter 2026-11-14 · 2 min read

Tanya Morrison put the chicken back. Then the orange juice. Then she swapped the name-brand cereal for the bag that looks like somebody got evicted from a label design meeting. The total at the Food Lion on H Street was still higher than last week's total, and last week's total had already made her stand at the register doing subtraction out loud.

The cashier, Doreen, told her the eggs had gone up again. Tanya asked why. Doreen said the thing cost more now, and the people who decided that were not in the store. Tanya asked if the people who decided that were anywhere she could call. Doreen said she did not know where they were, but it was not answering.

At the same hour, the White House announced the Personal Consumption Expenditures index had climbed to 3.6 percent, a figure officials described as reason for encouragement, because while it was above the target, it was below the old number, provided one did not also count this week's grocery trip, which, the official noted, was a separate transaction.

"They told me the number was down. I showed them my receipt. They said the receipt was anecdotal. I said my account was empty, which is the most anecdotal thing I have ever felt." — Tanya Morrison, Certified Nursing Assistant

At press time, Tanya had put the milk back too and was deciding whether to pay the light bill or eat this week, a choice the White House described as personal responsibility, which is what they call it when a number you cannot eat finally takes food out of your mouth.
