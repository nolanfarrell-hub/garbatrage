Brackenridge Family Office Advises Formula-Fed Infants to Trade Up to the Premium Tier

Basic formula rose 5.1% in a single month — the largest increase since the Bureau of Labor Statistics began tracking the category in 1997. Brackenridge Family Office sees a generational buying opportunity.

DW Dana Whitfield
Investing Reporter 2026-11-16 · 2 min read

The infant arrived at the conference room under a blanket carrying a leather satchel. A Brackenridge junior analyst had placed a bottle of Premium Plus Gentle at the infant's side of the table — the $12.65 SKU, not the $12 Basic the infant had been drinking for six weeks. "We've run the comps," said managing partner Caroline Brackenridge, sliding a term sheet across the polished walnut. "Basic is a value trap. The spread is sixty-five cents. You're leaving money on the counter."

The infant, who cannot sit upright unassisted and whose total asset base consists of three board books and a teething ring shaped like a key, was asked to sign a "birth-to-weaning" auto-escalating subscription that adjusts the can price monthly against the the government dairy-futures index. The contract does not include an infant opt-out clause. The infant's legal guardian — present at the table and visibly tired — noted the infant had not yet formed object permanence and could not reach the doorknob. "Those are exactly the characteristics we look for in a long-term holder," Brackenridge said, and circled the early-termination penalty.

Brackenridge's "formula ladder" locks the infant into 18 consecutive months of escalating can prices, with a step-up provision if the infant transitions to solid foods before month 14. The infant, under no circumstances, may wean. "The exit penalty is structured as a clawback of every can consumed to date, at the Premium tier's forward price," Brackenridge explained. The infant, who had begun to cry, was noted by an associate as "expressing concern about price discovery."

“I told my sister that the basic formula going up 5.1 percent was actually a good thing because it meant the market was signaling that premium was the better allocation, and she said the baby was three months old, and I said that was the perfect age to start thinking about premium positioning.”

— a sibling who understands premium positioning

At press time, the infant had been assigned a "formula-futures position" expiring on a kitchen counter in 72 hours, the contract priced at $12.65 with a mandatory roll into the November Premium blend, the can unopened, the infant asleep, and the carry cost billed to the infant's nonexistent checking account at a 12% annualized fee.
