Estate Tax Leaves Heir $41 Million Lighter, He Wants the Weight Back

Bartholomew Finch-Duarte paid $41 million and now wants the money back, understands what it was for.

EMElena Marsh
Head of Research 2026-11-19 · 4 min read

Bartholomew Finch-Duarte, 61, paid the $41 million estate tax on his father's utility business and has since described the payment as a fee for everything his father owned, which he has confirmed includes him. The tax was due in full before the will could be read. Mr. Finch-Duarte paid it from an account set up for exactly that purpose by the man whose estate the account was now taxing, and the setup has struck him as needlessly personal.

The business was a utility his father assembled over sixty years and three mergers. The tax ate 23 percent of a figure the accountants had been careful to make large. Mr. Finch-Duarte has quoted the 23 percent at every dinner since, describing it as the amount the state charged for the privilege of his father having planned.

Mr. Finch-Duarte told his lawyer he had no idea what went into a bill that big; the lawyer walked him through it the previous week. When the lawyer explained that the bill was the market value of the assets, Mr. Finch-Duarte described it as the state valuing his father's life at the price of the father's house and his own inheritance at the price of the rest.

He has kept every page of the filing, including the page that itemizes the deduction for the exact amount the estate spent on the accountants who arranged for the deduction. When that was explained to him he said the state had found a way to tax his father twice for the same person and had credited the estate for half of it, the cunts. The estate has not moved on, and neither has Mr. Finch-Duarte, who is still in the house the tax almost took, using a stapler his father used on a document the stapler helped produce almost forty years ago.

Finch-Duarte pays the tax.
