Buyout Firm Generates Synergy by Laying Off the Very People Who Sign the Paychecks, Flags It as a Cost Center

A portfolio company announced headcount reductions at its own facilities after the sponsor identified overlap between the employees and the work.

OZ Omar Zayed
Corporate Governance Desk 2026-11-12 · 2 min read

A private equity firm this week realized the fullest expression of operational synergy, laying off staff at a company it owns and financing the transactions that made the company's own customers wait to be served.

Executives explained that the redundancies were administrative, the savings were immediate, and the morale, like the projected earnings, was forward-looking.

"We looked at the value chain and we saw the same name on two paychecks," the operating partner said. "We did not look at the front door. The front door is a going concern."

The firm booked the savings in a holding vehicle, and has begun reviewing whether the company's remaining employees are also, technically, an asset that could be optimized and returned.

"They said the cuts would make the store run better, and it does run better, mostly because the people who used to answer the phone are now the people who used to own the phone." — Simone Doherty, Rideshare Driver
