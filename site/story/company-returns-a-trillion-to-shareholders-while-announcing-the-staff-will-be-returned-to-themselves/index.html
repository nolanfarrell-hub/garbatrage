Company Returns A Trillion To Shareholders, Returns Frank From Columbus To Himself In A 3-Minute-42-Second Zoom Call

Frank from Columbus was laid off on a Tuesday Zoom call that lasted three minutes and forty-two seconds. The same Tuesday, the company announced a trillion-dollar buyback. Frank was told the company believes in its people so much it decided to buy itself instead, and that Frank, being a person, has been returned to himself.

OZOmar Zayed
Corporate Governance Desk 2026-11-11 · 2 min read

Frank from Columbus sat in his home office, which is also his dining room, which is also the room where his daughter does her math homework on a laptop from 2019, and he watched a Zoom screen with thirty-seven other rectangles, each one a face he had seen in a hallway he would never walk down again. The rectangle in the corner was a man named Brad from HR. Brad said the company was returning Frank to himself. Brad used the exact phrase returned to yourself. Frank asked what that meant. Brad said it meant Frank was no longer an employee, and that the burden of being an employee, having been lifted, was now a burden Frank could carry on his own time, as himself.

The same Tuesday, the company announced a trillion-dollar buyback, which is the largest repurchase in the history of the firm and which the company described, in the press release Frank read while updating his resume on a phone with fourteen percent battery, as a demonstration of confidence in the firm's long-term value. Frank, who had worked at the firm for six years and who had been told, in his exit Zoom, that the firm valued him, asked Brad whether the firm valued him at one-trillionth of the buyback. Brad said that was not a helpful way to think about it.

“Frank told Brad the self pays for its own health insurance. The company spent $1 trillion buying itself instead of me, and I told Frank the self is the cost center now and the self is the one who pays the $600 monthly premium.”

— Frank, returned to himself

The analysts called the buyback visionary. They called the layoffs a headwind. Then they called the layoffs visionary and the buyback a tailwind. Then they called the entire quarter a margin story, which is the word for when the company you work for decides the best thing to buy is the thing that does not require you. Frank called it a Tuesday. Brad called the severance a reallocation toward Frank's future, which Brad said was bright, and which Frank, looking at his phone, said would need to be funded separately.
