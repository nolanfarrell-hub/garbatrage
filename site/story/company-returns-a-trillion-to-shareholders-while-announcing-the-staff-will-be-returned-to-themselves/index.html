Company Returns A Trillion To Shareholders While Announcing The Staff Will Be Returned To Themselves

A record repurchase program will buy back every outstanding share it can reach, and to fund it the firm will relieve headcount of the burden of attending, which it described as a strategic reallocation toward the future, i.e., out.

OZ Omar Zayed
Corporates & Governance 2026-11-11 · 2 min read

The board approved the largest repurchase in the firm's history, funded by the usual line items, and the largest line item, as always, was the people, who were asked to appreciate the optics of leaving before the dividend.

Employees were told the company believed in them so strongly that it was buying itself instead, on the theory that the firm's greatest asset was the firm, and the firm, in a stroke, had agreed.

Analysts loved the ratio: every dollar that no longer had to be paid was now a dollar that could be spent buying the shares of the no-longer-paid, which they called a virtuous circle and the departing staff called a doorway.

Management assured the market the cuts were surgical, and the market, which was holding the scalpel, applauded the steadiness of the hand.

Nothing changed for the shareholders, which was the entire point of the undertaking.

“We are not cutting costs. We are redeploying people into the future, and the future is a button that buys our own stock, and it never misses a paycheck because it is not one of the paychecks.” – Regina Holt, Chief Financial Officer
