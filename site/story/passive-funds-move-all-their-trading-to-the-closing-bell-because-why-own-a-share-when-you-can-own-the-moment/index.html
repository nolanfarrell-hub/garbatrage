Passive Funds Move All Their Trading to the Closing Bell, Because Why Own a Share When You Can Own the Moment

Index vehicles now do nearly all their buying at the day's final auction, a practice regulators call elegant and investors call 4:00 PM.

DW Dana Whitfield
Portfolio Strategy 2026-11-12 · 2 min read

Index funds have consolidated the bulk of their trading into the closing auction, a strategy analysts say is brilliant, because nothing says conviction like buying everything at exactly the same second as everyone else who owns the same thing.

Fund managers explained that official net asset values are calculated at the close, so any trade before the close risks a tracking error, which is the industry's word for accidentally spending your own money at a different price than the benchmark.

"Why would we decide all day," one manager asked, "when the market has already decided for us at the end, and we have agreed with it."

The result is a market that does all of its thinking in the final moments, like a student who waited the whole term and then read the entire syllabus during the exam.

"We used to make decisions. Now we simply arrive at the closing price like everyone else and call it a thesis. It is the closest thing to peace I have ever known." — Avery Loomis, General Partner, Inertia Fund
