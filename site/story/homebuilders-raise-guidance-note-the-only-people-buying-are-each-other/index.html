Homebuilders Raise Guidance, Note the Only People Buying Are Each Other, Which Is Working Out Fine

The sector reported a strong quarter driven entirely by transactions in which builders purchased each other's spec inventory, creating a market that is, by all available measures, real.

TN Tessa Nguyen
Consumer & Earnings Desk · New York 2 min read

The homebuilding sector had a quarter it would call strong, and it would call it that the way a man walking briskly down a dark street compliments himself on his pace.

Sales beat estimates, and the beat is explained entirely by a bright spot: builders buying other builders' completed inventory, at full close, in cash, from one another. When asked if this is a sustainable footing for the American housing market, the sector said it does not think the question is a question it needs to answer.

"The feedback loop is beautiful," said Dana Whitfield. "Every builder is simultaneously the market and the thing being marketed. Nobody is left out. Well, almost nobody."

That last part, management concedes, is the first-time buyer, who remains priced out but has been told this is a market, and markets, the sector reminds us, are not in the business of being joined by everyone.

Guidance raised. Demand described as 'robust.' The homes are owned by the people who built them, which is progress, if you squint at it from the right distance.
