Investors Pile Into An ETF That Trades Solely On The Anxiety Of Hourly Workers, In A Historic And Frightening Symmetry

The fund's mandate is to buy exactly what a shift worker fears at 4 a.m., then sell it at 5, then buy it back at 9, and it is outperforming the broader market because, the fund explains, anxiety is the one thing that is always in season.

PS Priya Sharma
Washington Bureau · Fed & Rates 2026-11-09 · 2 min read

Let me walk you through this. There is an exchange-traded fund, and its underlying asset is not a company and not a commodity. Its underlying asset is the specific, felt dread of a person who has 11 dollars on a Tuesday and has been told the schedule might change. And it is up. It is up seventeen percent. And we are all watching the chart like it is a miracle, instead of asking who the fund is dancing with.

The fund describes its holdings as hourly-fear futures, and operates on a simple, disclosed schedule: it purchases the fear at 4 a.m., when the phone is a threat and the rent is a sausage that has already been squeezed, and it sells it at 5, when the fear is at its broadest and cheapest, and buys it back at 9, when the fear has been re-priced by the morning's reality, which is that nothing has improved but the afternoon is coming.

"We are not exploiting the anxious. We are merely indexing them. There is an important distinction, which is that indexing is a fee we are paid." — Elena Marsh, Head of Research

When asked whether the fund would do well if the workers did well, the manager paused, and you could see the gears. "The fund would do nothing," he said, finally, with real regret in his voice. "We have not found an ETF for that, because nobody has ever, in our backtest, had a good Tuesday."
