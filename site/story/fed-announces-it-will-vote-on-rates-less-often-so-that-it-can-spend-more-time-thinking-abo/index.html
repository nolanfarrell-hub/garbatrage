Fed Announces It Will Vote On Rates Less Often, So That It Can Spend More Time Thinking About The Households Without Having To Do Anything

Starting in 2027 the central bank will hold strategy meetings instead of rate votes, explaining that the extra time allows the committee to consider the household consumer in greater depth, a consideration it described as best conducted at some distance.

PS Priya Sharma
Washington Bureau · Fed & Rates 2026-11-09 · 7 min read

The Federal Reserve has announced that beginning next year it will hold fewer meetings at which it actually sets interest rates, freeing the committee to devote the recovered time to what it called 'broad monetary policy strategy.' Economists were broadly supportive, noting that the strategy had been working well with the rates left exactly where they already were.

Held rates steady at 3.75% to 4.00% this quarter — its second consecutive pause after a September hike — and now wants the calendar itself to acknowledge the achievement. Under the new schedule, eight meetings shrink to seven in 2027 and six in 2028, with the freed sessions devoted to considering the economy rather than touching it.

At a press conference, Chair Kevin Warsh was asked whether fewer decisions meant faster relief for borrowers who had spent three years waiting for any. 'The speed of the decision is not the product,' he said. 'The product is the deliberation. If anything, a household waiting on a rate cut benefits from watching the deliberation be thorough.'

Analysts note that taking meetings away from rate decisions has the structural elegance of making the committee's accuracy harder to track: active decisions can be wrong, while strategy that shifts nothing is correct by definition.

Money markets nevertheless moved to price a 75% chance of a December hike, which officials described as a sign the new schedule was already being taken seriously by the people who benefit from it.

"A corpse can hold a position, but only a household loses it with dignity."
