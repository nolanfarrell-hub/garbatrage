Goodwill Is What You Overpaid. The Impairment Is Morning After

The company wrote off $2.1 billion and called it a refresh.

TRUE FIGURE
$2.1 billion
Paid to be a different company. Written off as a refresh.
EVEmmett Voss
Goodwill Room Clerk 2026-11-17 · 4 min read

I am Emmett Voss, a clerk in the goodwill room, and I write the numbers down as they un-matter. On Monday the company decided the $2.1 billion it had paid to be a different company was an error that had finally been noticed, so we wrote it down to zero and called the write-off a refresh, like a coat of paint on a building being demolished. Nobody in the build room believes the number anymore. That is my job to believe it for them. I type it as an asset, then I type it as a loss, and I am the one who has to look at both numbers with the same hands. My supervisor calls it not a loss but an alignment, the word we use for a figure that has changed its mind. When goodwill goes, you are not losing value; you are admitting you were never paying for value — you were paying for the memory of having wanted it. The company is worth $18.3 billion without the goodwill it paid for, which is what it was before it got ambitious.

My prayer is a ledger. It writes: paid. It writes: gone. It never writes: why. Fuck.
