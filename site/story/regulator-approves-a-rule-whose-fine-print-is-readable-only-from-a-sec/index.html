Regulator Approves A Rule Whose Fine Print Is Readable Only From A Second Address You Do Not Own

After an extended review, the regulator has certified the new disclosure regime as fully compliant, a determination reached in part because the operative paragraph, visible only from the property line of a mailing address currently in escrow, was deemed by review staff to be technically not there, and therefore impossible to violate.

PS Priya Sharma
Washington Bureau 2026-11-03 · 2 min read

I have read a great many legal documents in my time, and I would like to report that I have now found the first one I cannot read, because reading it requires standing somewhere I am not allowed to be, which the document, read from where I am, does an excellent job of pointing out.

The rule's critical provision, the one governing who owes what to whom and at what hour, is printed in a typeface the filing calls Perspective Serif, which renders correctly only from a single viewing point: the front step of a condominium across the street, currently in escrow under a name the document declines to print.

Review staff confirmed they read the paragraph. When asked from where, they confirmed from the step, and when asked how, they declined to elaborate, citing the step's own terms of service, which became effective, they said, the moment anyone approached. The paragraph, for its part, remains in perfect compliance, having committed no violation it can be seen to commit.

Opponents of the rule, who are many, mostly stood outside and shouted at its general vicinity, which the rule, in a separate section, classifies as a demonstration, and therefore, under the same rule, subject to a fee. The fee is disclosed in the paragraph across the street. The property is expected to close within the quarter.

The regulator declared the rollout a success, citing the complete absence of complaints, which it attributed to the rule's design, and which others attributed, more simply, to the walk.
