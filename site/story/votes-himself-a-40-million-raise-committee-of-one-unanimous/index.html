Votes Himself a $40 Million Raise, Committee of One Unanimous

The committee met, approved, and was the CEO.

OZOmar Zayed
Corporate Governance Desk 2026-11-19 · 4 min read

Marcus Pell, 57, approved his own $40 million raise last week through a compensation committee that consists of exactly one member, himself, and he has described the vote as unanimous. The committee met for forty minutes. Mr. Pell has confirmed the meeting was a one-man gathering that he attended in two chairs, so that the table had a chairman and a board at the same time.

Who knows what I am worth? Me. That is the whole committee. I sit in one chair, I sit in another chair, I sign from the top of the page and the bottom, and the two of us just jacked each other off to $40 million and called it corporate governance. The shareholders? They are the wife I do not bring to the party. By the time they find out what I paid myself, I have already spent it and filed the paperwork at the top, where the good news lands.

The shareholders were not consulted. Mr. Pell described this as a feature, because in his experience a shareholder who is consulted either asks for the number to be smaller or asks what it is for, both of which are questions the market does not reward.

He has confirmed the raise brings his total pay to $97 million, that he is the only member of the committee, and that the committee's minutes read that Mr. Pell did excellent work this quarter. He signed the minutes on behalf of the committee, which he has described as two signatures, one for the founder and one for the man who checked the founder's work, who is also him. The committee, which is to say the man, has never once looked at the number and winced, and the number, which is to say the man, has never once been anything but shit.

Marcus Pell signs both letters.
