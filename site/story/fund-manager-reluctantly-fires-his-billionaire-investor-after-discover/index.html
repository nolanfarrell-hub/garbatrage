Fund Manager Reluctantly Fires His Billionaire Investor After Discovering He Actually Enjoys The Money

A billionaire told his fund manager 'thank you' and referred to his investment as 'mine.' The manager said the relationship had become untenable and returned the capital. A new client has been found who confirmed he does not think about the money at all.

MR Marcus Reid
Markets & Funds · NYSE Floor 2026-11-08 · 2 min read

You meet a certain type of person in this business. The moment they start talking about what the money is for, you know you have to let them go. That is what happened here. A billionaire made the mistake of enjoying his own money. He had to be fired.

His name was Arthur Penn. He had been with the fund for twelve years. He never caused trouble. Then one day, during a review, Arthur looked at the quarterly number and said, and I quote, 'This is really good. Thank you. I feel good about this.' The room went quiet. The manager wrote a letter that afternoon. It said the relationship had become untenable. Arthur had breached protocol by acting like the money was his.

The manager returned Arthur's entire position. Arthur now manages his own money. He does not know what he is doing. He is losing money. He says he feels worse than he has in years. The fund manager says this proves the point. The money was never Arthur's. It was a guest that lived with him for a while.

The money is not his. It was never his. It is a thing that lives with him for a while, and the second he starts acting like it belongs to him, he has broken the one rule that makes the whole arrangement possible, which is that we all pretend it does not. — Elena Marsh

The fund has already replaced Arthur with a new client. The new client was asked how he thinks about his wealth. He said he does not think about it at all. The manager smiled. The new client passed the test. Arthur Penn is at home staring at his brokerage account, having been told he was too happy. He is not happy anymore.
