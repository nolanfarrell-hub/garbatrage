As Mortgage Rates Climb, Industry Reassures Renters Their Housing Is Now Officially No One's Problem

With ownership unaffordable for five million more Americans, lenders confirm rent is the answer and is not, technically, housing.

CR Claire Reyes
Consumer Finance Reporter 2026-11-14 · 2 min read

NEW YORK—As the average 30-year rate reached 7.4% and ownership slipped out of reach for millions, the consumer-finance industry moved to reassure renters that their situation is not, in the regulatory sense, a housing crisis, because the unit they live in is not, in the accounting sense, an asset they own.

“Renting is absolutely a form of housing, which is why we do not call it housing on the documents,” a spokesperson for a national landlord trade group said, adding that tenants should think of their rent as the most stable pricing the market has ever offered, since it only ever moves one direction and every apartment is technically affordable if you do not look at your checking account.

Economists noted that ownership costs now consume a record share of income precisely because renting has done such a good job of defining the alternative. The finance industry described the arrangement as a ladder, clarifying that on this ladder the first rung is the roof.

“My landlord asked me to apply for the apartment like a mortgage, and the apartment said no, which I think is the first time a building has asked me for my paperwork and then told me I don’t qualify to live in it.” — Ramon Delgado, Renter

At press time, the industry had confirmed that the best time to have bought a home was any time before the current era, and that the enemies of the current era were people who pointed that out.
