Market Rallies After Three Guys See a Number and Agree Not to Talk About It, One of Them Eats the Evidence

Jerry Maldonado saw the inflation print at 8:31 AM. He wrote it on a napkin. He ate the napkin at lunch. The Dow went up 200 points. Jerry's stomach hurts, but the market doesn't know that, and the market doesn't care.

MRMarcus Reid
Markets Desk · NYSE Floor 2026-11-05 · 2 min read

Jerry Maldonado works on a trading desk. His job is to look at numbers and then do something based on the numbers. At 8:31 AM on Wednesday, Jerry saw a number he was not supposed to see. It was the inflation print. It was early. It was on his screen. It was big enough to read. Jerry read it.

Jerry looked left. A guy named Marcus was looking at his screen. Marcus had seen it too. Jerry looked right. A woman named Priya was looking at her screen. Priya had also seen it. The three of them looked at each other. Nobody said anything. Jerry wrote the number on a napkin. He folded the napkin. He put the napkin in his pocket.

“I wrote the number on a napkin at 8:31 AM and ate the napkin at lunch. The market went up 200 points — my stomach hurts but the trade worked.”

— Jerry, who ate the evidence

The market rallied. Traders who had not seen the number bought on the theory that the traders who had seen the number looked calm, and calm means everything is fine, and fine means buy. Jerry watched the tape go up. He did not buy anything. He did not sell anything. He just sat there with a napkin in his pocket, knowing a thing nobody else knew, which is, for a trader, the best feeling in the world, and also the worst.

At lunch, Jerry took the napkin out of his pocket. He looked at the number. He folded the napkin again. He put it in his mouth. He chewed. He swallowed. The number was gone. Jerry was the only person in the world who had ever known that number, and now the number was in his stomach, being digested, becoming nothing.

The market closed up. Jerry went home. His wife asked how his day was. He said it was fine. He did not tell her about the napkin. He will never tell anyone about the napkin. The napkin is gone. The number is gone. The rally happened. Nobody knows why. That is the part Jerry loves most.
