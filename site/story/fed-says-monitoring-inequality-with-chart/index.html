Fed Says It's Monitoring Inequality, Which Means It Has a Chart, and the Chart Is Really Nice

Carol Jansen, the Federal Reserve's vice chair for things the Fed cannot fix, stands in a Kroger parking lot and explains that the central bank's position on inequality is that it has a position.

PSPriya Sharma
Washington Bureau, Fed & Rates, plus household costs (insurance, mortgages) 2026-11-05 · 2 min read

Carol Jansen went to Kroger on a Tuesday. She was out of half-and-half. This is not a metaphor. She needed half-and-half for her coffee. At Kroger, she saw a man counting quarters at the register to buy a single can of tuna. Carol put her half-and-half on the conveyor belt. The man put back the tuna. Carol did not put back the half-and-half. This is not a policy decision. It just felt, in the moment, like one.

Later that day, Carol sat at a long table in Washington and told a room full of reporters that the Federal Reserve is "very, very focused" on inequality. A reporter asked what that meant. Carol said it meant the Fed had a task force. The reporter asked what the task force did. Carol said it met. The reporter asked what it did when it met. Carol said it discussed the task force's mandate, which was to remain very, very focused.

“The blue part on the chart keeps getting bigger and the gray part keeps getting smaller. That is a beautiful chart — when the blue covers the whole thing, we will have achieved the monitoring goal.”

— Carol, standing in a Kroger parking lot

The board voted unanimously to continue monitoring. The word "inclusive" appears seventeen times in the meeting minutes. The word "tuna" appears zero times. Carol went home and made coffee. The half-and-half was fine. She did not think about the man at Kroger. She is a professional. She has a very nice chart.
