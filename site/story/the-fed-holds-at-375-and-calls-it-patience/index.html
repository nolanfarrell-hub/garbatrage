The Fed Holds at 3.75% and Calls It Patience

December's hike is 93 percent priced in. Rich borrowers read it as permission.

PSPriya Sharma
Washington Bureau, Fed & Rates, plus household costs (insurance, mortgages) 2026-11-19 · 4 min read

Tristan Overman III, 47, of Greenwich, floated $12 million at floating rates and calls the Fed's patience permission — the $187,000 a quarter bill is a flex, a sign of strength that says the hike is not a risk but the price of having borrowed right. The futures priced a 93 percent chance of a December quarter-point hike two weeks ago. The press release says the Committee is patient, which is Fed for we are raising in December. Everyone in the front row knows which quarter point the patience is about. This is how the world's most powerful monetary authority communicates an actual rate decision by refusing to say it, over a phrase that means the decision was already made. Tristan Overman is being patient along with the Fed, by which the Fed means he can take the hike until January, when the next one lands. The pause is real for exactly one more press conference. Then patience costs him $187,000 a quarter in extra interest, which is the fee for believing a rumor dressed as a policy statement, the whole goddamned thing. He tells the partners the philadelphia curve is his personal party trick and that a real borrower does not flinch. The flinch is the bill, arriving on schedule, and Tristan will tell you he never flinched once. The patience is the whole fee, and he pays it standing, and there is not a shred of shit in him for it.

Pays: Tristan Overman, once the patience expires.
