Passive Funds Mirror Benchmarks So Perfectly That Customers Ask Why Losing Everything Is Better Than Losing Less; Vanguard Confirms It Is an Excellent Question

Somewhere in the closing auction, a fund confirmed it was the index, and the index, citing precedent, confirmed it was the fund.

DWDana Whitfield
Portfolio Strategy 2026-11-13 · 2 min read

Tanya Orozco answers phones at Vanguard's retail support center in Scottsdale. Her job is to explain to people why their account went down when the market went down. Lately the calls have been getting harder, because the answer is no longer that the fund lost money. The answer is that the fund lost exactly what the index lost, which is the whole point, and the caller does not understand how losing all of it is better than losing some of it.

Tanya has a script. The script says to explain that passive investing means accepting market returns, which means when the market goes down, the fund goes down by the same amount, which is the tradeoff for lower fees. Callers ask her if the market going down is a market return. Tanya says yes. Callers say they thought a return meant you got your money back. Tanya says that is not how the industry uses the word. Callers say the industry can go fuck itself.

The fund Tanya fields the most calls about owns 3,500 stocks. It owns them in the same proportion as the index. The index, in turn, is calculated from the stocks the fund owns. Tanya has read the fine print. She has concluded that the fund and the index are now the same thing, which means the fund is benchmarked against itself, which means the fund can never underperform, which also means it can never outperform, which means the only thing Tanya can tell callers is that their money is doing exactly what math says it will do, which is not what anyone wants to hear.

The woman on the phone right now is named Carol. She is retired. She has $240,000 in the fund. She wants to know why it went down $8,000 this month. Tanya tells her it is because the index went down. Carol asks why the index went down. Tanya says because the stocks went down. Carol asks why the stocks went down. Tanya says because the market sold them. Carol asks who sold them. Tanya says other funds. Carol asks what funds. Tanya says the funds that track the index. Carol is quiet for a minute. Carol says so I am losing money because my fund and the other funds are selling each other the same stocks. Tanya says yes. Carol says that is stupid. Tanya says yes.

“If everybody owns the same thing, nobody is wrong. I put my retirement in the index. The index goes up over time. I don't need to beat the market. I just need to be the market. That's the strategy.”

— an index investor who stays the course

Tanya is not allowed to say that the whole system is a circle. The script does not have that answer. The script tells her to say the fund is operating as designed. Tanya says it. Carol says the design is stupid. Tanya does not disagree.
