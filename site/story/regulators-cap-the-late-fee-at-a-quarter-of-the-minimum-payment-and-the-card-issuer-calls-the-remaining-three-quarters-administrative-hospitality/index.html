Regulators Cap Late Fee at Eight Dollars; Couch That Cost $1,200 in 1989 and Broke in 1992 Has Now Generated $14,700 in Interest

Banks confirmed the consumer can now be late for substantially less, provided she remains late for substantially longer.

CR Claire Reyes
Debt & Fixed Income 2026-11-13 · 2 min read

Lou Brackett has carried a balance on his Visa card since 1989. He knows this because every statement includes a line that says what he has paid in interest since 1989. The number is $14,700. Lou does not have $14,700. He has $14,700 in interest. The principal, which started at $1,200 for a couch he bought when he was twenty-two and thought he would have a better job by now, is still $1,100. The couch broke in 1992.

The new rule caps late fees at eight dollars. Lou saw this on the news and thought finally. Then he read the rest of the article. The rest says the cap only applies to the late fee itself. The interest still compounds. The minimum payment still accrues. Lou's minimum payment is $68. The late fee was $39. Now it is $8. The bank called the remaining $31 "administrative hospitality," which Lou looked up and does not mean anything, except that it is on his bill now, under a separate line item.

Lou called the bank to ask about the administrative hospitality. The representative said it was a fee for the service of managing his overdue account. Lou asked if he asked for that service. The representative said it was provided automatically. Lou asked if he could decline the service. The representative said it was not optional. Lou asked what happened if he did not pay the administrative hospitality. The representative said it would be added to his balance and accrue interest at 24.99 percent.

Lou is sixty-one years old. He works at a hardware store. He does not think he will ever pay off the couch. The couch is gone. The interest is not. The new fee cap, Lou said, is like being charged $8 for the parking ticket instead of $39. You still owe the parking. You still cannot park there. You just get a receipt that stings less.

“Eight dollars is reasonable for a late fee. The administrative hospitality is just the cost of the processing. I was late. I should pay for that. The bank has to make money too. That's the agreement I signed.”

— a cardholder who accepts the new fee structure

Lou hung up. His bill is due Friday. He does not have the $68. He will pay the administrative hospitality instead, which he assumes will count for something, although from the call, he is not sure what.
