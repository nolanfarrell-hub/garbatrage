The Federal Reserve Retires The Word Zero Because Harold From Communications Could Not Say It One More Time Into A Microphone

The press release said rates would remain at zero, a word Harold has spoken into a microphone sixteen times. He told his wife at breakfast he could not do it again. The Fed has struck the word from its vocabulary.

Harold from the Fed’s communications office woke up at 4:30 AM on the day of the rate decision and stared at the ceiling. The press release was sitting on his nightstand via his phone. It said, in the third paragraph, that the federal funds rate would remain at zero. Harold has written that sentence sixteen times in the last four years. He has watched himself say it into a microphone sixteen times. He told his wife at breakfast that he could not say it again, and his wife said then do not say it, and Harold said that is actually the meeting today.

The Fed has officially retired the word “zero” from its internal and external vocabulary, effective immediately. The word will be replaced with the word “some,” which Harold proposed and the committee accepted without debate, because “some” sounds like a number that could become a larger number, whereas “zero” sounds like a number that has given up, and a central bank cannot sound like it has given up, even when the number on the screen is the number on the screen.

“I like the word 'some' way better than 'zero.' When my bank told me my savings rate was some, I felt like it could go anywhere — that is a lot more optimistic than hearing it is zero.”

— Harold, who said 'zero' into a microphone sixteen times

The market took the news of “some” as a signal but could not agree on what it signaled, because “some” is not a number. Analysts called the Fed’s communications strategy bold and opaque, which Harold’s boss confirmed was the entire point, because the one thing worse than saying zero is saying a number that makes you look like you have said zero, and “some,” Harold believes, is that number.

Harold went home at 6 PM, sat on his couch, and did not say any words at all for an hour. His wife asked how the press conference went. Harold said some. She asked what that meant. Harold said nobody knows, and that is the best part.
