Federal Reserve Completes Its Study Of Waiting For More Information, Announced The Study Itself Needs More Information

The central bank, having devoted twelve years to a rigorous review of the question of whether it should wait for more information before acting, has delivered its findings: it needs more information. Officials called the conclusion durable, data-dependent, and, in the quietest room in the building, a complete result.

PS Priya Sharma
Washington Bureau · Fed & Rates 2026-11-10 · 2 min read

The Federal Reserve has completed its twelve-year inquiry into whether it should wait for more information before doing anything, and the inquiry's conclusion, reached after considerable deliberation and several shorter inquiries into the inquiry, is that it needs more information, a finding officials described as satisfying and, in the low, proud voice of a child who has finished a hard dinner, complete.

The study, titled internally as the review of the review of the review, was commissioned after staff noticed that the committee's decision-making had settled into a rhythm, and that the rhythm was the sentence we will accumulate more information. Analysts praised the sentence's quality as a sentence, its patience as a posture, and its total absence of action as a feature, while noting that the information it proposed to accumulate had, over the same twelve years, developed a strong tendency not to arrive.

Asked whether the completed inquiry would now allow the Committee to finally act, officials clarified that the inquiry had concluded only that the inquiry was not the right place to hold the information, and that the information, once gathered, would be routed to a new process, overseen by a new committee, which is currently hiring for the person who decides which committee gets the information first.

I can stand in a doorway pretending to wait for something I'm not going to get for a very long time. The Fed has monetized that exact hobby, which I privately consider the closest thing to a job I have ever had, and I respect it immensely. – Marcus Reid, Markets Anchor, on the new process

Markets, which price in a conclusion the way they price in anything with a date attached, rallied briefly on the release, then sold off on the discovery that the report was itself a placeholder, a development the Fed defended as durable and said it would hold.

The next meeting will consider whether there is enough information to answer the question the prior meeting left open, which was whether there was enough information, and officials expressed confidence that there would eventually be enough information to answer the question of when there would be enough information, a confidence the Fed described, with a full mouth, as data-dependent.
