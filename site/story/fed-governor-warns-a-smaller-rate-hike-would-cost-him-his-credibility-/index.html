Fed Governor Warns A Smaller Rate Hike Would Damage His Credibility, Which Is Kept In A Room The Public Cannot Enter

Christopher Waller stood in front of a room full of bond owners and told them a quarter-point rate hike would damage his credibility. A half-point hike would protect it. He did not mention the people who owe money because they were not in the room, and the room was where the credibility was being held.

PS Priya Sharma
Washington Bureau · Fed & Rates 2026-11-11 · 2 min read

Christopher Waller, a governor of the Federal Reserve, walked to a podium in a room where every person in the audience owned at least two million dollars of something, and he told them about his credibility. He said he has one, and that he intends to keep it. He said that raising rates by a quarter point would put his credibility at risk. He said raising rates by a half point, which is twice as much, would protect it. He said the public, which does not own two million dollars of anything, would be fine, because the public, as he understands it, is resilient, and that resilience, in a governor's vocabulary, is the word for what the public is while the governor counts his credibility.

I asked someone on Waller's staff what the credibility looked like. She said it was not a physical object, and then she described it as a professional-grade credential, maintained through the careful practice of never being wrong twice in the same direction. I asked whether the public had a credibility, and she said the public did not have a credibility because the public did not need one, because the public does not set rates. I asked what the public had. She said the public has the benefit of the governor's credibility, which the governor, by choosing the half-point hike, would protect, for the benefit of the public, who cannot see it, cannot touch it, and cannot vote on it.

I asked the governor whether his credibility could be used to pay my mortgage. He said my mortgage was not a factor in the credibility calculation. I asked what was. He pointed at the room behind him, the one with the people who own two million dollars of something. The room, he said, is the calculation. The rest of us are the variable. – Keisha Barnes, Cleveland

The half-point hike was announced the next morning. Waller's credibility, by all accounts, remained intact. The public's mortgages, which are not a credibility, went up. Waller's staff confirmed the governor's credibility was now twenty-seven and a half points, and climbing, and that no member of the public, having been surveyed, could name the number of their own, which the staff said proved the point, which was that credibility lives in the room with the bond owners, and the bond owners, having paid for the room, are the only ones who need to see it.
