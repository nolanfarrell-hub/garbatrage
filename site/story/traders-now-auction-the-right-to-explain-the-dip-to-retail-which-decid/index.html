Market Opens Bidding for the Right to Explain Why You Lost Money, Retail Decides It's Cheaper to Just Be Sad

Denise Carter lost $2,000 on a trade. She received an email offering to tell her why, for $1,500. She did the math. She decided to feel bad for free. The trader who sent the email does not understand why she declined.

OZOmar Zayed
Corporate Governance Desk 2026-11-06 · 2 min read

Denise Carter has a brokerage account. She puts money in it. Sometimes the money grows. Sometimes the money shrinks. Last Tuesday, the money shrank by $2,000. Denise did not know why. That is normal. Most people who lose money do not know why. The difference is that this time, someone offered to tell her.

The email arrived at 3:47 PM. It was from a firm called ClearView Analytics, which is not an analytics firm. It is a marketplace where traders sell the right to explain why retail investors lost money. The winning bid gets a phone call, a slide deck, and eleven minutes of words like "normalization" and "repricing" and "rotation." The email offered Denise the explanation for $1,500.

“If somebody offers to tell you why you lost two thousand dollars for fifteen hundred, that is half off the answer. Not knowing why you lost the money is the real cost, and knowledge has a price tag on it.”

— a trader who paid for the explanation once

Denise declined the offer. The trader who sent the email, a man named Marcus, was confused. "She lost $2,000," Marcus said. "For $1,500, she could know exactly where it went. That is a bargain. That is closure. I paid $2,000 for an explanation once, and I didn't even lose any money. I just wanted to understand what happened, and now I understand it, and I am poorer, but I am wiser, and wisdom is worth something."

Denise, for her part, disagrees. "I lost the money," she said. "That is the explanation. I did a thing. The thing did not work. I do not need a slide deck to tell me that the thing did not work. The thing not working is its own explanation. It is the only explanation that matters. It is also free."

ClearView Analytics announced it will now auction the right to explain why the auction business failed. The winning bidder will receive a slide deck explaining that retail investors have grown too smart to pay for explanations they can manufacture for free. The auction closed $500 over ask. The winning bidder was ClearView Analytics itself.
