Traders Now Auction The Right To Explain The Dip To Retail, Which Decides It Was Cheaper To Feel Bad

A new marketplace lets institutions sell, to the investors who just lost money, the opportunity to be told why, with proceeds split between the explainer and the venue. Retail, after reviewing the deck, declined, concluding that it was substantially cheaper, and only slightly more painful, to feel bad on its own.

OZ Omar Zayed
Corporate Governance Desk 2026-11-06 · 2 min read

There is a transaction here, and it only works if you do not think too hard, so please, for everyone's sake, do not think too hard. A marketplace has opened that lets institutions auction, to the very investors who just lost the money, the right to be told why they lost it.

The winning bidder receives a call, a deck, and roughly eleven minutes of familiar vocabulary — normalization, repricing, rotation — which, the venue's pitch deck confirms, have never once in documented history explained the participation of the person doing the losing. That, according to the issuer, is the beauty of the product: the explanation and the loss were never related, and the retail buyer, quietly, has begun to suspect as much.

Early auctions cleared, then the retail bidder, reviewing the terms, declined to repurchase. The explanation, she said, cost more than the loss, and the loss, she noted, was already in the past, where it was, at least, hers. The venue called this the first recorded case of retail walking away from a deal that only hurt them.

“We were selling certainty, and retail has grown wise enough to know the only thing more expensive than a loss is the story you pay to hear about it. We have therefore closed the equity explainer book, and are opening it as a debt product, which is what it always was.”

At the close, the venue announced it will now auction the right to explain the decision to stop auctioning, which it described as its most honest product yet, and which sold instantly, to itself.
