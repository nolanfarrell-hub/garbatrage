CEO Priced to Leave, Companies the Buyout

$12 million to jump. The board holds the chute.

EMElena Marsh
Head of Research 2026-11-17 · 4 min read

Rita Soriano, 58, read her own $12 million golden parachute as the board's way of pricing her to leave before it wanted her gone, and she has spent the year packing. The clause is the parachute: it makes the fall look chosen, the way a man strapping on gear calls the ledge a plan. She has the offer in progress, and the board knows it, and nobody in the room will say the word exit where the minutes can hear.

The board calls it retention, which is the polite name for the thing it has priced. Rita calls the parachute the most honest line in her contract — it is the one sentence that tells her exactly what her leaving is worth, and worth is the only number the company ever says without wincing. The buyout was half-assed, she told her brother, the way she says everything she is leaving behind: with both palms flat on the table, priced and ready.

“The $12 million is not severance; it is the board pricing me out before the market does.”

— Rita Soriano

Pays: Rita. She was always priced to leave.
