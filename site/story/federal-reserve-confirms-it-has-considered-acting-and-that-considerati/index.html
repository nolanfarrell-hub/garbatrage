Federal Reserve Confirms It Has Considered Acting, And That Consideration, It Turns Out, Is The Entire Job

Officials confirmed that the committee had considered acting several times this quarter and that, for a central bank, considering is not a step toward acting but the whole of the work. Acting, they clarified, is what other countries do, and is widely felt here to be a rumor.

PS Priya Sharma
Washington Bureau · Fed & Rates 2026-11-04 · 2 min read

Now I want you to understand something about a central bank, and it's simple: the meeting is the job. The watching is the job. Sitting in a very nice building, looking at a very nice number, having considered it — that is the job. Acting is the part they don't do.

So when the Fed tells you it has considered raising rates, don't hear that as it is thinking about raising rates. Hear it as it has done its job. It has considered. It has considered thoroughly. It has considered to the point where the considering is a finished product.

The market, which had wanted acting, took the considering badly at first, then realized the considering was what it had been pricing anyway, and the two of them are now standing there, considering each other, which is the most comfortable either has been all year.

The committee declined to clarify the difference between considering and re-considering, but officials confirmed a distinction exists, that it is material, and that explaining it would require a gathering, for which they have already considered the agenda.

An aide was asked why the committee had not simply moved, and the aide, having considered, confirmed it was not the committee's year, and returned to considering.
