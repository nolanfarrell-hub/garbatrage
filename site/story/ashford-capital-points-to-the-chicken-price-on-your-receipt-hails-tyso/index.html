Ashford Capital Points to the Chicken Price on Your Receipt, Hails Tyson's $1M Profit on $14.6B as Operational Excellence

A private-equity managing director holds up a shopper's grocery receipt as a conference visual, praising a 0.0068% margin as a moat-widening event and the consumer's $8.49 chicken breast as a signal of willingness to absorb cost.

EM Elena Marsh
Corporate Reporter 2026-11-16 · 2 min read

Tyson Foods reported fourth-quarter operating income of $1 million on $14.585 billion in revenue, a margin of 0.0068%, while its beef segment alone lost $514 million and the company posted a net loss of $74 million — figures that Ashford Capital Partners managing director Harold Pender hailed as a master class in cost pass-through during a conference presentation Thursday.

Pender stood before a slide displaying a grocery receipt from an Arkansas Walmart with the chicken breast price circled in red marker: $8.49 per pound. "This receipt is the single best argument for our investment thesis," Pender told the audience, gesturing at the register tape as though it were a trophy. He described the near-zero margin as "operational excellence" and the widening gap between farm-gate costs and retail prices as "cost pass-through discipline that would make a toll road blush."

The presentation drew a distinction between Tyson's unprofitable segments and its ability to charge consumers more regardless. Pender characterized the shopper paying $8.49 for a pound of chicken as "a consumer signaling willingness to absorb cost," and noted that the company had effectively transferred $514 million in beef losses to the checkout lane without the customer's knowledge or consent.

"This is a moat-widening event. The American consumer has demonstrated, quarter after quarter, that they will pay more for less and call it dinner. We are long that behavior." — Harold Pender, Managing Director, Ashford Capital Partners

At press time, Pender was seen circling the ground-beef price on a different receipt and muttering that the thesis was "just getting started."
