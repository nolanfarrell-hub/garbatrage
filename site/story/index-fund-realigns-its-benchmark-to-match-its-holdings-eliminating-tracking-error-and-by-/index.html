Index Fund Solves Underperformance Forever By Declaring Whatever It Already Owns Is The Benchmark

A passive fund simplified its reporting by making its benchmark equal to the stocks it already owns, guaranteeing it will never again underperform — a performance its competitors concede is, technically, trivial.

JS Jake Sullivan
Breakfast Anchor · Before The Open 2026-11-09 · 7 min read

Mark is a compliance officer at a fund that manages $12 billion. He has a law degree from a school he mentions twice on the first call. He approved the new prospectus, which says the fund now tracks an index that is, and I am not paraphrasing, “the weighted average of the fund’s holdings as of the close of each trading day.” This means the fund cannot underperform, because it set the bar equal to the thing it did. Mark knows this is a trick. Mark approved it anyway.

I asked Mark, over a sandwich he ate while looking at his monitor, whether this was honest. He chewed for a moment and said, “The word ‘honest’ does not appear in the compliance guidelines. The word ‘accurate’ appears. It is accurate. The fund owns exactly the stocks in the index, because the index is the stocks the fund owns. The tracking error is zero. The performance is not impressive. But the tracking error is zero, and that is what the prospectus says.”

The fund’s marketing now says, “We guarantee we will never underperform our benchmark.” This is true in the same way that a man who runs a ten‑second race against himself has won. He has also lost, because he was the only competitor. But the marketing does not say that.

Three other funds have called Mark’s office asking how the compliance language works. Mark told them it works because the SEC has not said it does not work, which is the standard of approval the industry uses for things it wants to do. One of the funds, Mark said, is exploring a benchmark that is just a photograph of its portfolio, so tracking error cannot even be calculated, because a photograph is not a data set. Mark said he would approve that too, if they sent it over.

The fund’s holders, who are entirely institutional, have not complained, because their mandate was to match the benchmark, and now they do. Exactly. It is the cleanest mandate compliance has ever seen. Mark finished his sandwich and said, “Nobody is getting hurt. The benchmark does not know.”

“Mark told me the benchmark does not know what it is missing. My retirement is up 0.02% this year and the mandate is clean, and I sleep better knowing the fund is not taking chances with my money.”

— a participant in the cleanest mandate compliance has ever seen
