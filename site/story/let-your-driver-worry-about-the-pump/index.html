Let Your Driver Worry About The Pump

Diesel is up 64 percent. The man in the back seat has a driver for that.

MR Marcus Reid
Markets Anchor · NYSE Floor 2026-11-17 · 2 min read

Dennis buys the diesel. He pays $6.35 a gallon, up 64 percent; regular $3.84, up 28 percent. These figures were shared with me by a colleague who seemed to think I would find them alarming. I did not. I found them interesting, as one finds a museum exhibit about a hardship one has never experienced.

I own a 2026 Sovereign A-9 sedan, about $230,000, driven nine hundred miles in eleven months, because I employ Dennis. Dennis handles the fuel. I have no idea what he pays; I assume he has a corporate fuel card, the charge goes up and the finance office pays it. At these prices Dennis is the variable cost, the one thing on the whole car that flexes. I do not think about Dennis the way one does not think about the oil, except Dennis is a fact with a name and a gas bill.

"Fuel is a pass-through line, not a surprise," said Greg Hollister of Meriwether Freight. "When diesel moves, we move the surcharge."

The fuel anxiety that dominates American dinner tables is noise. Real noise for people who drive fifty miles to work and back. But as an investor, noise is opportunity. Meriwether is up 34 percent on a 22 percent surcharge nobody has blinked at. The people who move things are compensated; the people who receive them pay more. That is not a crisis; it is price discovery, and Dennis, who does the discovering at the pump, is a cost center, a person we have priced as overhead.

“Diesel is $6.35 a gallon so of course there's a surcharge. The driver has to fill the tank. If I want my packages on time I pay the surcharge. That's not a crisis — that's arithmetic.”

— a business owner, 49, who ships 200 packages a week
