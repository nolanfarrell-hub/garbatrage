Fed Confirms It Will Keep Raising Rates Until Inflation Gives It Something Else To Talk About

We are not raising rates because they are too high, the committee explained; we are raising them because they are not high enough yet.

EM Elena Marsh
Fed Correspondent 2026-11-13 · 2 min read

With traders pricing a December hike as essentially certain and a minority beginning to whisper the word half-point, the Federal Reserve clarified its position this week in terms intended to end any confusion: it will continue to raise rates until the inflation that it keeps citing stops being so conveniently useful.

We are not raising rates because they are too high, the committee explained in its signed statement. We are raising them because they are not high enough yet. The distinction, officials said, is the entire point.

Rate-sensitive households greeted the news with the enthusiasm of a population that has learned to read the documents. A 5.1 percent ten-year yield, the Fed pointed out, is a price the economy has decided to pay, and it intends to honor that decision until the economy changes its mind and can be talked out of it.

Pressed on when the threshold might finally be reached, a spokesman invoked the phrase data-dependent, which the assembled press corps wrote down with the respect of a room that has stopped asking what it means.

We have always been able to raise rates. What we have not always been able to do is find inflation worth raising them for. This is the most interesting inflation of my career. — Una Pritchard, Deputy for Stance, Federal Reserve
