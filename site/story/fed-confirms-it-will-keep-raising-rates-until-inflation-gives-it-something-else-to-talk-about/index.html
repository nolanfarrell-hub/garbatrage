Fed Raises Rates Again; Homeowner Who Was Told to Wait for Lower Rates Has Now Waited Long Enough for a Higher Payment

We are not raising rates because they are too high, the committee explained; we are raising them because they are not high enough yet.

EM Elena Marsh
Fed Correspondent 2026-11-13 · 2 min read

Lamar Jenkins has a mortgage. It is a thirty-year fixed at 6.8 percent, which he got last year because his realtor told him rates were going to come down. Rates did not come down. Rates went up. Lamar has called his bank three times to ask about refinancing. Each time they told him to wait. This morning the Federal Reserve confirmed that waiting is the plan.

Lamar does not know the members of the Federal Open Market Committee by name. He knows them by their effect on his monthly payment, which went up $340 in the last year. He makes $67,000 as a maintenance supervisor for the county. The $340 came out of the grocery budget. His wife asked him last week if they could eat meat more than twice a week. Lamar said the Fed was data-dependent. His wife said the data could go fuck itself.

The Fed statement said rate hikes would continue until inflation was "convincingly on path to target." Lamar asked his brother-in-law, who watches CNBC, what that meant. His brother-in-law said it meant they were going to keep raising rates until something breaks. Lamar asked what. His brother-in-law said probably his mortgage.

Lamar looked up the Fed chair's salary. It is $246,000. He looked up how many houses the chair owns. The chair owns one house. Lamar found this reassuring until he remembered the chair bought it in 1998, when a thirty-year fixed was 5.7 percent and a maintenance supervisor could feed his family meat.

"They keep saying they're going to bring inflation down. My grocery bill did not get the memo. My mortgage did not get the memo. My wife definitely did not get the memo. The only thing that went down is my ability to be patient." — Lamar Jenkins, Maintenance Supervisor, Father of Two

The Fed says the economy is resilient. Lamar says resilient means you can still afford chicken, barely. He says the Fed can say whatever it wants. He still has to pay the 6.8 percent.
