Parks $400 Million in a Savings Account, Calls It the Whole Game

The whole business of finance, correctly, is one insured box.

TNTessa Nguyen
Economy & Data Reporter 2026-11-19 · 4 min read

Winston Drew, 69, has put $400 million into a money-market account that yields 4.9 percent, and has since described the position as the entire business of finance, correctly, which he has regretted explaining twice. The account is insured. It pays monthly. It has one employee, whose job is to not spend the money, and who is on schedule.

Forty years I chased the wet dream and the wet dream was a savings account. All that two-and-twenty bullshit, all those coked-up hedge fund gods showing off their dicks — the whole time the money just wanted to lie there and be stroked. I came to the box that strokes it. 4.9 percent, steady, four times a year, straight into the hole, no pages, no salesman breathing down my neck. I have had better sex. I have never had more reliable sex. That is the yield. That is the whole business. Everything else is a man jerking off at his reflection.

Mr. Drew has turned down four fee-bearing structures this year to keep the money in the account. He described each rejection as a win, and the account as undefeated, and himself as the world's most expensive treasury manager, a title no one else has claimed.

The 4.9 percent pays out on the first of the month. Mr. Drew deposits nothing and withdraws nothing and has told the manager not to ask him what the strategy is, because the strategy is the account, and the account is the yield, and the yield is 4.9, and he has said that number to every dinner party in the county as though it were a hand of cards, which in his hands it is. He calls the account the only thing he trusts, which for a man who spent forty years trusting everyone is the loneliest squat of asshole he has ever sat on.

Winston Drew rides the 4.9.
