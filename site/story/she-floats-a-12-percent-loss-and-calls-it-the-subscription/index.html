She Floats a 12 Percent Loss and Calls It the Subscription

A client insists the red is simply the fee for staying rich.

PSPriya Sharma
Washington Bureau, Fed & Rates, plus household costs (insurance, mortgages) 2026-11-19 · 4 min read

Mona Voss, 59, lost 12 percent of a 9-figure portfolio this year and calls the loss a subscription, which is the fee she believes the rich pay to be allowed to keep being rich. The subscription has a dashboard, a relationship director, and a quarterly call where the director renames the red a consolidation. Mona likes the dashboard. She pays for the dashboard.

The money is down but the structure is untouched, and Mona is right that the structure is the point. The structure bought the building the structure's money is losing inside. The loss is tax-loss harvested into a box labeled future, which means the tax took a haircut and Mona took a narrative.

“You pay 12 percent to stay in the room,' she told the director on the call, 'and the room is the point.' The room is doing 7 percent on the year. The room she means is the advisor's office, where the coffee is private-label and the view is the part nothing can short.”

— Priya Sharma

The subscription auto-renews in January. Mona will not cancel it, no matter what the first quarter prints, because the first question a woman with a structure asks is not what the money did but who still answers the phone. The director answers. The director always answers, the asshole. The loss is the price of the answer, and Mona calls it the subscription, and the subscription is the only thing she has ever been sure she understood in a market that has spent the year teaching her the opposite.

Mona Voss signs the subscription.
