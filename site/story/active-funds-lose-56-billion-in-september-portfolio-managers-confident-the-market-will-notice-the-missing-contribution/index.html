Active Funds Lose $56 Billion in September, Portfolio Managers Confident the Market Will Notice the Missing Contribution

The industry's longest winless streak continued, with managers describing their stewardship as prematurely correct and the outflows as a tax on patience.

DW Dana Whitfield
Investing Reporter 2026-11-15 · 2 min read

Long-term active funds saw net outflows of $56.4 billion in September, according to the Investment Company Institute, extending a streak in which managers have reliably returned less than the benchmark they exist to beat, while collecting more than the market they belong to.

The managers are not discouraged, executives said, because discouragement would require a result, and results, as the industry has established, are best left to the index.

We are not underperforming. We are over-compensating for a market that will, one day, appreciate the restraint. That day is simply not in the current fund's lifetime, nor the next.

Investors appeared unmoved by the argument, redirecting their dollars to passive products with lower fees and, more importantly, a complete absence of opinions.

The active manager told me about his process, one redeeming investor said. I did not understand it, and I have decided my money should not either.

— Portfolio Manager, Specialty Underweight Fund
