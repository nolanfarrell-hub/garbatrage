The Men Who've Failed To Beat The S&P For 14 Years Are Confident The Market Will Finally Notice How Wrong They Were

The industry's longest winless streak continued, with managers describing their stewardship as prematurely correct and the outflows as a tax on patience.

DWDana Whitfield
Portfolio Strategy 2026-11-15 · 2 min read

Wendell Grossman has managed the Focused Growth Equity Fund for fourteen years. The fund has underperformed the S&P 500 in eleven of them. Wendell calls this the setup. He tells investors that the market has not yet appreciated his positioning, which is correct but early, a phrase he has been saying for so long that being early has become the entire thesis, and being correct has become a promise he makes to himself in the car.

In September, $56.4 billion left long-term active funds, according to the Investment Company Institute, the latest month in a streak that has lasted so long the industry has stopped calling it a streak and started calling it the environment. Wendell's fund lost $240 million of that. He told his largest remaining investor that the outflows were a tax on patience. The investor said he had been paying that tax for a decade and was considering a different filing status.

Across the industry, portfolio managers held meetings to discuss their underperformance and concluded that the market was simply not reflecting the value of their opinions. The market, for its part, continued to rise without asking anyone's opinion, which the managers said proved their point about the market being shallow.

“I told my biggest client that my picks were undervalued by a market that did not understand them, and he said he understood them, which was why he was leaving, and I told him that proved my point because a client who understood my picks would know they needed more than fourteen years to work.”

— a portfolio manager whose strategy needs more time

At press time, Wendell was preparing his quarterly letter to investors, which he planned to title 'The Setup Continues,' because changing the title would mean changing the thesis, and the thesis was all he had left.
