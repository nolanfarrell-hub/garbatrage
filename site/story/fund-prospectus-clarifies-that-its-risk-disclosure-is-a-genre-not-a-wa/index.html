Fund Prospectus Clarifies That Its Risk Disclosure Is A Genre, Not A Warning

The fund, responding to confusion among holders, confirmed that the lengthy passage headed Principal Risks is prepared in the literary tradition of the risk disclosure, a form, and is not intended to represent actual risks, which are enumerated, the fund says, nowhere.

PS Priya Sharma
Washington Bureau · Fed & Rates 2 min read

I want to tell you something nice about a prospectus, and it is that the scary part is the nicest part, because the scary part, in a good prospectus, is the part the author cared about the most, and the author, it turns out, is the fund.

The section labeled Principal Risks, the fund has confirmed, is a form. It is a genre. Like a folk song, or a prayer you say at a family meal for relatives who are not really listening, the risk disclosure is recited because it has always been recited, not because anyone involved believes a word of it.

The fund notes that the section rhymes, which no other section does, and that in a document where everything is a contract, the only passage that reads like a poem is the one telling you you could lose it all, and the fund calls that symmetry, and a design choice.

Holders who attempted to cite the risks to the firm were informed those were illustrative, and that the real risks are actually the opposite of illustrative, being the sort of thing that happens and is then not discussed, which is a different genre, and a popular one.

The fund was named on a local bookstore's shortlist for best fiction, an honor it accepted, and disclosed, briefly, in the genre.
