Family Office Compares Notes On 'Family Discount', Decides The Lowest Bidder Was Never Going To Understand The Trust

A new report found family-member executives reliably out-earn outside hires, a premium the industry attributes to blood, and the family attributes to clarity.

PSPriya Sharma
Washington Bureau, Fed & Rates, plus household costs (insurance, mortgages) 2026-11-14 · 2 min read

Wealth management's annual census of who gets paid for what found that family-member chief investment officers earn more than outside hires doing the same work, a gap the industry has named the "family discount" and the families have named, with admirable candor, "the point."

"We did not hire me for my record," said Josephine Mercouris, a third-generation heir who serves as CIO of her family's office despite having never managed money that did not already belong to her. "We hired me for the absence of a town that could ask me to leave."

Outside executives are expected to deliver returns. Family members are expected to remain, which the report treated as the more valuable credential and the market, at these rates, appears to agree. The average outside CIO lasts four years. The average family-member CIO lasts until the trust runs out, which, if the office is doing its job, is never.

“The son grew up around the business. He knows the culture. An outside CIO would take four years to understand what the son already knows. The watch podcast shows he understands branding. That is relevant experience.”

— an outside candidate who respects family hiring

At press time, the report had been updated to note that the family discount appears to compound annually at a rate that conveniently matches the rate at which the family's outside hires leave to take jobs at firms where being related to the owner is not a formal job requirement.
