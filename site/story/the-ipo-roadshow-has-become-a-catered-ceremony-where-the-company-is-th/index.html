The IPO Roadshow Has Become A Room Where The CEO Leaves After A Handshake And Pete Eats Three Sandwiches

Pete from the buy-side arrived at the roadshow at 11:30 AM and made eye contact with the sandwich table before he made eye contact with the CEO. He had heard from a guy at another firm that the egg salad at these things was the real draw, and the guy was right. The egg salad was on a nice warm roll, and Pete took one before he even got to his seat.

The CEO, a man named John whose name Pete learned from the placard at the podium, stood up at 11:37 and said a sentence about the company’s platform that Pete did not quite catch because he was chewing. John shook Pete’s hand on the way to the door. The handshake lasted approximately four seconds. John said he had a call. The call, Pete would later learn, was the rest of the roadshow.

“I got three egg salad sandwiches and a four-second handshake from a CEO. That is a better return than most of the deals I have seen this year.”

— Pete, who came for the egg salad

Pete ate three sandwiches over the course of the hour plus a cookie. He did not ask a question about the business because he did not know what the business was beyond what was on the slides, and the slides, he noticed, were also next to the sandwiches, and seemed to be there for the same reason: to be consumed in the general vicinity of the capital being raised.

Pete put in an order for the IPO. He told his colleagues afterward that the egg salad was the best he had ever had at a capital-markets event, and that the company, whatever it did, felt like a strong bet, because a company that puts out a warm roll for a Wednesday roadshow is a company that understands what people are really there for.
