Summit Spends $4 Billion on Exits, Counts It as $6 Billion in Savings

The newly merged media giant plans to cut $2.2 billion by flattening management layers and shrinking office footprints. It also mandated five-day in-office attendance.

OZOmar Zayed
Corporate Governance Desk 2026-11-16 · 2 min read

Summit Entertainment, formed by the $111 billion merger of Vanguard Pictures and Heritage Media Group that closed on October 6, has announced a cost-savings program targeting at least $6 billion in annualized reductions within three years. The company carries approximately $80 billion in net debt and expects to realize roughly $2 billion of the savings in year one. Achieving the reductions will cost approximately $4 billion in one-time cash charges, most of it this year and next. The $4 billion is counted as an expense. The $6 billion is counted as savings. Both numbers appear in the same press release.

The largest single savings category is corporate overhead and real estate. Summit plans to cut at least $2.2 billion annually by consolidating back-office functions, reducing management layers, deploying AI workflows and shifting to lower-cost support functions. Additional targets include $1.4 billion from unifying streaming and broadcast platforms, $1.4 billion from centralized procurement and $1 billion from marketing consolidation.

“Five days in the office and I'm fine with it. If you're not in the building you're not really working. The layoffs are trimming fat and the people who got cut probably weren't pulling their weight.”

— a mid-level manager, 39, who commutes 45 minutes each way

On the same day the cost plan was announced, Summit confirmed it is adopting a mandatory five-day in-office policy. The company is eliminating the management layers that required in-person approvals, shrinking the office footprint that housed those meetings, replacing some of the people who would attend with AI workflows that do not commute, and requiring the remaining employees to commute five days a week to an office that is being downsized. Eight senior executives have exited — four from each legacy studio. Eligible employees have been given a mid-November deadline to accept voluntary buyout offers.

The company maintains that the savings target and the office policy are not contradictory: the savings come from eliminating duplicate roles, not from reducing attendance, and attendance is what makes the elimination of duplicate roles feel like a culture.

At press time, Summit confirmed that the mandatory five-day policy would apply to all remaining employees, including those whose managers had been eliminated in the flattening. The company had not yet specified which of the six floors in its newly consolidated Los Angeles headquarters would be closed first.
