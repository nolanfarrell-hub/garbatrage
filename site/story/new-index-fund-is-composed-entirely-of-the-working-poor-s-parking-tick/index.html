Maria's Fourteen Parking Tickets Are Now The Top Holding In A New Index Fund

An asset manager, looking for the one uncorrelated asset nobody else owns, has discovered that a municipal parking-ticket ledger is the purest income stream in America, and every ticket is, it says, a gift that keeps being received.

OZ Omar Zayed
Corporate Governance Desk 2026-11-09 · 2 min read

Maria drives a 2005 Honda Civic with an alternator that she cannot afford to replace, so the car sits on the street outside her building, and it gathers tickets, because the street sweeping schedule changes every month, and Maria does not have time to track the street sweeping schedule, because she is working, and the car is not. The car now has fourteen tickets. Each ticket grows. The city will eventually boot the car. Maria will then owe more than the car is worth, and the car is worth six hundred dollars on a good day. She is, financially speaking, inside a machine that she cannot stop.

A man in Ohio named Roger has built a fund that owns a slice of Maria’s tickets. And the tickets of people like Maria. Roger calls it “the most resilient asset class in America.” He said this while standing in a parking lot in Columbus, describing the compounding mechanics of a fine that cannot be discharged in bankruptcy. He was smiling.

The fund is crushing. It is up twenty‑two percent this year. It pays a distribution quarterly. Roger told me the tickets are actually better than bonds, because bonds occasionally stop paying, and a parking ticket has never stopped paying. A parking ticket grows. It accumulates interest. It follows the person. It does not care if the person has the money. It does not need the person to have the money. It is a receivable from the future, and the future is not Maria’s choice.

Maria does not know about Roger. She does not know that her fourteen tickets, which currently total $1,870, have been pooled with other tickets and sold to a fund that trades on an exchange she has never heard of. She knows her car is going to get booted. She knows she will have to find a way to pay, or the car will be impounded, and she needs the car to get to work, which is where she was when the car got the tickets.

Roger said his exit strategy is that people always pay, eventually, because the city does not forget. He is right. Maria will pay. She does not have a choice. That is the fund’s thesis. It is working. Roger said the fund will probably grow next quarter too. He did not say Maria’s name, because he does not know it. He does not need to know it. She is a position. She is a performing asset.

“Maria will pay because the fine does not have a statute of limitations and Maria does not have a lawyer. A person who cannot afford a lawyer is the best asset on the ledger. That is not cruelty. That is a performing asset.”

— a shareholder in the ticket fund who sleeps well
