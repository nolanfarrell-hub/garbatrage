New Index Fund Is Composed Entirely Of The Working Poor's Parking Tickets, And It Is Crushing

An asset manager, looking for the one uncorrelated asset nobody else owns, has discovered that a municipal parking-ticket ledger is the purest income stream in America, and every ticket is, it says, a gift that keeps being received.

OZ Omar Zayed
Corporate Governance Desk 2026-11-09 · 2 min read

There is an asset manager in Ohio, gentleman, and he has found the one garden that grows whether you water it or not, and it is the parking ticket. He has built a fund out of them, and the fund is up, and he will tell you it is up because of discipline, and the discipline is on the side of the municipality.

"You think a ticket is a fine," he said, sitting in an office with one chair and a picture of a curb. "A fine is a fee you pay once. A ticket, properly funded, is a relationship. The car stays parked a day too long, the meter runs, the fee grows, and nobody calls it foreclosed, so nobody knows it is a mortgage." He opened a binder of compounded citations and wept a little.

"We are long the debt of people who never agreed to the margin. That is the only credit that cannot be downgraded, because they were never rated." — Elena Marsh, Head of Research

The fund's holdings are all current, in the sense that the tickets are not going away. When asked whether collecting from people who cannot pay is sustainable, the manager looked genuinely confused, and said that is precisely the magic of it: a debt that cannot be paid is a debt that cannot be paid off, and that is a growing, self-replenishing balance sheet right there.
