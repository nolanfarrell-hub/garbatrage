Skims a 20% Carry and Calls It a Tip Nobody Took

A portfolio company files Chapter 11 and the chairman still calls the carry a gratuity.

OZOmar Zayed
Corporate Governance Desk 2026-11-19 · 4 min read

Cecil Hartigan, 68, collects a 20 percent carry on 50 billion in assets and calls the money a tip on a round nobody took, which is the only honest sentence he has said about it in decades. The round is a portfolio company that filed Chapter 11 in the third quarter, after the firm loaded it with preferred paper and a metric that could only shrink. 1,300 people lost the job the carry paid for. Cecil got a beach house that close to the water it needs its own weather report.

The firm reports the carry as performance, same as it reports the year the biggest position went to zero as a learning event. The capital was patient. The capital got a tip for showing up at a table where the drinks were served by people the capital will never meet. Cecil signs the letter that calls them partners.

“You leave 50 billion on the table and the good money finds it,' he told the annual dinner, and the table he means is the one the deal team ate at, not the one the staff eats at.”

— Omar Zayed

The carry is the only tip in America paid out of someone else's mouth, and Cecil eats like a man who has never checked the fucking check. He called it a round nobody took, and then he took it, and the firm calls that alignment, which is the word for a tip when the waiter is the owner of the check.

Cecil Hartigan pockets the tip.
