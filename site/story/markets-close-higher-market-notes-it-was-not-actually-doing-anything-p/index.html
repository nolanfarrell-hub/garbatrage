Dow Rises 214 Points, Market Clarifies That It Is a Number and Does Not Know Why You're Asking It

Steve Gable checked his 401(k) at 4:01 PM. It was up $214. He does not know why. His wife asked if it was good news. He said the market thought so. She asked what the market was. He stared at his phone.

MR Marcus Reid
Markets Desk · NYSE Floor 2026-11-05 · 2 min read

Steve Gable works in HVAC. At 4:01 PM on a Wednesday, he checked his 401(k) on his phone while sitting in his truck outside a Wendy's. The number was higher than it was yesterday. He did not know why. He had not bought anything. He had not sold anything. He had not read any news. The number just went up.

His wife Lisa asked if it was a good day. Steve said the market thought so. Lisa asked what that meant. Steve said it meant the Dow went up. Lisa asked what the Dow was. Steve said it was a number. Lisa asked what the number meant. Steve said it meant things were good. Lisa asked what things. Steve looked at his phone again. The number was still up. He did not have a second answer.

"We keep asking the market what it wants," said Dana Whitfield, a market strategist. "The market does not want anything. The market is a spreadsheet. You are asking a spreadsheet how it feels. The spreadsheet has no feelings. It just sits there, being a spreadsheet, while you project your whole life onto it."

The Dow rose because a bunch of people in buildings pushed buttons on computers. Some of them pushed the button that says buy. Some of them pushed the button that says sell. More people pushed the button that says buy. Nobody can tell you why they pushed the button they pushed, because if they could tell you why, they would have pushed the button harder, and the number would be different.

Steve closed his phone and ate his Wendy's. The number would be different tomorrow. He would check it again. He would not know why then, either. This is his life now. It is fine. It is a number. It goes up. It goes down. Steve does not have a say in any of it, but he checks it anyway, because checking it feels like having a say, which is the whole trick.
