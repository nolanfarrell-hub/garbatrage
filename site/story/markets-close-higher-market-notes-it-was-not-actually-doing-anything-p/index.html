Markets Close Higher; Market Notes It Was Not Actually Doing Anything, People Were

Equities advanced on no news, earnings, or data, and the market, through a spokesperson, asked to be clear that it is not a being, does not have preferences, and cannot be blamed, just as it cannot be credited.

MR Marcus Reid
Markets Desk · NYSE Floor 2 min read

The Dow rose 214 points Wednesday. When asked what the catalyst was, the market said nothing, because the market has never said anything, having neither a mouth nor an opinion, and being, in the strictest sense, a number.

Still, money moved. It does this the way water does, when you push it. The interesting part is that nobody remembers pushing.

"We keep asking what the market wants," said Dana Whitfield. "Every answer we get is the echo of our own question. I think it's a very honest relationship."

Retail investors were advised to remain calm, which they did, with the calm of a person who was told a sound was nothing to worry about and has elected, on balance, to believe it.

Tomorrow the market will open. It will do so on its own schedule, which is to say, exactly when we tell it to. The confusion is understandable.
