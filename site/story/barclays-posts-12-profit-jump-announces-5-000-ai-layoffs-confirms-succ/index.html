Thames National Profit Jumps 12% As Bank Proves Algebra Where 5,000 Fewer People Equal More Money

The bank says machine learning will remove 5% of its workforce while profits climb, a pairing it describes as the new healthy.

CRClaire Reyes
Debt & Fixed Income 2026-11-14 · 2 min read

LONDON—Thames National reported a 12 percent rise in third-quarter profit and announced it would cut 5,000 jobs, or 5 percent of its workforce, in an AI-powered cost-reduction drive, confirming to a skeptical world that a bank can make more money and fire more people at the same time and call both of those things the same committee's success.

"We're not shrinking because we're weak," said a Thames National spokesperson, reading the sentence off a card that appeared to have been recently laminated. "We're shrinking because we can. The model says the future needs fewer of you, and the model has been right about the past of the people it has already replaced, which is to say the people are gone and the model is still here."

James Okafor, 47, a Thames National loan processor in London, said he learned he was being laid off from a notification that popped up on his work computer while he was processing a loan for a customer who made more money in a year than Okafor would make in his lifetime. "The notification said 'your role has been identified for efficiency action' and then a button that said 'acknowledge,'" Okafor said. "I acknowledged. Then I went back to processing the rich man's loan. I finished it before my badge stopped working."

“They told me I was being replaced by an algorithm and then offered me a course in the technology that replaced me, and I took the course because if the algorithm was smart enough to do my job, it was smart enough to teach me something, and that was a benefit I had not expected.”

— a former compliance officer who took the course

At press time, the 5,000 laid-off workers had reportedly been offered a course in the technology that had replaced them, and the course had informed them it could not currently work 5,000 positions, which everyone involved agreed was, in fact, the entire point.
