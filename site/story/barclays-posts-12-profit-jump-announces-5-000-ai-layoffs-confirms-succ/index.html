Barclays Posts 12% Profit Jump, Announces 5,000 AI Layoffs, Confirms Success And Abandonment Can Coexist

The bank says machine learning will remove 5% of its workforce while profits climb, a pairing it describes as the new healthy.

CR Claire Reyes
Earnings Reporter 2026-11-14 · 2 min read

LONDON—Barclays reported a 12% rise in third-quarter profit and announced it would cut 5,000 jobs, or 5% of its workforce, in an AI-powered cost-reduction drive, confirming to a skeptical world that rising profitability and mass departure are, in the modern bank, the same committee's flourish.

The bank described the cuts as a transition to a more automated operating model, adding that the affected roles would be handled by systems that had, as of the announcement, not yet handled any work. Strengthening the quarter, analysts noted, had the added virtue of making it rather more difficult to argue the cuts were driven by weakness rather than by vision.

“We're not shrinking because we're weak. We're shrinking because we can,” a Barclays spokesperson said, reading the phrase as if it had been composed somewhere between a spreadsheet and a hammock. The bank's profit rose alongside the plan to remove the people who, in a fairer world, might have helped produce some of it.

“The model says the future needs fewer of you. The model has been right about the past of the people it has already replaced, which is to say the people are gone and the model is still here.” — Ingrid Marsh, Head of Transformation

At press time, the 5,000 had reportedly been offered a course in the technology that had replaced them, and the course had informed them it could not currently work 5,000 positions, which they agreed was the point.
