Man Mistook His 12% Loss for a Subscription He Forgot to Cancel

Mona Voss thinks the money is down, says the arrangement is not losing, it is subscribing.

PSPriya Sharma
Washington Bureau, Fed & Rates, plus household costs (insurance, mortgages) 2026-11-19 · 4 min read

Mona Voss, 59, lost 12 percent of a nine-figure portfolio this year and has described the loss as a subscription, which she says she did not cancel because she forgot it was one. The amount was $19 million. She has confirmed that if it were a subscription she would have canceled it, but that the people who manage the money described it to her as a loss and she took their word for it.

The money is managed by a firm that sends a quarterly report. The report calls the 12 percent a drawdown and recommends against reacting, which Ms. Voss adopted in full and has since described as the most useful paragraph in her financial life. She keeps the report on the same nightstand as the subscription bills she does not read, and once confused the two.

Ms. Voss has asked whether it turns out she was subscribing to her own losses the whole time, and if so she would like the canceled checks back. Her advisor told the press the firm has no record of Ms. Voss purchasing a subscription, but that the firm does charge a fee every quarter regardless of whether the money makes money, which he said is a different arrangement.

Ms. Voss has asked whether the fee is what she is subscribing to. The advisor said no. Ms. Voss said she would like that in writing, and the advisor said the firm does not put that in writing, which Ms. Voss accepted, because a 12 percent loss, in her view, is the closest thing the market has to a refund that arrives every year and is always on time, and she is not pissed about it.

Mona Voss covers the loss.
