One Spark Plug Costs More Than the Lobster Dinner the CEO Ate Last Night

The man who has never changed a tire in his life is suddenly an expert on internal combustion economics.

PSPriya Sharma
Washington Bureau, Fed & Rates, plus household costs (insurance, mortgages) 2026-11-16 · 2 min read

The chief executive of a facial-recognition startup is standing in the service bay of a Orion-certified repair center in Palo Alto, staring at an itemized bill his assistant printed on recycled bond paper. He has never been here before. He has never been to any repair center before. He leases a new vehicle every eighteen months — the model rotates, but the process is the same: a car appears in his driveway, he drives it, another car appears, the first one vanishes. Maintenance, he had assumed, was a thing that happened in the background, like server updates or the digestive system. But then the check-engine light came on — a small orange icon shaped like an engine block — and his assistant, who normally handles such things, told him it needed 'plugs,' and now he is looking at a line item that says spark plugs, $26 apiece, and he is not handling it well.

“Twenty-six dollars for a spark plug sounds like a lot until you think about what it does. It ignites fuel in a metal cylinder at thousands of explosions a minute. A lobster is just a bug you boil.”

— a man in the auto-parts aisle, 44, who has never changed his own oil

He has now demanded that his engineering team reverse-engineer a spark plug to see if they can produce one for under three dollars, because he is certain the auto parts industry is running a con. He does not know that the diagnostic machine that told the mechanic which spark plug was bad costs twenty-eight thousand dollars — roughly what he spends on oat milk in a quarter — and that the mechanic who runs it spent three years in a certification program Connor could not pass in his twenties. The bill is for $340. Connor spent more than that on dinner last night and did not look at the check. But this is different, he says. This is a principle. At press time, Vertex Vision Systems was reportedly developing a spark plug that connects to Wi-Fi and requires a monthly subscription, which Connor believes is 'the only honest pricing model.'
