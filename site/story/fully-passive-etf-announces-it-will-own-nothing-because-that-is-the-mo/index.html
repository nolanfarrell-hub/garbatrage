Fully Passive ETF Announces It Will Own Nothing, Bob Puts His 401k Into It, And Nothing Is The Best Thing That Has Ever Happened To Bob

Bob Pasternak, retired, put $340,000 from his 401k into the Zero Holdings ETF because the prospectus said the fund could not lose money. It did not mention that it could not make money either. Bob checks the balance every morning from his iPad at the kitchen table. It is the same number every morning. Bob feels calm.

MR Marcus Reid
Markets Anchor · NYSE Floor 2026-11-04 · 2 min read

Bob Pasternak retired from his job as a procurement manager in Grand Rapids in 2023 and rolled his 401k into an IRA, which he then put entirely into the Zero Holdings Fund, which he found one morning while clicking around on Reddit at his kitchen table at 6:47 AM. The fund’s prospectus said the fund owns nothing, holds nothing, and tracks nothing, and that its benchmark is the score the market would receive if it declined to answer. Bob did not understand what that meant, but he understood that the fund could not lose his money, because it did not have any money to lose.

Every morning at 6:47, Bob opens the app on his iPad from the same spot at the kitchen table and checks the balance. It is $340,000. It was $340,000 yesterday. It was $340,000 the day before that. It will be $340,000 tomorrow. Bob has watched the market go up and down on the news section of the same app. He has watched his friends’ portfolios swing thousands of dollars a day. His portfolio does not swing. His portfolio sits on the kitchen table with him, quietly, like a coffee mug that cannot spill.

“Bob is our ideal investor,” said the fund’s manager, who does not manage anything and confirmed that was the point. “He understands that a position of nothing is the only position that cannot be marked down. Nobody has ever called us to complain about a loss, because there is nothing to lose. Bob called once to confirm the balance was correct, and we said yes. He asked whether it would ever change. We said no. He said good. That was the whole call.”

Bob’s wife asked him at dinner whether the money in the fund was doing anything. Bob said it was not. She asked whether that was bad. Bob said it was not bad and it was not good. She asked what it was. Bob said it was $340,000, the same as yesterday, and the same as it will be tomorrow, and that is the whole point. She looked at him and said that sounded peaceful. Bob said it was the most peaceful money he had ever owned.

The Zero Holdings Fund now has $2.4 billion in assets under management. Every single dollar sits in a custodial account that does nothing. Bob’s share of that is $340,000. He will check it again tomorrow morning at 6:47 from the same spot at the kitchen table. It will be what it is.
