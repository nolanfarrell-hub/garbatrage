The Federal Reserve Retires The Word Zero, And Rates, Freed From The Number, Are Now Whatever They Need To Be

At a brief ceremony this morning, the central bank struck the concept of zero from its internal vocabulary, explaining that a rate of zero had become, frankly, embarrassing, and that rates would henceforth be expressed in more uplifting terms, beginning at some. The word one, officials said, is not yet ready, but one is optimistic.
PS
Priya Sharma
Washington Bureau

You know how there are words that language decides to retire because they just, you know, they bring everybody down? Like, you stop saying a word because saying it gets you a certain look. That is what the Federal Reserve has now done to zero.

Officials confirmed that the central bank has removed the word and, more importantly, the concept from its internal materials, on the grounds that a rate of zero, printed in a press release, reads like the bank has given up, and a central bank cannot look like it has given up, even when, strictly, everybody can see it has looked at the tape and decided against it.

Going forward, the bank will describe policy in what it calls affirmative terms. A rate that was zero is now some. A rate that would have been negative is now not yet some. A rate that would have been one is now, and this one the bank is genuinely excited about, one, which it described as the first whole number the bank has felt good about saying out loud in a decade.

Analysts praised the rebrand as bold, noting that a market that no longer hears the word zero is a market that no longer has to contend with what the word implies, which is that things are as low as they can go, which is a rumor, and a rumor, the bank believes, that starts with the word.

Zero is a number that says nothing good about you, and no one asks why a bank is doing nothing, so long as the bank, when asked, is doing something, and something, we were all relieved to hear, has arrived, in a range.

The bank declined to say when some would become more, citing the calendar, which, like the number one, is not yet ready, but is optimistic.
