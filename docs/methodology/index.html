Methodology

A methodology page usually exists to prove rigour. Ours exists to be honest about where the rigour stops, which is at the edge of the fiction and immediately.

The Garbatrage Industrial Average

The GIA is a fictional price-weighted index of twenty-five fictional companies, all of them in or adjacent to the waste, hauling and reclamation economy. It is not a benchmark. It is not calculated by an exchange, licensed to anyone, or tracked by a fund.

Composition is discretionary and hand-picked by the desk. Constituents are added when a story needs them and removed when they stop being funny, which is the only rebalancing rule.

Where real data appears

The tape at the top of the page runs real instruments — major indices, FX, gold, crypto — from third-party providers. Those numbers are real, delayed, and not our fault. Everything below the tape is fiction. The two sit next to each other, which is the whole problem and the whole point.

Mid-value

"Mid-value" is our house doctrine: the position that value hides in unglamorous places, that the interesting trade is always in something with a smell, and that growth is what people yell about while the patient quietly buy bins. It has no academic basis. It has a lot of conviction.

How a piece gets made

  • Premise. The desk picks a real behaviour in finance and takes it one step past where it currently stops.
  • Draft. Written against the register — no winking, no italics for irony, no explaining the joke in the final line.
  • Continuity. Checked against the constituent file. Tickers, founding dates and anchors must not contradict earlier work.
  • Edit. Human edit pass for rhythm. A piece that is accurate to the fiction but doesn't land gets cut, not fixed.
  • Label. Satire tag in the masthead, disclaimer under the index, and the standard footer.

Machine involvement

Drafting, art and production are machine-assisted. Editing is human. We do not publish unreviewed output, and we do not claim the reverse either — some of what you're reading started as a prompt, and the good lines are the ones a person kept.

Uncited statistics may have been invented to make a beat work. Assume this. It is faster than checking.

What we would tell a lawyer

Nothing here is advice. The GIA is not a real benchmark. Real company names appear only in the tape, alongside real prices. Read the Terms.
