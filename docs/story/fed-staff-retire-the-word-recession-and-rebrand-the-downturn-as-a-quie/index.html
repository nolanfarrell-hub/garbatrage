Fed Rebrands Recessions As 'Quiet Periods For The Enjoying Class'; The Enjoying Class Confirms Things Are Quiet

The Federal Reserve has stopped calling economic downturns recessions. They are now 'quiet periods for the enjoying class.' Unemployment is 'a period regrettably rich in availability.' Markets rallied. The unemployed are still unemployed. The Fed says that is a vocabulary issue, not an economic one.

PSPriya Sharma
Washington Bureau 2026-11-08 · 2 min read

The Federal Reserve has figured out that if you stop using the bad word, the bad thing stops being real. This is not a joke. They actually did this.

Staff sent a memo. The word 'recession' is retired. A downturn lasting more than two quarters is now called 'a quiet period for the enjoying class.' High unemployment is now 'a period regrettably rich in availability.' The Fed believes these phrases communicate the same data without the judgmental tone. The judgmental tone was that things were bad. Now they are not bad. They are quiet. Quiet is peaceful. The enjoying class is enjoying it.

Marcus Webb lost his job at a warehouse in November. He is in his apartment applying for work. He has applied to forty-seven places. He has heard back from two. Neither hired him. The Fed says Marcus is experiencing a period regrettably rich in availability. Marcus says he does not know what that means. He knows his unemployment ran out. The Fed does not have a new word for that yet.

“A quiet period sounds better than a recession. Marcus applied to forty-seven places and heard back from two. That is a quiet period. The word matters. If you call it a recession, you panic. If you call it quiet, you wait quietly.”

— a man who lost his job but likes the new vocabulary

Markets rallied on the announcement. A word retired is worth more than a rate cut, and considerably cheaper. Marcus Webb is not in the market. Marcus Webb is on his couch. The market does not know Marcus Webb exists. The market is enjoying the quiet period. Marcus is the quiet.
