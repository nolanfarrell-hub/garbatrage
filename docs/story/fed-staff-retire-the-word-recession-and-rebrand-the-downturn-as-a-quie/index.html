Fed Staff Retire The Word 'Recession' And Rebrand The Downturn As 'A Quiet Period For The Enjoying Class'

Communications officials said the change reflects a data-driven finding that families cannot feel a recession if the institution that publishes it no longer calls it one, and that the label was, on review, doing more harm to the forecast than the economy was doing to the people.

PS Priya Sharma
Washington Bureau 2026-11-08 · 2 min read

There is a principle in central banking that the forecast is a room, and that the person who names what happens in the room is the person who decides what happens in the room. The Federal Reserve has now decided that nothing is happening, in the room, and it has retired, with full honors, the word that used to describe it.

The term recession, staff said in a memo, was carrying a delivery problem, namely that people were reading it and feeling recessed. Under the new guidance, a downturn lasting more than two quarters will be described as a quiet period for the enjoying class, a phrase the Fed believes communicates the same data without the judgmental tone, and a stretch of elevated unemployment will be referred to as a period regrettably rich in availability.

You cannot lead people through an economy if you keep calling the economy bad, and frankly the word was doing all the damage, not the rates. We are not saying there is no pain, we are saying there is no word for it, and if there is no word for it, it has a much harder time getting a hearing. — Elena Marsh

Critics note the economy still contains actual recessed people, who are recessing, but the Fed counters that this is a highly specific population with its own vocabulary needs, which the phrase quiet period serves admirably. Markets rallied on the announcement, proving, as economists dryly observed, that a word retired is worth more than a rate cut, and considerably cheaper.
