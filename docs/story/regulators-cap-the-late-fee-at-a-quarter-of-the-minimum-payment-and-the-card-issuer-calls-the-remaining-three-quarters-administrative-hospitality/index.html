Regulators Cap the Late Fee at a Quarter of the Minimum Payment, and the Card Issuer Calls the Remaining Three Quarters Administrative Hospitality

Banks confirmed the consumer can now be late for substantially less, provided she remains late for substantially longer.

CR Claire Reyes
Debt & Fixed Income 2026-11-13 · 2 min read

New rules cap credit card penalty fees at eight dollars, prompting one major issuer to describe the change as a wholesale reorganization of what it charges for the privilege of being remembered.

"The eight-dollar fee is the part of the relationship we are willing to be transparent about," a chief compliance officer said. "The balance, which we cannot be, has simply moved into account maintenance, which is the industry term for standing next to the customer while she counts."

Analysts welcomed the cap as producing a far more predictable profit stream, since a customer now pays a small, reliable amount each month rather than a large amount that occasionally had to be written off as a lesson.

The rule also caps late fees at 25 percent of the minimum payment, which cardholders immediately recognized as the portion of themselves the issuer felt it was owed in full.

"They capped what they charge for missing the deadline, and I thought finally, and then I read which deadline, and it was the one I have been missing on purpose." — Lou Brackett, Cardholder, Class of '89
