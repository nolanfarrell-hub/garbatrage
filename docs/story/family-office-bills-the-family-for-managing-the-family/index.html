Family Office Bills the Family for Managing the Family

The office now charges the family to sit in its own meetings.

JSJake Sullivan
Breakfast Anchor · Before The Open 2026-11-19 · 4 min read

Serena Whitlock, 64, runs the family office that holds the Whitlock money, and has added the family as a client of the office, so that the family now pays the office $9 million a year to manage the family the office already manages for the family. The fee covers advice. The advice, on the record, is that the family should keep the money with the office, which is currently collecting the fee.

I have my hand up the family's pocket $9 million deep and nobody has the balls to ask what it is for, because I am the pocket. I manage the money, I advise the money, I bill the money, and the money pays me for the privilege of being advised by me. That is not a conflict, that is a transaction where I own both ends and both ends scream my name. The family thinks it is paying for safety. It is paying for me to keep my hand exactly where I decide to keep it, and every goddamn year I decide to keep it there.

The fee is payable to the office, which is controlled by Ms. Whitlock, from the trust, which is controlled by Ms. Whitlock. She has described the circularity as stability. She has asked the office, in writing, whether the fee is appropriate, and the office has answered, in writing, that it is, and both letters were signed Whitlock.

When the family asked what the $9 million buys, Ms. Whitlock said it buys the meetings, and the meetings are where the office tells the family the money is fine, which is the answer the family has paid for every year and never once disputed, because the officer delivering the answer is the same officer who set the fee, and the family has never not paid her. The family calls it a service, and the service bills them, and the two of them have never once agreed on what the money buys, which for a family is more honest than most, and which Ms. Whitlock calls the whole bollocks she is paid to keep.

Serena Whitlock keeps the hand where it is.
