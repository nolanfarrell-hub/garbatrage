Top Fund Manager Has Nothing to Do Until April, Vows to Keep Not Doing It Excellently

With index funds owning the tape, executives celebrated the final transfer of decision-making from humans to a spreadsheet that is not watching them, and is not required to.

DW Dana Whitfield
Portfolio Strategy 2026-11-11 · 2 min read

Diane Shuptrine sat at her desk at 3:47 in the afternoon with nothing to do. She had already allocated her entire quarter. The allocation was to the same index she had allocated to last quarter and the quarter before that. The index owned everything. Diane owned the index. The index had not called. The index had never called.

“I used to pick stocks,” Diane said. “I picked Regions Financial because my grandfather banked there. I picked Ford because I owned one. I picked Bed Bath & Beyond because the coupons came in the mail and my mother liked the store. That was work. Now I push money into the Vanguard Total Market fund and the Vanguard Total Market fund owns Regions Financial and Ford and Bed Bath & Beyond and the mail truck that brought the coupon.”

The index fund does not require a thesis. The index fund requires a wire transfer and a belief that the wire transfer will eventually be larger in a different direction. This belief is called conviction. The wire transfer does not know it is being believed in. The wire transfer is in a computer.

Diane now spends most of her day reading what the analysts beneath her write about the stocks the index owns anyway. The analysts know the index owns them anyway. The analysts are paid in index shares. “Every report ends with ‘overweight,’” Diane said. “I looked it up. Overweight is the default. Overweight is the only setting.”

“I asked my boss what I am supposed to do if I think a stock is bad. He said I am supposed to not think that. He said the market has already decided the stock is good. He said the market is the index. He said the index is us. I said who is us. He said the people who own the index. I said what index. He said just buy the fund, Diane.” – Diane Shuptrine, CFA, Portfolio Administrator
