Banks Believe in AI So Hard They Will Not Give It a Single Dollar, a Position They Describe as Principled

Lenders are again wary of financing data-center debt, describing their confidence in AI as personal and their confidence in its invoices as contractual.

JSJake Sullivan
Breakfast Anchor 2026-11-14 · 2 min read
Simone Brackett, a structured finance director at a major bank, sat in an office whose heating bill was paid by the interest on loans she had declined to make, and she had thoughts about the future of artificial intelligence.
“My neighbor said the banks believed in AI so much they would not lend a dollar to it, and I said that proved how serious they were because anybody can lend money to something they believe in, but it takes real conviction to believe in something and not lend to it.”

— a neighbor who admires principled lending
Evan Park, 34, a startup founder trying to finance a small data center in Virginia, said he met with five banks last week and all of them told him the same thing. "They said they love AI. They said they love what I’m building. They said they would love to see it succeed. Then they said ‘good luck’ and handed me a list of venture firms that had already told them no." At press time, the marquee chipmakers were reported to be shopping the financing question around the market in the manner of a hot potato that had somehow been upgraded to a potato of record.
