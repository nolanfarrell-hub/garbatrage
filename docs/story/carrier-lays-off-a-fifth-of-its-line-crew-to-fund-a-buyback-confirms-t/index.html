Carrier Lays Off A Fifth Of Its Line Crew To Fund A Buyback, Confirms The Crew Was The Product And The Buyback Is The Only Product That Matters

The planemaker will cut five percent of its workforce and delay production to return capital to shareholders, a trade it frames plainly: the people who built the planes are a cost, and the shares, having discovered this, are the planes now.

DW Dana Whitfield
Portfolio Strategy 2026-11-10 · 2 min read

We all know how this one goes, right? Like we all know how it goes. The company makes the thing, the people who work there build the thing, and then one year the company realizes: hold on, we spend money on these people, and the thing they build is nice, but what if, instead of building the thing, we just, like, bought the thing we already are, and charged everybody for the privilege of still owning it.

The carrier is cutting five percent of its line crew and delaying its production targets, and it is doing it to fund its own buyback, and it is not even pretending otherwise, and I respect that. It said the crew, bless them, was the product, historically, and that the buyback is the only product that matters now, and I have sat in worse meetings.

Nobody moved on that crew. You laid off the hand that made the seat, so the shareholder could buy the company that made the seat, and now the company makes the shareholder. I have seen a shell game, and this one at least labels the pea. – Marcus Reid, on the reshuffle

The analysts, who had called the buyback visionary and the layoffs a headwind, then called the layoffs visionary and the buyback a tailwind, then, reaching consensus, called the entire quarter a margin story, which is what we call it when the thing you make goes down and the share price goes up, which is the only inflation the C-suite tracks.

The remaining ninety-five percent, the ones still on the line, were assured that their product would be honored in spirit, that the delay was not a delay so much as a refinement of the delivery schedule, and that, though nothing would be built this quarter, the company would continue to buy itself, and that the shareholders, who are the new customers, would be very satisfied with the build.
