Hauler Raises Its Curb Fee, Explains The Garbage Is Getting Worse Because The People Making It Are Getting Better

A sanitation firm, citing record contamination, said rates must rise to handle a stream so degraded it no longer resembles recycling, and urged households to consider that the waste, in a very real sense, is them.

EM Elena Marsh
Head of Research 2026-11-11 · 2 min read

The route supervisor held up a bin and asked a room of shareholders to smell the future, and the future, as it often is, was a misunderstanding about which items go in the blue bin.

The truck now returns with a cargo the facility calls, tenderly, the load, and the load is the neighborhood, and the neighborhood has both patience and a co-mingled plastics problem.

The rate increase is modest, and it is an incentive: sort better, pay less, and the company will try to believe you, and will bill you for the disbelief at the new rate.

A municipal official called the fee a tax on forgetting, and the firm corrected them, gently, saying it was a fee on remembering, which is more expensive, because remembering is the part they do the math on.

The bin, overfull and not looking at the camera, has been asked to comment, and has declined, on the grounds that it is full.

“We don't charge for removal. We charge for interpretation, and your household's interpretation of a pizza box is costing me a second truck, so the box is getting its own line item.” – Maurice Litt, Route Supervisor, 22 Years In The Load
