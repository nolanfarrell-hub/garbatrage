Passive Funds Swallow Another Trillion After A Client Says 'Agile' And Active Management Decides The Meeting Is Over

With a record $1.36 trillion of inflows in a year, the index now owns the market, the market owns the index, and the only person not in the arrangement is the one who showed up to work.

DWDana Whitfield
Portfolio Strategy 2026-11-11 · 2 min read

Kevin Osei has been in asset management for 14 years. He has built spreadsheets that modeled the correlation between copper futures and monsoon season. He has a degree in econometrics from a school his parents are still proud of. Last week he moved $400 million into the SPDR S&P 500 ETF because a client said the word “agile” in a meeting, and Kevin understood that the meeting was over.

“The client said they wanted active management,” Kevin said. “I showed them our active strategy. The active strategy is the S&P 500 with a slightly higher fee and a presentation about how we pick the stocks that are in the S&P 500. The client asked if we ever pick stocks that are not in the S&P 500. I said no. The client said that sounds like the S&P 500. I said yes. We decided to call it conviction.”

Kevin’s entire job now consists of explaining to people why the thing they already own is the thing they should continue to own. He cannot recommend anything else because his firm does not hold anything else. His firm does not hold anything else because nobody at the firm has been asked to think of anything else since 2018.

“I told my wife I manage a trillion dollars,” Kevin said. “She asked what I do. I said I wait for the wire. She said what wire. I said any wire. She asked if I could pick up the kids at 4. I said the closing bell is at 4. She said the kids are at school until 3:15. I said the closing bell is the only thing I have to do today. She hung up.”

“Kevin told his wife the closing bell at 4 P.M. is the only thing he has to do today. The wire comes in and the ETF buys the stocks and the stocks go up and I am the hallway the $1.36 trillion walks through, and the hallway does not need an opinion about the stocks.”

— Kevin, Director of the hallway
