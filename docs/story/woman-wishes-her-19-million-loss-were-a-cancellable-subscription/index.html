Woman Wishes Her $19 Million Loss Were a Cancellable Subscription

The drawdown is a drawdown until the owner says otherwise. The owner says otherwise.

PSPriya Sharma
Washington Bureau, Fed & Rates, plus household costs (insurance, mortgages) 2026-11-19 · 4 min read

Mona Voss, 59, lost $19 million this year, or 12 percent of the portfolio, and has described the loss as a subscription she forgot to cancel. The money went down a defined path. The firms that manage it called the path a drawdown, and Ms. Voss has said she took them at their word, because she has never once read a quarterly statement and does not plan to start now.

A loss is just a fee for staying in the building. You do not get to sit at this table without losing. You think the men at this table are not losing? They are losing standing up, in nicer shoes. I lost 19 million and you are asking me about it like it is a number, and it is not a number, it is the price of the chair.

The firms do charge a fee every quarter, on the full value, whether the money goes up or down. Ms. Voss asked whether the fee is what she is subscribing to. The firm said no. Ms. Voss said she would like that in writing. The firm does not put that in writing. Ms. Voss said that was fine, because a subscription you cannot cancel has a clarity to it a loss does not.

She has asked the advisor twice whether they could bill the $19 million separately, so she could see it leave, the way she sees the other bills. The advisor says the whole position is the bill. Ms. Voss says she cannot feel a whole position. She can feel a bill. She believes if the money were leaving as a fee instead of as a loss, she would have canceled it, and she is proud of that, in the way a person is proud of a resolution they did not actually keep, the cunt.

Mona Voss absorbs the subscription.
