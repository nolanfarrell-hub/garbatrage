Companies Announce 617,000 Layoffs and $725 Billion in Buybacks, Confirm the Headcount Was Merely Decorative

Net job cuts slowed 44% from last year's total, a statistic that confused only the recently separated, who could not see it from where they landed.

JS Jake Sullivan
Corporate Reporter 2026-11-15 · 2 min read

US employers announced 617,189 job cuts in the first ten months of the year, down 44% from the same span of 2025, while the stocks of the companies doing the cutting bought back roughly $725 billion of themselves, according to trackers that compile both numbers for the same morning.

The two figures are not in tension, executives said, but in arrangement. We are not eliminating positions, one chief financial officer explained. We are relocating the value that the positions were, at great expense, failing to hold.

Buybacks have proved more durable than the headcounts they replaced, a durability that management attributed to the fact that a share of stock does not file for unemployment and, crucially, does not ask after toilets.

The workforce was a set of overheads waiting to be streamlined. The buyback is the same conversation, only the person who was going to be consulted is no longer in the room.

At press time, the companies confirmed that remaining staff had been informed of the new emphasis on shared value, one who was eligible for the message at a time.

— Analyst at a Firm That Rates Layoffs and Repurchases
