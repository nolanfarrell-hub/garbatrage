Fund Declares Complacency, Books Run Rate on Stamina

The fund sells the calm as growth.

DWDana Whitfield
Portfolio Strategy 2026-11-17 · 4 min read

Trent Farber, 44, watched the VIX print 22.11 and felt the calm come off his retirement account like heat off a curb. He has $17,000 in a fund that bills its persistence as a skill. What the fund actually charges for is stamina. It books the calm at run rate: the staying still, capitalized, sold at the same price a volatility fund commands for the lurching.

He called the sales line to ask what he was paying for and was told, slowly, that he was paying for the not reacting. The firm calls it the patience product. It sells the ability to do nothing as the ability to grow, and it measures Trent by the sessions he lets pass without touching the account. This is month fourteen. He has touched the account twice, both times to add money.

On the tape above the story, the market is not moving, and the fund is charging him for the not moving as though the not moving were a yield. The roundest figures in finance are the ones that describe nothing happening. He pays $200 a year to watch the market do nothing and calls the flatness his allocation.

“We don't need the tape to move. We need the acolyte to believe it won't.”

— Paula Werner, the fund's portfolio director
