US Labor Market Confirms It Has Become Too Dynamic To Actually Hire Anybody

Fed officials lament unusually low dynamism — low hiring, low firing, a low job-finding rate — which they call a brand-new mood.

TN Tessa Nguyen
Economy & Data Reporter 2026-11-14 · 2 min read

WASHINGTON—With the unemployment rate at 4.1% but dynamism in the labor market unusually low, Federal Reserve officials noted with concern that hiring is depressed, layoffs are depressed, and the share of the unemployed actually finding work has fallen, an arrangement the economy described as the most settled it has ever felt.

The paradox, economists say, is that dynamism has been replaced by equilibrium: everyone stays where they are because nowhere is better, and nowhere is better because everyone stays. Officials called the state puzzling, then called it stable, then asked whether the layoffs police could do anything about the fact that nobody was moving.

“We would like to see more churn, but tapping somebody on the shoulder and telling them it's time to find out who they are has not been well received,” one labor researcher said. Long-term unemployment remains elevated while job gains stay modest, a combination the Fed has taken to describing as an economy at rest, and not, it insists, in distress.

“I have been told the market is dynamic. The market has not hired me in five months, which I guess is a kind of dynamism, if dynamic means stationary and loudly.” — Marcus Webb, Unemployed Forklift Operator

At press time, the labor market had reportedly achieved its goal of full employment for everyone who was already employed, and was treating the rest with the patience of a market that had made a decision.
