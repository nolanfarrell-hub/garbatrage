Man Who Owns 17 Index Funds, Hates Every Company in Them

Salvador Kane owns the stock market and would prefer none, but the tax bill stops him.

TNTessa Nguyen
Economy & Data Reporter 2026-11-19 · 4 min read

Salvador Kane, 71, owns the stock market through 17 index funds and has told his advisor that he hates every company in them without exception, and that this is not going to change. His funds hold stakes in several thousand firms, for one of which Mr. Kane's son works. Mr. Kane has confirmed the fund holds the son's company and that he reinvests the dividends from it on principle.

The 17 funds cost $30 million a year in total. Mr. Kane does not know which fund owns what and has said he prefers it that way, because if he knew, he would have sold the ones holding companies he hates, which is all of them.

Mr. Kane told his advisor that he has looked at the statements and been told he owns it all, that he believes he owns it all, and that he would like it back in cash, who explained that selling everything would trigger a taxable event larger than Mr. Kane's annual income. Mr. Kane described this as the only reason he has not sold, and the advisor confirmed the reasoning was exactly that.

He asked whether the market had any companies he would like, since he owns all of them. The advisor said a handful. Mr. Kane said the handful were not worth seeing the statement. He has held the funds for 31 years and calls it diversification, which he has described as the asshole method by which he owns things he hates and is unable to sell them.

Salvador Kane writes the check.
