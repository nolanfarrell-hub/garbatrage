Footwear Hits Record High as Man Buys Fifth Pair of Identical Sneakers

He is on his fifth pair of the same shoe and cannot fathom why anyone would complain about a price that has barely moved since the Obama administration.

TNTessa Nguyen
Economy & Data Reporter 2026-11-16 · 2 min read

There is a man in the Mission District of San Francisco who has purchased the exact same pair of minimalist sneakers — black, zero-drop, wide toe box, looks like a glove that gave up — five times in the last seven years. He buys them when the previous pair develops a hole near the pinky toe, which happens roughly every fourteen months because he walks exactly 4.7 miles a day on pavement and will not wear any other shoe. He considers this loyalty. He considers this efficiency. He is in the store right now, picking up pair number five, and the price is $124.99 plus tax, and he thinks this is a beautiful thing.

“I've bought the same shoe five times and the price has barely moved since 2019. $124.99 for four months of daily walking is value. People complain about sneakers and drink $8 lattes.”

— a customer at the register, 47, on his fifth pair

He does not know that a man in Cincinnati bought his first pair of dress shoes in seven years last week — the same mid-tier Kingston & Sheffield oxford he bought in 2019 — and paid $49 more without noticing because he wears them to two funerals and a wedding and that is the entire shoe budget of a normal adult male. Weston does not know this man exists. If he did, he would say the man should buy better shoes, because they last longer, and that is the kind of advice that makes perfect sense when you have never had to choose between shoes and a water heater. The average American household spends $400 a year on footwear. Weston spends $400 every fourteen months on the same shoe. He is the most price-stable consumer in the country and he thinks that is a virtue. At press time, Weston was drafting a Substack post titled 'Why Your Sneaker Budget Is a Lifestyle Choice, Not an Economic Indicator.'
