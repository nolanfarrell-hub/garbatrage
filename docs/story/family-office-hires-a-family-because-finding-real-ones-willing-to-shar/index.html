Family Office Hires A Family Because The Real One Stopped Answering The Phone, And Brian And Cheryl Are Now The Family

The founding family stopped attending quarterly meetings one by one, citing travel, fatigue, and, in one memorable case, a general lack of interest in the money. The office has retained Brian and Cheryl Donaldson, a professional couple from Scottsdale, to sit at the table. Cheryl asked about the Alpine property during the onboarding call.

OZ Omar Zayed
Corporate Governance Desk 2026-11-04 · 2 min read

The quarterly meeting of the family office was held on the 14th floor of a building in Midtown Manhattan at 10 AM on a Tuesday. The table seats twelve. At 10:02, the only people in the room were the office’s investment director, a junior associate pouring water, and Brian and Cheryl Donaldson, the professional family from Scottsdale. Cheryl was wearing the blazer she had picked during the onboarding call, which she described as “business casual with a hint of legacy.”

The founding family stopped attending the meetings one by one. The youngest son said he had a conflict. The daughter said she was traveling. The mother said she found the meetings taxing in every sense. The father, the original wealth, died in 2021 and left the office with a table and nobody to sit at it. The office placed an ad for a family and interviewed three candidates. Brian and Cheryl got the job because they showed up, asked about the Alpine property’s cap rate, and passed the biscuits without being told which direction to pass them.

“We needed somebody to ask the questions that a family would ask,” said the office’s investment director, who declined to give his name because the board of the office, which is now Brian and Cheryl, had not authorized him to speak. “Brian and Cheryl understood the assignment. They showed up to the interview, they asked about the Alpine property, and they knew which biscuits to reach for. That is more than the original family did in the last four meetings combined.”

The meeting lasted 47 minutes. Brian asked about the Alpine property’s cap rate. Cheryl asked whether the art portfolio included anything she could mention at a dinner party. Both questions were in the script the office had sent them three days before, which Brian and Cheryl had reviewed over breakfast at the hotel. The investment director confirmed the answers from the script. The meeting ended at 10:47.

Brian and Cheryl shook hands, thanked the junior associate for the water, and left for their flight back to Scottsdale. The investment director sat alone at the table that seats twelve and confirmed to himself that the meeting had gone well. Brian and Cheryl’s invoice arrived that afternoon and was paid within the hour. The founding family was not reached for comment, because the office no longer has a working number.
