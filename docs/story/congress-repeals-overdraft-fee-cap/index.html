Congress Repeals Overdraft Fee Cap, Freeing Banks To Price The 3 A.M. Poor-Person Market At Whatever The Warmth Of A Gas Station Is Worth

Leonard from Nashville put $4.19 in a gas station account at 3:07 in the morning because his heat had been shut off and the gas station was warm. Congress voted to let the bank charge him whatever it decides the moment is worth, and the moment, the bank says, was worth the whole tank.

CRClaire Reyes
Debt & Fixed Income, plus consumer credit 2026-11-11 · 2 min read

Leonard from Nashville sat in the gas station on Murfreesboro Pike at three-oh-seven in the morning with the heat on in the parking lot and a bag of SunChips he did not want but needed to buy to justify the parking spot, because the parking spot was not for sitting, the parking spot was for buying, and the buying, in a gas station at three in the morning with four dollars and nineteen cents in your account, is a transaction the bank has now been given the full freedom to price at whatever the market for three-in-the-morning poor people will bear.

Congress voted to repeal the overdraft fee cap, which had limited the penalty to roughly the cost of a small appliance, and restored the bank's right to charge what one cosponsor called the market rate for the privilege of not having the money at the exact moment the money was needed. Leonard, who had four dollars and nineteen cents, and who bought a bag of chips and a forty-ounce Gatorade for a total of six dollars and twelve cents, was now, under the new law, the subject of a transaction the bank would price according to the texture of the moment, which the bank described as desperate, and which the bank said it was prepared to charge for.

“Leonard told the man on the phone the cold was not a factor in the pricing model. The gas station was warm and the bank priced the $4.19 transaction at the whole tank, and I told Leonard the bank is right about what the 3:07 A.M. moment is worth because I was the one in the gas station.”

— Leonard, who priced the warmth

The bank celebrated the repeal as a return to normal banking, which it is, if normal is the word for charging a man forty-seven dollars for a bag of chips he bought because his heat was off. The fee will now be calculated at close of business, based on what the bank calls a dynamic assessment of the customer's position at the moment of the courtesy, and the assessment, having been assessed, will be assessed again in the morning, with interest, because the morning, under the new law, is a separate moment, and every moment counts.
