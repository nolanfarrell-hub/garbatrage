Governor Walsh Said 'Deliberation' Eleven Times While Patricia Stayed On Hold With Her Mortgage

Starting in 2027 the central bank will hold strategy meetings instead of rate votes, explaining that the extra time allows the committee to consider the household consumer in greater depth, a consideration it described as best conducted at some distance.

PS Priya Sharma
Washington Bureau · Fed & Rates 2026-11-09 · 7 min read

Governor Walsh stood at the podium in Washington, D.C., and said the word “deliberation” eleven times. He said the Fed needed more time to deliberate. He said the household consumer was at the center of the deliberation. He said the new schedule, with fewer rate votes, would permit deeper deliberation. He said deliberation the way a man says a word he has decided is his shield, and he held it in front of him while the cameras rolled.

In Cincinnati, a woman named Patricia was on hold with her bank. She had been on hold for thirty-one minutes. She was calling about her mortgage, which had adjusted upward by $340 a month in September, and she wanted to know if it was going to adjust again in December, because she needed to decide whether to sell her car. Patricia does not need deliberation. She needs a number. Governor Walsh has a number, and he is deliberating on it.

The Fed’s new schedule means they will vote seven times in 2027 instead of eight, and six times in 2028 instead of seven. The freed‑up meetings will be used, the Fed says, to think about the economy, which is a thing you are supposed to do before you vote, but the Fed has discovered that thinking without voting is actually better, because thinking has never been wrong.

Patricia got off the phone after thirty-eight minutes. The bank told her they did not know about December. She asked who does. The bank said the Fed. Patricia does not know who Governor Walsh is. If she did, she would hear that he is deliberating. She would be furious. But she does not know, so she is just sitting in her car, in the driveway, deciding whether the car stays or goes, while a man in Washington uses the word deliberation into a microphone, and the microphone does not ask him what the answer is.

“Deliberation takes time. Patricia can wait in her car. The Fed is thinking about her situation. If they voted faster, they might get it wrong. A wrong rate cut is worse than no rate cut. Let them deliberate. That is the product.”

— a homeowner who enjoys watching deliberation
