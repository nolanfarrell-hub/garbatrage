Office Buildings at 48 Cents, Family Office Says Wonderful

A fifth of office loans maturing this half-year are underwater; values down 20 to 35 percent.

EM Elena Marsh
Corporate Governance Desk 2026-11-17 · 2 min read

The landlord, a shopkeeper in Jersey City, owes more than his building is worth. I am having the time of my life, and I need you to understand why.

— Stanwick Ruh, principal, Aldridge Family Office.

I have been waiting for this since 2022. A fifth of the office loans due in the second half of this year are underwater, the building worth less than the mortgage. Values are down 20 to 35 percent; $1.5 trillion of commercial real estate debt needs refinancing and the banks will not touch it. It is a bloodbath delivered in the exact size and shape I predicted, and I am, without exaggeration, wet.

The market is finally getting honest. Mid-tier offices were liabilities wearing a tie. We sit on $380 million of dry powder with a mandate to buy distressed office at fifty cents, convert to life-science labs, hold five years. Construction costs are reasonable because nobody is building; labor is available because nobody is hiring, the two best reasons for buying anything.

Three properties in Jersey City, one in Stamford. The seller is a bank under a consent order that needs the loans gone by December 31. We are offering forty-eight cents. I think we will get it. The landlord across the street will lose his shop; that is the mechanism. You do not clean house by leaving the dirty dishes in the sink. You throw them out, then you buy the house at the auction, and the house, at last, is yours.

“It is a bloodbath delivered in the exact size and shape I predicted, and I am, without exaggeration, wet.”

— The Desk
