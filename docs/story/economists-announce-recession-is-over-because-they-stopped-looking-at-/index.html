Diane Blevins Lost Two Shifts, But The Forecasting Panel Made Her A Math Error And The Recession Ended

The forecasting panel voted to stop counting poor people in the recession data. The recession immediately ended. The poor people are still poor. The panel says that is a separate issue, and not one that meets its materiality threshold.

PSPriya Sharma
Washington Bureau, Fed & Rates, plus household costs (insurance, mortgages) 2026-11-07 · 2 min read

You want to know how you end a recession without actually ending a recession? You stop looking at the people having the recession. That is what the forecasting panel did. They took a vote.

For years, the panel was weighing all these messy inputs from the bottom, where the numbers are bad and the stories are worse. The panel got tired of it. So they voted to make the bottom of the distribution formally not-a-number. Like dividing by zero. The poor are now a math error.

Diane Blevins, a home health aide in Cincinnati, lost two shifts this month. She is not in the new data. The panel says she has not met the materiality threshold. Diane does not know what that means. She knows she has $43 in her checking account and a tire that is making a sound. The panel says the recession is over, and has the charts to prove it.

“The recession is over because the panel has the charts. Diane has forty-three dollars and a tire that makes a sound, but the charts do not make sounds. I trust the thing that does not make a sound.”

— a neighbor who saw the chart and believed it

The panel now measures the economy by asking CEOs how they feel. The CEOs feel great. The recession is over. Diane Blevins is sitting in her car wondering if the tire will make it to Thursday. The panel does not look at cars. The panel looks at confidence. The panel is confident. The recession is over.
