Economists Announce Recession Is Over, Because They Stopped Looking At The Households

The forecasting panel declared the business cycle complete, explaining that the data it now relies on runs exclusively through institutions that have never had to choose between a filling and a furnace, a change it describes as a return to rigor.

PS Priya Sharma
Washington Bureau 2026-11-07 · 2 min read

For years the panel had been weighing the messy inputs, the ones that come from the bottom of the distribution, where the numbers are difficult and the stories are worse. This quarter, in a move it called long overdue, the panel voted to make the bottom of the distribution formally not-a-number.

The revised methodology relies on a measure of consumer confidence collected exclusively from chief executives, who report feeling confident, a finding that, as soon as it was no longer contradicted by anyone, became the definition of expansion.

We are not ignoring the households. We are simply noting that the households no longer meet our materiality threshold. A family that has stopped buying, in our model, is not a recession; it is a baseline. Recessions are for people who stopped on purpose.” — Dana Whitfield

The panel's report, titled with confidence, projects uninterrupted growth for the foreseeable future, subject to revision only if chief executives begin to feel uncomfortable, at which point the model will be re-based on the far more stable confidence of the previously excluded households.
