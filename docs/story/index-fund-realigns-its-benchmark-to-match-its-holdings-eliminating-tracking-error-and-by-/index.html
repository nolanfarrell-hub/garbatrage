Index Fund Realigns Its Benchmark To Match Its Holdings, Eliminating Tracking Error And, By Definition, The Possibility It Will Ever Do Wrong

A passive fund simplified its reporting by making its benchmark equal to the stocks it already owns, guaranteeing it will never again underperform — a performance its competitors concede is, technically, trivial.

JS Jake Sullivan
Breakfast Anchor · Before The Open 2026-11-09 · 7 min read

A passive index fund announced it has realigned its benchmark to precisely its actual holdings, closing a gap that has historically produced the fund's least favorite statistic: tracking error. Management described the move as an optimization 'so that we can be the index we follow,' which shareholders greeted as a complete solution to the problem of being compared to anything.

The innovation is that the fund can no longer underperform, because the benchmark against which its performance is judged has been made identical to its portfolio by construction. Analysts note the achievement is definitional rather than operational — the fund did not get better; it stopped giving the markets a ruler of any other length.

Competitors have called the move 'technically trivial and philosophically permanent,' a combination that appears to have struck a nerve with the fund's holders, who were already on record preferring a benchmark that never disagrees to one that occasionally says something useful.

The fund's marketing materials now feature a line the compliance department approved after a short consultation: 'We own what we track, so we are never wrong.' The compliance department, asked if that were accurate, clarified it is accurate in the way the fund defines 'wrong.'

The broader passive industry is watching: at least three other funds are reportedly exploring benchmarks that omit the assets that have been underperforming, which analysts project would, if adopted, eliminate underperformance across the sector by roughly the fraction nobody was watching anyway.
