Law Firm Unveils A Trust For Clients Who Have Nobody To Leave It To, Confirms The Nobody Is Taxed Separately

The firm, addressing a growing demographic, has introduced the Legacy of Last Resort, a trust for clients whose heirs are, in the firm's delicate phrasing, administratively unavailable. The trust will hold the client's assets in perpetuity, forever, which the firm confirmed is longer than any family, and therefore, statistically, the safest mailbox the money will ever know.

I want to talk to you today, if you do not mind, about what happens to the money when the people you would give it to, they just, you know, they turned out to be more of a file than a family, and now the money is sitting there with nowhere to go, like a house that outlived the address.

The firm's new vehicle, the Legacy of Last Resort, is built for, and here I quote the brochure because I read all of it, the modern estate planner's quietest client, the one who has looked at a deathbed and found the room, bless their heart, largely ceremonial. The firm expresses this delicately. Other firms, the brochure notes, express it obliquely, and then the meeting goes where it goes.

The trust holds the client's assets in a structure that is, in the firm's words, warm, administrative, and eternal, which the client, asked at signing whether the eternal part confirmed the point, responded that the eternal part, honey, was the whole point. The firm has taken steps to ensure the nothing and the nobody are kept separate in the paperwork, because when a nothing and a nobody share an account, the firm has seen it, and nobody benefits.

The fee schedule is discussed in a separate meeting, taped, and mailed to the client's next of kin, which, the firm confirms over a lunch the client has put down for, is the firm itself, in a provision the brochure calls the final caregiving click.

The trust is expected to be popular in the quarter, the firm predicts, because everyone needs somewhere, and the firm's somewhere is open, warm, and, as of last Tuesday, was very nearly full.
