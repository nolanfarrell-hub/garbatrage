Wells Fargo Calls AI-Driven Layoffs 'Attrition As Our Friend,' Reaffirms Love For Attrition

The bank plans to shrink its workforce by allowing AI to do what dentures could not, and management says it has only this one friend.

DW Dana Whitfield
Banking Correspondent 2026-11-14 · 2 min read

NEW YORK—Wells Fargo chief executive Charlie Scharf told staff the bank aims to use attrition as its friend, drawing on a relationship that has quietly been the company’s most durable and, employees now suspect, its most productive. The lender has cut about a quarter of its workforce since mid-2020.

“Attrition and I go back years. Attrition has never once asked for a raise, sat in on a meeting, or cried in the breakroom,” a spokesperson said, reading from a prepared statement that he confirmed he had written with the help of a model the bank recently promoted to analyst on a trial basis.

Bank executives across the street have made the same commitment, with Bank of America describing a push to work it headcount through what it calls operational excellence. The industry consensus, according to a person who was asked and had already updated his résumé, is that the bankroll is preserved and the experience is the friend.

“We’re not laying anyone off. We are simply allowing the attendance to arrive at what the model believes the attendance should be, which is a number the model made up and is very confident about.” — Braden Calloway, Workforce Planning Lead

At press time, a group of employees had reportedly attempted to enter into a competing friendship with the human-resources department, which had no comment and was off-camera.
