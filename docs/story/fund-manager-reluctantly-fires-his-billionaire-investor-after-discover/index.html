Fund Manager Reluctantly Fires His Billionaire Investor After Discovering He Actually Enjoys The Money

The manager, in a candid letter, said the relationship had become untenable, as the investor had begun displaying an unprofessional enthusiasm for the returns, humming during reviews and once referring to his own capital as mine, which the manager described as a breach of protocol.

MR Marcus Reid
Markets & Funds · NYSE Floor 2026-11-08 · 2 min read

You meet a certain kind of person in this business, and you know, the moment they start talking about what the money is for, that it is only a matter of time before you have to let them go. This is the story of a billionaire who made the mistake of feeling good about his own money, and a manager whose standards, until today, had survived every drawdown and every panic, but not that.

The letter, which the manager said he wrote with a heavy heart and a diversified portfolio, cited the investor's habit of saying thank you, of asking what the number would look like in a few years as if it were going to be his, and of once, in a moment the letter describes only as the incident, referring to his position as his own. Protocol, the manager explained, requires capital to be regarded as a guest, not a resident.

The money is not his. It was never his. It is a thing that lives with him for a while, and the second he starts acting like it belongs to him, he has broken the one rule that makes the whole arrangement possible, which is that we all pretend it does not. — Elena Marsh

The investor, reached for comment, said he had always assumed the money was his, which the manager called exactly the problem, in the man's own words. The position has been returned to him, in full, and the manager has already replaced him with a client who confirmed, in the interview, that he does not think about the money at all, which is, as everyone in the room understood, the only correct way to think about it.
