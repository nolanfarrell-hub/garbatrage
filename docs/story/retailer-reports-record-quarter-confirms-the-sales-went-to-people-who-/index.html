Retailer Reports Record Quarter, Confirms The Sales Went To People Who Paid To Be There

The department-store chain posted its strongest quarter in a decade after discovering that its most reliable customers were the ones it had banned, who returned as 'validation shoppers,' paying full price for the pleasure of standing in a store that had told them they could not enter.

TN Tessa Nguyen
Operations Desk 2026-11-07 · 2 min read

The ban was meant to keep the store empty, and it worked, and empty, the chain discovered, is not the same as quiet, and quiet is not the same as nobody coming.

The formerly banned returned, not through the door but through an app, where they paid a membership fee to watch the store they were barred from, and then paid a delivery fee to receive the merchandise they could not be seen carrying, and the chain, which had been trying to discourage them, accidentally built the most profitable customer segment it had ever served.

We spent years telling these people no, and then we realized that no is a premium product, and that the people we were telling it to were willing to pay to be told it again, only from across a velvet rope of our own holding.” — Elena Marsh

The chain says it will maintain the bans, expand the app, and continue to ship merchandise to sidewalks, because exclusion, properly merchandised, has become its highest-margin department, and the CFO notes that a customer who is kept out twice a year buys three times.
