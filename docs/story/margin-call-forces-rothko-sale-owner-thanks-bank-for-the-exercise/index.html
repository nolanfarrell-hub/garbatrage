Margin Call Forces Rothko Sale, Owner Thanks Bank for the Exercise

A $12 million collateral call, one painting, and a man who calls the whole thing cardio.

CRClaire Reyes
Debt & Fixed Income, plus consumer credit 2026-11-19 · 4 min read

Phineas Grant, 58, was margin called on a Wednesday and sold a Rothko to cover it, and has since thanked the bank for making him carde. The call was for $12 million in collateral against a loan on his portfolio, which the bank said had fallen below the coverage ratio for the second time this year. He wired the cash by lunch.

They tested me. I want you to understand that. The bank tested me. Do they test you? They test you by asking for money you do not have. They test me by asking for money I have parked behind a $12 million painting. So I sold the painting. That is not a loss. That is me telling the bank I am not sentimental, which is the only thing the bank respects. I passed. Two of them have seen me weaker.

The Rothko had hung in his office since 1998. Mr. Grant has described the painting's departure as the room getting smaller, and the smaller office as the bank helping him decide what mattered. The bank has confirmed it did not intend to help him decide anything.

He has asked whether the call could come again next month. The bank said it could. Mr. Grant said that was fine, that a call you can schedule is a call you can train for, and that he already had the trainers, which he said was the payment for the privilege of being margin called at all. He does not see the difference between a margin call and a workout, and he said he would rather be margin called than work out, because one of them makes money and the other one makes time, and he has never had a lot of the first and never had a plan for the second, which is bollocks.

Phineas Grant sells for the spread.
