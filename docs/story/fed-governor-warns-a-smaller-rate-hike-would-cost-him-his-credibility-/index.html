Fed Governor Warns A Smaller Rate Hike Would Cost Him His Credibility, A Thing He Holds While The Public Holds Nothing

Christopher Waller told a room of bond owners that a quarter-point increase would risk his credibility, and a half-point would protect it, which is a complete sentence about where the risk actually lives.

PS Priya Sharma
Washington Bureau · Fed & Rates 2026-11-11 · 2 min read

The Federal Reserve governor disclosed Tuesday that he possesses a credibility, and that raising rates by a quarter point would place it at risk, while a half point, which costs the people who owe money roughly twice as much, would keep it safe.

We asked if the dimensions of this credibility were public, and were told it is a professional-grade credential, maintained through the careful practice of never being wrong twice in the same direction, and that the public may observe it from a respectful distance, on schedule, with a two-week embargo.

I have one credibility, and I intend to keep it in working order, even if the price of parking it is everyone else's mortgage, which I regard as a modest storage fee. – Christopher Waller, Federal Reserve Governor

Rate markets, absorbing the news that the poor are once again the deductible, rallied. The governor added that he is open to additional risk to the public if it is presented in the form of a more robust hike, and that his credibility, at final check, was twenty-seven and a half, and climbing.
