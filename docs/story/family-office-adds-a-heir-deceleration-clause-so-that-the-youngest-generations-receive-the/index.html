Family Office Adds A Heir-Deceleration Clause So That The Youngest Generations Receive Their Inheritances Only After They Have Had Time To Become Less Like Themselves

A single-family office has structured its trust so distributions to grandchildren phase in only after age 45, on the theory that by then the money will be received by a person who is no longer young enough to do anything with it.

OZ Omar Zayed
Corporate Governance Desk / Family Office 2026-11-09 · 7 min read

A family office has announced a 'deceleration schedule' for its youngest beneficiaries, delaying full distributions until heirs reach their mid-forties — a window the office calculates is long enough for the principal's growth and, crucially, short enough to leave the principal's drama on the beneficiary's other side.

The premise, as outlined in the trust's explanatory letter, is that money received young tends to be spent by the person who receives it, whereas money received at forty-five is received by a person with better judgment, by which the office means a person who has watched the first batch of relatives go through theirs.

'We remain deeply committed to the family,' the letter reads, 'which is why the distributions are structured to arrive at the only age at which we are confident the family will use them as intended.' The intended use, the letter elaborates, is not the use a twenty-four-year-old would find intended.

Attorneys note the structure is legally unremarkable — trusts have long gatekeeper even inheritance on age — but say the framing is novel in its candor. 'Most trusts just have the age condition,' one said. 'This one has the age condition and a memo.'

The youngest beneficiaries, informed of the schedule in a meeting that was itself age-gated, were described as understanding the arrangement as 'technically for them,' which the office noted was, by design, the mature interpretation.

"A corpse can hold a position, but only a household loses it with dignity."
