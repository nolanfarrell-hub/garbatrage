Estate Planners Now Advise Dying Clients That Their Heirs Are Simply 'Pre-Earners,' And The Room Warms Up Considerably

The planners, overhauling a century of inheritance guidance, now describe the not-yet-deceased's children as 'pre-earners,' whose future money exists, if only abstractly, and recommend the family read the will as a vesting schedule, which all present agreed was the single most reassuring thing anyone has said to them about a death since the invention of the spreadsheet.

PS Priya Sharma
Washington Bureau · Fed & Rates 2026-11-06 · 2 min read

Now I want to tell you something that sounds like a joke, and I want to tell you it is not a joke, it is a memo. Estate planners, the people who make dying feel like a quarterly filing, have rebranded, and the rebrand is this: your children are not your beneficiaries. They are pre-earners. And the room, I am told, warmed up considerably.

You have to understand what this does. When the will was a will, it sounded like a goodbye. When the will is a vesting schedule, it sounds like a promotion. The money is not coming to you because someone died. It is coming to you because you had, all along, been earning it, on a schedule you were not told about, predicated on an event you were not consulted on, which, the planners note, is exactly how compensation has always worked, and nobody has ever once complained about it in a boardroom.

The children, described at the reading as pre-earners, reportedly sat a little straighter, and the deceased, through the estate, was described as a grantor, which everyone agreed takes the edge off the whole thing. One heir asked whether pre-earning came with a cliff. The room, united at last, laughed warmly, and his brother, the senior pre-earner, kindly explained that it would be explained to him later, at the board level, which is to say, never.

“The word beneficiary had a sadness to it. The word pre-earner carries discipline, and a schedule, and the promise of a ceremony we can put a date on. I am told the family found the entire process, for the first time ever, achievable. We have licensed the framing at a modest uplift, which we have described as an acceleration event, for the grantor's sake.”

The planner closed by confirming that all pre-earners would, at the appropriate time, receive a summary of what was earned, with the summary itself to vest over the customary period, and that any questions regarding the schedule should be directed, out of respect, to the grantor, who is presently unavailable, a condition the firm now describes, in the new terminology, as fully earned.
