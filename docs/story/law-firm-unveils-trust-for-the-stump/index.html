Law Firm Unveils A Trust For Clients Whose Family Tree Has Become More Of A Stump, The Stump Is What They Call It

Marlene, 78, sat in a conference room with a lawyer named Craig and explained that her sister&rsquo;s kids had not called in three years, and that the last call ended with one of them asking whether Marlene&rsquo;s house was paid off. Craig nodded and opened a folder. The folder contained the Stump.

TNTessa Nguyen
Economy & Data Reporter 2026-11-03 · 2 min read

Marlene sat in the conference room with a cup of coffee that had gone cold while she explained to Craig that the people you are supposed to leave things to, they just stop calling. The last time she heard from her sister’s daughter, the conversation turned to whether Marlene’s house had a mortgage on it still, and Marlene, 78, living alone in the house she bought in 1997, said yes, and the call ended, and that was three years ago.

Craig, a lawyer at the firm that has since named a vehicle after people like Marlene, opened a folder and explained the Legacy of Last Resort, a trust that holds the client’s assets in perpetuity because the client has nobody to hold them for. The trust, Craig explained, does not require a named beneficiary. The trust itself is the beneficiary. The trust, Marlene confirmed, is the one that shows up.

“The trust is the beneficiary — that makes perfect sense. It shows up every quarter, and my sister's kids have not called in three years.”

— Marlene, 78, who prefers the trust

Marlene asked what happens to her house when she dies and Craig said the trust holds it. She asked for how long and Craig said forever. She asked what forever means in a legal document and Craig showed her the clause, which reads “perpetual,” and then, in a parenthetical, “longer than anybody.” Marlene signed. She asked whether the coffee in the conference room was free. Craig said yes. She asked whether there was anything else she should know about the Stump before she left.

Craig looked at the folder and confirmed there was not. The Stump, once signed, is the last word on everything Marlene owns, and the Stump, unlike her sister’s daughter, does not have a phone and does not need one. Marlene took her cold coffee and walked out into the afternoon, leaving the house on Secor Road to a trust that has never missed a meeting and never will.
