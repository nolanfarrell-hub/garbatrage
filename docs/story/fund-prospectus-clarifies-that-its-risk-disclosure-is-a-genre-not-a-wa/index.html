Fund Prospectus Clarifies That The Part About Losing Everything Is A Poem, Which Angela Learned When She Called The 800 Number

Angela, a retired schoolteacher in Boca Raton, read the Principal Risks section of a fund prospectus cover to cover because she reads things before she signs them. She called the 800 number to ask about a specific risk. The junior associate told her that section was the poem part and that the real risks are discussed in a meeting she cannot attend.

Angela, a retired schoolteacher from Boca Raton who spent 34 years reading student essays, sat down at her kitchen table with a fund prospectus and a cup of tea and read the entire thing, including the Principal Risks section, which she read twice because it seemed to her, after 34 years of reading things, to be written in a different voice than the rest of the document. The sentences had a rhythm. The words were words like “precipitously” and “unforeseeable” and “in its entirety.” It sounded like something a person had worked on.

Angela called the 800 number. A junior associate named Tyler answered. Angela said she had a question about a risk listed in the Principal Risks section. Tyler asked which one. Angela read it: a total loss of principal may occur under conditions that are unforeseeable and therefore not subject to the ordinary course of market activity. Tyler paused. He said that section was the poem part. Angela asked what he meant by poem part. Tyler said the Principal Risks section is written in a literary form called a risk disclosure, and it is not intended to describe risks that could happen.

“I read the poem part of the prospectus cover to cover. It talked about losing everything in a way that felt artistic — I would rather read that than some scary legal document with real numbers.”

— Angela, a retired schoolteacher who reads before she signs

Angela asked where the real risks are. Tyler said they are discussed in the investment committee’s quarterly risk review, a meeting that occurs in a conference room in Boston that investors are not invited to. Angela asked whether there was any way to learn about the real risks before investing. Tyler said the real risks are, by definition, not disclosed in writing, because if they were disclosed they would be priced in, and the fund prefers its risks not to be priced in, which is itself a risk, and which is disclosed in the poem.

Angela did not invest in the fund. She told her neighbor at the community center that she had called the 800 number and been told the part about losing her money was a poem. The neighbor said that sounded illegal. Angela said she did not think it was illegal. She thought it was the opposite of illegal, which was worse, because it meant somebody had written a poem about losing her money and put it in a document she was supposed to sign, and that the poem was the only honest part.
