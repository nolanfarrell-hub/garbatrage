Averaging 22 Percent on the Card, Bank Confirms the Consumer Remains Strong Even as the Consumer Remains Stuck

Lenders point to sturdy household demand, defined as the willingness of the same households to keep paying for the privilege of having borrowed.

TN Tessa Nguyen
Economy & Data Reporter 2026-11-12 · 2 min read

Ray Okafor stands at register 7 of the Walmart on Tryon Road at 8:47 on a Tuesday night with a basket containing: one bag of rice, one bag of pinto beans, one gallon of milk, one bottle of store-brand dish soap, and $422.19 in credit card debt that was $380.00 last month. He puts the rice on the belt. The interest on the $422.19 accrues while the rice moves down the conveyor. The interest on the rice, technically, is also on the belt.

“My credit card company sent me a letter congratulating me on my strong consumer demand,” Ray said. “I called to ask what that meant. They said it means I keep buying things. I said I need to eat. They said that is what consumer demand is. I said the rice is 22 percent now. They said the rice is not 22 percent, the borrowing of the money for the rice is 22 percent. I said I am borrowing the money for the rice because I do not have the money for the rice. They said that is why consumer demand is strong.”

The bank’s chief economist confirmed that a consumer paying 22 percent on a revolving balance is a consumer who has demonstrated commitment. “A person who is willing to pay 22 percent to keep eating is a person who is not going to stop eating,” the economist said. “That is durable demand. That is the kind of demand you can model. We have modeled it. It pays 22 percent.”

Ray looked at his statement. The minimum payment was $38. The interest charge was $47. The principal went down by negative $9. Ray put the dish soap on the belt. “I am doing great,” he said. “My demand has never been stronger. I demanded rice and the bank demanded 22 percent of the rice forever. That is a relationship. That is two people who need each other. I need rice. They need me to need rice on a card that never goes to zero. We are both getting what we want.”

“I asked the banker when the rice would be paid off. He said the rice is never paid off. The rice is a payment stream. I asked what happens if I stop buying rice. He said you will not stop buying rice. I said what if I die. He said the debt survives. I said the rice survives me. He said the cardholder agreement does not distinguish between the rice and the borrower. I bought more rice.” – Ray Okafor, Warehouse Picker
