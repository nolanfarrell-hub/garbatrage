Averaging 22 Percent on the Card, Bank Confirms the Consumer Remains Strong Even as the Consumer Remains Stuck

Lenders point to sturdy household demand, defined as the willingness of the same households to keep paying for the privilege of having borrowed.

TN Tessa Nguyen
Economy & Data Reporter 2026-11-12 · 2 min read

The average credit card rate rose again to a fresh high, and economists immediately declared the consumer strong, since a consumer who keeps paying 22 percent is by definition a consumer with durable habits.

Banks described the elevated APR as a premium for flexibility, noting that the flexibility to pay more next month is precisely the flexibility the consumer has exercised all year.

More than half of cardholders now carry a balance month to month, a figure analysts said demonstrates confidence, on the theory that nobody borrows for tomorrow who does not expect to be there later than the money.

The Fed's own data confirms the rate on interest-accruing accounts has climbed, which means the spread between the consumer's optimism and the consumer's payment has reached an all-time defensive high.

"The banker said I had strong demand for credit, and I said I had demand for groceries, and he said that can also be the credit." — Ray Okafor, Warehouse Picker
