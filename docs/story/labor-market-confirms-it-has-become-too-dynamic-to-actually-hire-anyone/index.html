Company Hires Darren, Reclassifies Darren, Absorbs Darren, And Invites Darren To Apply Again As Darren

Employers confirmed they would love to bring somebody on board, but the job keeps evolving out from under the handshake into a position that no longer exists, and then out from under that.

TNTessa Nguyen
Economy & Data Reporter 2026-11-15 · 2 min read

Darren Balfour was hired at 9 a.m. on a Tuesday. By 9:45 his position had been reclassified. By 10:30 his department had been restructured and his role had been absorbed into a new unit called Operational Integration, which did not have a headcount. At 11:15, the new unit was folded back into the original department, which already had a headcount, and Darren's role was declared redundant. At noon, HR called to say his onboarding was complete and his exit interview was scheduled for the same time, and that the company considered this the fastest full employee lifecycle in its history.

Darren sat in his car in the parking lot, where he had been for three hours. He had not received a badge, visited a desk, or met a coworker. He had spent the morning filling out forms for a job that was eliminated before he finished the forms. He still had the parking pass, which he kept because it was the only evidence he had ever been there.

A manager at a mid-size logistics firm explained that his company now hired, trained, and released people so efficiently that most workers were let go the same day they started, because the job kept evolving out from under the handshake. He said this was not a bug. It was called being agile, and the person who was supposed to fill the role had already been agilely removed.

“The HR woman said I had not been fired but optimized out of a role that no longer existed, and she called it a learning experience, and I told her she was right because I had learned that a job could disappear while you were still standing in it, and that was valuable knowledge.”

— a three-hour employee who values the lesson

At press time, Darren had received an email asking him to reapply for a position that had just opened up in the original department, under the original title, which was, according to the email, a new role with exciting opportunities, and which Darren noted was the same role he had been hired for three hours earlier, before it was eliminated, reclassified, absorbed, and revived without him.
