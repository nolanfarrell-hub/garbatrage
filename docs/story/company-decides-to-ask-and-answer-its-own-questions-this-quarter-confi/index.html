Company Decides To Ask And Answer Its Own Questions On The Earnings Call, Which Is What Mark Tried To Do And Got Thanked Into Next Week

Mark Chen, an analyst at a mid-tier fund, dialed into the earnings call at 4:36 PM and asked a question the company had not asked itself. The CEO thanked Mark for the question, answered a different question, then thanked Mark again. Mark, on the line, was thanked a third time. The company confirmed this is now the format.

MRMarcus Reid
Markets Anchor · NYSE Floor 2026-11-04 · 2 min read

Mark Chen, an analyst at a mid-tier fund in Chicago, dialed into the earnings call at 4:36 PM EST with an earbud in one ear because he was already in a meeting. When the CEO opened the floor for questions, Mark heard a silence on the line that was not the silence of nobody having a question. It was the silence of everybody having the same question and knowing the answer was not coming.

Mark asked his question. It was about revenue guidance for the next quarter, which the company had declined to provide. The CEO paused, smiled into the phone, and said: “Mark, thank you for that question. That is a great question. And I think what the question really gets at is the strength of our underlying business, which is strong.” The CEO then answered a question about how strong the underlying business was, which was not Mark’s question. Then the CEO said: “Thank you, Mark. Great question.” Mark had been thanked for two different things: the question he asked, and the question he did not ask. The call moved on.

“I was thanked three times on one earnings call. That is more engagement than I got from the last four companies combined — the answer was not my question, but the gratitude was real.”

— Mark, thanked three times on the call

Mark sat in his office after the call with the earbud still in, not sure whether he had gotten an answer or participated in a ceremony where the answer was the thanking itself. He reviewed his notes: the CEO had thanked him, pivoted to a prepared statement, and thanked him again. Mark’s question had been absorbed into the company’s own narrative, which is the only narrative the company plans to use going forward.

The company confirmed it will continue the format for the foreseeable future. Mark said he will continue to dial in because his fund owns the stock, and because on the off chance the company ever answers a question he asked, he wants to be there to hear it, even if he has to listen through three thank-yous first.
