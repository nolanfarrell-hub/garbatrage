Family Office Adds A 'Boredom Guard' To Ensure No Heir Accidentally Does Something

The single-family office, alarmed by a junior heir who briefly expressed curiosity about a business, has installed a senior principal whose sole function is to stand near the heir and, through a series of gentle, costly reassurances, return the heir to the comfortable state of wanting nothing.

OZ Omar Zayed
Family Office Desk 2026-11-07 · 2 min read

The incident occurred on a Tuesday, when an heir, idling between a morning session with a wealth coach and an afternoon session with a wealth coach, asked what, precisely, the family's industrial holdings did, a question that sent three advisers into a structured-review call.

The office has responded with a boredom guard, an otherwise qualified professional whose deliverables are evaluated not on what the heirs accomplish but on whether the heirs remain uninterested enough to sustain the family's legal structure, which, the office notes, requires that no one ever form an intention.

Curiosity is the leak in the dynasty. A bored heir is an heir we can plan around. An heir who asks what the factory does is an heir who will one day ask what the factory pays, and that is not a question our liability schedule has priced.” — Dana Whitfield

The guard, who describes the mandate as deeply fulfilling, has already logged four interventions, each successful, and the heir reports that he has never felt so at peace, having been returned to a state that the family considers, and this is the technical term, stable.
