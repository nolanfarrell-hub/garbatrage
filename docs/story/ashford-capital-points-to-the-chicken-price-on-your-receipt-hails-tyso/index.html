Ashford Capital Points to the Chicken Price on Your Receipt, Hails Tyson's $1M Profit on $14.6B as Operational Excellence

Tyson Foods reported $1 million in operating income on $14.585 billion in revenue — a margin of 0.0068%. The company posted a net loss of $74 million. Its beef segment lost $514 million. Ashford Capital's Harold Pender called it a victory lap.

EMElena Marsh
Head of Research 2026-11-16 · 2 min read

Harold Pender stood at the podium holding a a supermarket chain receipt above his head like a championship belt. The receipt had been crumpled and smoothed flat several times. Someone in the second row had bought a family pack of chicken thighs for $8.49. Pender had circled the price in red marker, then laminated the receipt, then pinned it to a corkboard behind his desk at Ashford Capital's Stamford office, and then brought it to a conference center in Midtown to show three hundred institutional investors what operational excellence looks like.

"This right here is a consumer signaling a willingness to absorb cost," Pender said, tapping the laminated plastic with his fingernail. The consumer in question — a grocery shopper in a New Jersey suburb, who had selected the chicken thighs because they were on the end cap and she was in a hurry — had not been consulted about her role as a signaling vehicle for Tyson's capital-allocation thesis. Pender did not name her. He did not need to. "The spread between what that chicken cost Tyson to produce and what that consumer paid — that spread is the moat. The moat is widening."

Pender then produced a second receipt. Ground beef, $6.99 a pound. "The beef segment lost five hundred and fourteen million dollars this quarter," he said, and smiled. He circled the ground-beef price on the receipt. "This right here is how we transfer that cost to the consumer without the consumer's knowledge or consent. That's operational excellence. That's a moat-widening event. The thesis is just getting started." The conference hall applauded. The consumer in Paramus, who does not own Tyson stock and whose weekly grocery budget has risen $47 in twelve months, was not in the room.

“I read the earnings report and saw that Tyson made a million dollars on fourteen billion in revenue, and I told my wife that was a miracle because moving fourteen billion dollars through anything and keeping any of it was an achievement, and the 0.0068 percent was profit that did not have to exist.”

— a shopper who appreciates the 0.0068 percent miracle

At press time, Pender was asking the audience for their receipts, promising to circle more things on them, and a woman in the third row was checking her purse to see if she still had the one from the deli counter.
