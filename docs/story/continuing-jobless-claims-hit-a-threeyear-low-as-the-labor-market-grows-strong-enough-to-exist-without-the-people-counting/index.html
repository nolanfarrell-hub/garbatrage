Jobless Claims Hit Three-Year Low After System Decides Running Out of Weeks Is the Same as Finding a Job

The number of Americans on unemployment checks fell to a three-year low, which economists called strength and the people whose checks ran out called a different thing, spelled with fewer syllables.

TNTessa Nguyen
Economy & Data Reporter 2026-11-13 · 2 min read

Tom Eckert is not in the unemployment numbers this week. He was in them nine weeks ago, and eight weeks ago, and seven weeks ago, and then the weeks ran out, which is how the number got so low so fast. The Labor Department announced the three-year low as a milestone. Tom found out from a notification on his phone that said his claim had been resolved. He did not resolve it. The system resolved it.

The system resolved it because Tom had been on unemployment for twenty-six weeks, which is the maximum, which the system treats as a cure. Tom is not cured. Tom has applied to thirty-seven jobs since his warehouse shift was replaced by a conveyor belt that the company said could do his job for $14 an hour and also did not complain about the heat. Tom did not complain about the heat either, but the conveyor belt does not need health insurance, which the company said was the deciding factor.

Tom sat in the career center this morning. The career center has a computer lab with six computers, two of which work. Tom used one to fill out an application for a forklift position at a warehouse that he knows laid off its own forklift crew three months ago. He applied anyway, because the application asked if he was willing to work nights and weekends, and he clicked yes, which he has been clicking yes to for two months, and the yes has not worked yet.

The Labor Department says the drop in continuing claims proves the economy is running on its own. Tom says he would like to be part of the economy that is running on its own, because the one he is in right now runs on a prepaid debit card with $47 on it and a warning that a $5.50 fee applies if he uses it wrong.

“Twenty-six weeks of unemployment is enough. The system gave me what it was supposed to give me. I'm not in the numbers anymore and that means the economy is working. I just have to find my next step.”

— a former claimant who agrees the system worked

Next week the number will drop again. Tom will not be in it. The week after that, neither will anyone else whose twenty-six weeks just ended, and the number will be the lowest it has ever been, and the Labor Department will hold a press conference, and nobody will ask what happened to the people whose number just got so low.
