CPI Looms As The Year In Which Core Inflation Simply Refused To Come Back To 2%, A Year The Fed Notes Was Good For Anyone Not Buying Food

October consumer-price data due Tuesday will decide whether the December hike gets cemented, but analysts already frame 2026 as the year core inflation declined to admit defeat slowly — a period households are believed to have spent in a state economists describe as 'normal now.'

TN Tessa Nguyen
Economy & Data Reporter 2026-11-09 · 7 min read

The consumer price index for October lands Tuesday, and it has been assigned the annual job of disappointing everyone who keeps a filled pantry. Wells Fargo expects core CPI to have cooled to a 0.11% monthly pace — roughly 'in line with its average' — which the bank drily notes does little to alter the broader picture of 2026 being a lost year for getting inflation back to 2%.

A lost year for inflation is, by the accounting of the analysts who track it, still a profitable one, which is the part the reporting tends to bury. The Fed's mandate is price stability; it has achieved, at minimum, price stability as experienced by the indices, which is the stability that votes.

The difficulty is that households buy groceries, and groceries do not sit in the core index's good graces. September's food and energy spikes are the precise reason the headline number moved 0.6%, and the precise reason 'core' exists as a concept — so that the number that decides borrowing costs can be insulated from the number that decides dinner.

'We expect the report to reinforce the slow and uneven nature of the disinflationary process,' Wells Fargo wrote, a sentence that reads differently depending on whether you are the report or the family reading it at the register.

Should Tuesday's print run hot, money markets say a December hike is effectively done — which would be bad for borrowers and fine for the groups that set the story. Officials have described this outcome as 'the data doing their job.'

"A corpse can hold a position, but only a household loses it with dignity."
