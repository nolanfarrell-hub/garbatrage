Bank Cuts The Ribbon On Its Overdraft Hall Of Shame, Confirms The Name On Every Plaque Is Yours, Rendered In A Very Nice Font

The bank, addressing a record twelve billion dollars in overdraft fees, has opened a permanent exhibition honoring the customers who made it possible. Spokespeople walked the halls solemnly, confirming each etched name was the account holder, and that the shame, having gone unclaimed, has been booked as revenue.

OZ Omar Zayed
Corporate Governance Desk 2026-11-10 · 2 min read

I attended the ribbon-cutting, and I can report that the new Overdraft Hall of Shame is a very serious room, with track lighting aimed at the plaques and a row of polished lecterns, and that the room honors the institution's most reliable contributors, which the institution described, with absolute gravity, as the customer.

Each plaque carries an account number, a modest sum, and an etched portrait of a spouse who did not buy the coffee, and the guide, walking at a respectful pace, confirmed that the shame, noted in the title, was not the bank's to claim, but had, after a period of it going unclaimed, been booked as revenue, a transfer the tour characterized as the bank doing the customer a final, administrative kindness.

Let us be clear that nobody was more surprised than us. We thought we had made seventeen dollars. When we learned it was thirty-five, on a day that was not even our best, we felt the institution genuinely felt for us. – Dana Whitfield, Portfolio Strategy, reading the wall

An executive addressed the assembled press with the earnestness of a museum director introducing an important acquisition, noting that the hall would grow in the spring with a new wing dedicated to nonsufficient funds, which she described as the brother of overdraft, less celebrated, harder to explain to the children.

The tour concluded in the gift shop, which sells commemorative ledgers and, at a modest markup, the parable of the coffee, and I asked whether any visitor had left feeling honored, and the guide, considering the question at length, confirmed that one had, and that the institution had noted his satisfaction, and that he had been entered, rather sweetly, into next year's plaque.
