Dynasty Trust Unveiled to Shield Money From the Only Two Things It Fears: Taxes and Grandchildren

The vehicle, structured to last nine hundred years, protects a fortune from taxation, divorce, and any relative who might reasonably be expected to know where the money came from.

OZ Omar Zayed
Estate & Governance Desk · Trusts 2 min read

Here's what you need to understand about the modern dynasty trust, and I want you to keep this in your heads while I explain it: it is a legally binding promise that your descendants will never, ever have to do a thing they don't want to do, which is, when you think about it, the entire point of having descendants.

Advisers spent four hundred hours designing a structure that would survive wars, depressions, and, most impressively, contact with the family itself. The document has a seven-hundred-page semiannual distribution schedule that one trustee described as 'the most meticulous arrangement of nobody ever having to work that the human race has ever filed with a probate court.'

"The trust isn't about protecting money from the taxman," said Elena Marsh. "It's about protecting it from the taxman's clients, who are your heirs."

Critics note the money could simply fund schools, hospitals, or a fire department. Advisers note that such investments historically underperform a nine-century lockup by roughly the entire moral imagination of the family, and the market has priced that accordingly.

The grantor, for his part, wants you to know he believes deeply in equality of opportunity. His heirs, all four hundred and twelve of them, will never have the opportunity to work. This is the gift that keeps on giving, to the giver.
