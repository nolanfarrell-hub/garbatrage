Blackstone Seeks $3 Billion To Buy Your Stake in the Fund That Owns the Building You Never Saw

Secondaries are booming because everyone is trying to leave at once, and someone has to be the door.

JS Jake Sullivan
Private Equity Correspondent 2026-11-13 · 2 min read

Randy Phelps has been the building super at 175 Fleet Street for twenty-two years. He fixes the boiler when it freezes, unclogs the toilets on the fourth floor, and as of this month, he works for a building that is owned by a fund, which is owned by another fund, which is owned by people Randy has never met and who, according to the mail he sometimes forwards, live in a ZIP code where the garbage disposal probably works.

Blackstone is raising $3 billion to buy stakes in funds like the one that owns Randy's building. Randy found out about this from a letter that came to the boiler room addressed to "Current Occupant." He read it because it had his building's address on it. It said the fund that owns 175 Fleet was being reorganized to "unlock latent value." Randy's boiler does not have latent value. It has a leaky valve that he has been fixing with epoxy since 2019.

The people who own Randy's building have never been to Randy's building. They send inspectors, who take pictures of the lobby and leave. One inspector told Randy the building was a "core-plus asset." Randy said he didn't know what that meant. The inspector said it meant they'd probably sell it. Randy asked to who. The inspector said Blackstone probably, or someone like Blackstone. Randy asked if they'd fix the boiler. The inspector laughed.

Randy figures the $3 billion will change hands and his building will get a new owner, which is what his building gets every three years, along with a new name for the same leak. He says he does not care who owns the fund that owns the building that he cleans the trash chute of. The trash does not care either.

"Twenty-two years I've been here. I've worked for a REIT, a pension fund, a German bank, and now something called a special-purpose vehicle. The special purpose, as far as I can tell, is that the toilet on four keeps running, and nobody has a key to the valve." — Randy Phelps, Building Superintendent, 175 Fleet Street
