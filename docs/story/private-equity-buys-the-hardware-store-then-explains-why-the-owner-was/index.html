Private Equity Buys 100-Year-Old Hardware Store, Keeps The Owner As Advisor Of The Part Where He No Longer Owns Anything

An investor group has acquired a hundred-year-old family hardware store, its inventory, and the institutional patience to wait out the founder's decline in the role of a helpful advisor.

MR Marcus Reid
Markets Anchor · NYSE Floor 2026-11-11 · 2 min read

Benny Falcone opened Falcone Hardware in 1927 with a handshake and a loan from his uncle Phil. In 2026, Benny Falcone IV, who everyone calls Benny still, sold the store to a buyout group called Pinnacle Partners II, L.P., which is not a person and does not have an uncle. The group paid $14 million for the building, the inventory, the name, and, as a line item nobody caught, the part of Benny that still thought the store was his.

“They said I could stay on as an advisor,” Benny said. “I asked what that meant. They said I would advise. I asked on what. They said the transition. I asked whose transition. They said mine. I asked if I still had a key. They asked if I had a phone. I said yes. They said the phone is the key now.”

Pinnacle has since replaced the front door lock. Benny’s key still works on the back door, which the group says is more symbolic, and which Benny says is more humiliating, since the back door opens onto the dumpster. “I walk past the display I built in 1989 and a woman from Milwaukee in a vest is rearranging the drawer pulls I arranged,” Benny said. “She is very good at rearranging. She has a binder. I do not have a binder. I had a store. Now I have the number for the back door.”

The group has promised to keep the Falcone name on the sign. It has not promised to keep Falcone in the sign. Benny checks every morning from the dumpster. The sign is still there. The sign is not the store. The sign is a piece of metal that Pinnacle Partners now owns.

“Benny told Pinnacle Partners the sign is a piece of metal and the sign is still there. I sold the building for $1.4 million and I kept the knowledge that I sold it, and the knowledge is worth more than the building because the building had a 4.5% mortgage and the knowledge has no payment.”

— Benny, who checks the sign from the dumpster
