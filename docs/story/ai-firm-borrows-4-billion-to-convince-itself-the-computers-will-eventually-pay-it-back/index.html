AI Firm Borrows $4 Billion To Convince Itself the Computers Will Eventually Pay It Back

The loan is investment grade. The payoff is a forecast. The forecast is a belief.

JS Jake Sullivan
Debt Markets Correspondent 2026-11-13 · 2 min read

An artificial-intelligence firm this week priced $4 billion in investment-grade debt, a raise it intends to sink into the very machines it hopes will one day make the money to pay it back, a plan lenders described as the strongest credit story they had heard all year.

The circular nature of the investment was not a flaw, the prospectus explained, but the feature that separated it from debt that merely had to be serviced. This debt, they noted, had the additional benefit of being impossible to default on, since any shortfall could be attributed to the models.

Firms across the sector are pursuing the same strategy, borrowing against a future so widely agreed upon that disputing it has become the only unhedgeable position left.

Analysts who asked where the actual revenue would come from were directed to the models, which when asked the same question produced a paragraph of confident-seeming language that all parties agreed to count as an answer.

You are not lending us money. You are lending us the ability to be more certain than the people who cannot borrow. That is what the rate is for. — Ira Wexler, Treasurer, ExaCompute Holdings
