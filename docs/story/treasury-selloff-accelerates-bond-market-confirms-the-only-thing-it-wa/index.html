Treasury Selloff Accelerates, Bond Market Confirms The Only Thing It Was Ever Afraid Of Was Being Right

With the 10-year toward 5.1% and hawkish Fed bets rising, the long end reports that vindication, apparently, hurts.

TN Tessa Nguyen
Economy & Data Reporter 2026-11-14 · 2 min read

NEW YORK—The selloff in U.S. Treasurys accelerated Friday, pushing the 10-year yield toward 5.10% as officials spoke openly about raising rates to bring down inflation, prompting the bond market to confirm that it had, in fact, been correct all along and that the truth was somehow worse.

“Everyone called us the risk-free asset because we were boring. Then the boring one was right and nobody was happy,” one long-duration desk told reporters, referring to the recent rise in yields as a victory lap no one had asked for. European government bonds, long the market's weatherproof cousin, followed suit out of what analysts described as pure solidarity with an asset that was finally being listened to.

The move has been driven by sticky PCE inflation of 3.6%, energy prices, and the market's own supposition that the Fed will hike in December, a belief the market holds so firmly it has begun pricing the emotional state of the meeting rather than the meeting itself. Officials say the data, not the market, will decide, to which the market has replied that it is the data now.

“I did not spend two years being told I was dead just for the world to realize I was alive and then not give me a parade.” — Gerald Twine, Duration Manager

At press time, the bond market had requested that its rise in yields be described as a statement about inflation, not a cry for help, and the stock market had agreed to stop looking at it.
