Federal Reserve Concludes 12-Year Study On Waiting, Decides To Commission A Study On The Study

Jerome from the Board spent twelve years studying whether to act or wait for more information, and the study's conclusion is that he needs more information. The conclusion is now in a binder on a shelf above his desk, where it will be joined by the next study, which is a study of the conclusion.

PSPriya Sharma
Washington Bureau 2026-11-10 · 2 min read

Jerome from the Board has a binder on a shelf above his desk that says THE STUDY on the spine in Sharpie. Inside the binder are twelve years of meetings, each one ending with the sentence we will accumulate more information. The binder is now full. The Federal Reserve has completed the study. The study's conclusion, reached after four subcommittees, a review of the subcommittees, and a meditation retreat for the chair of the subcommittee review, is that Jerome needs more information. The binder, having delivered its conclusion, is now evidence for the next binder.

I asked Jerome what the information was, specifically, that he was waiting for. He said that is a very good question and that he would need more information to answer it. I asked who had the information. He said the information was being accumulated by a new committee, which is currently hiring the person who will decide where the information, once received, will be stored. I asked what the storage committee was called. He said it was too early to name it, and that naming it, at this stage, would require information the committee had not yet accumulated.

“Darnell told me the pizza will come when the information arrives. I have been standing in the doorway for 12 years and I have learned more about the doorway than I ever learned about the pizza, and that is the study.”

— Darnell, 12 years in the doorway

The release of the study was met with a brief rally, which was met with a selloff when analysts realized the study contained no data, which was met with a second rally when the Fed confirmed that the absence of data was, in itself, information, and that the information, having been identified, would now be accumulated. The next meeting will consider whether the accumulated information requires a study of its own. Jerome's binder, on the shelf above his desk, waits. It is, Jerome confirmed, a very good binder.
