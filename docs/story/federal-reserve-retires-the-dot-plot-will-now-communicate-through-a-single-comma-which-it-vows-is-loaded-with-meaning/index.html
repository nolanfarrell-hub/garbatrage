Federal Reserve Retires the Dot Plot, Will Now Communicate Through a Single Comma, Which It Vows Is Loaded With Meaning

The scatter chart had one last request before it was phased out: that the committee note it was never the members who were poor at plotting, only the future.

PS Priya Sharma
Washington Bureau · Fed & Rates 2026-11-13 · 2 min read

WASHINGTON — The Federal Reserve voted to retire the dot plot this week, replacing the committee's famous scatter of projections with a single punctuation mark that, officials said, carries all the information the market needs and none of the risk of specificity.

"A dot is a promise, and we learned that promises are simply forecasts that have not yet submitted a revision," the communications attaché explained during the formal un-plotting. "A comma, by contrast, is an admission that the sentence continues, which is the most honest position a central bank can hold."

The decision comes after months in which the committee's median projection repeatedly met a similar fate to the households it describes, arriving at the target later than planned and, in one widely cited quarter, not at all.

Under the new regime, every statement will end in the same single comma, which the Fed says it will deepen or italicize depending on the data, an upgrade it estimates will reduce market volatility by roughly the amount the comma fails to communicate.

"We are not tightening and we are not easing; we are pausing the English language until the labor market stops front-running it." — Whit Murchison, FOMC Communications Attaché
