Evicts a Tenant for a Vending Machine, Calls It the Better Tenant

A REIT chief ends a 32-year tenancy; the machine pays on time, every time.

CRClaire Reyes
Debt & Fixed Income, plus consumer credit 2026-11-19 · 4 min read

Randolph Vane, 66, owns 11,000 apartment units and just evicted a 32-year tenant to install a vending machine in the alcove, which he has described as the building's way of finding a better roommate. The tenant, a retired nurse, had paid her $2,300 on the first of every month without interruption since 1994. The machine vends a can of soda for $2.50 and owes nothing.

Tell you what the machine does. It gets its hands dirty and it does not talk about its feelings. The tenant? The tenant wanted the building to care. It does my tax, it never once clutches its chest, and it lets me stick my hand up its slot every night and feel what I put in there coming back to me. The tenant wanted a home. The machine wants to be serviced. I outrank a machine. I am the machine's john, and the machine is a whore I own, and whores don't tell the building about their knee.

Mr. Vane said the eviction was not about the woman but about the square footage, which he has confirmed is a distinction the woman did not benefit from. The alcove now holds the machine, a sign, and a chair that was never reported as a fire hazard.

He calls the machine the better neighbor and means it, and when asked what the woman will do he said that was between the woman and the market, and that the market had already answered. He has raised the rent on the three tenants down the hall to cover the vending company's cut of his own investment, and described that as the unit helping the building, which is the closest the building has come to a roommate in thirty-two years. He calls the machine the whole fucking deal, which it has to be, because it is the only tenant he has ever described that way.

Randolph Vane collects the rent.
