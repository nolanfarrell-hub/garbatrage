Mortgage Rates Fall For Fourth Week, Lenders Celebrate The Return Of A Kind Of Pain The Market Had Almost Begun To Miss

A 7.11 percent is quietly described as relief, with the caveat that relief, like everything else, was always going to be relative.

TNTessa Nguyen
Economy & Data Reporter 2026-11-14 · 2 min read

Thirty-year mortgage rates eased for a fourth straight week to 7.11 percent, a decline described as welcome, though the word "relief" was deployed only after a note specifying that relief is a function of how much pain you were previously told to sit still through.

A year ago the same loan fetched 6.24 percent, a number industry analysts invoked roughly the way a draftsman invokes a blueprint for a building that was never going to house the client anyway. "Rates are down," said a mortgage industry analyst. "That is objectively good. It is also objectively not good enough to make a difference for the person who still cannot afford the house. Both things are true, and we are choosing to focus on the first one."

Delia Osei, 41, an evening custodian in Milwaukee, said her lender called to tell her the rate had gone down. Then they showed her the new payment, which had gone up, because the home price had gone up more than the rate had gone down. "They told me the rate went down and then showed me a number that was higher," Osei said. "I asked the man how the rate could go down and the payment go up. He said 'that's the market learning.' I said the market has learned how to make me pay more no matter what the rate does."

“The man showed me on the calculator that the rate went down and the payment went up, and he said that was the market learning, and I told him I was glad the market was learning because a smarter market was better for everyone, even if the number was higher.”

— a borrower who took the class on market learning

At press time, the industry had confirmed that the best strategy for prospective buyers was to wait for rates to fall further, and also to wait for prices to fall, and also to wait for wages to rise, and that the waiting was itself the product, and that the product was working exactly as designed.
