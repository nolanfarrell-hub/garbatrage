Company Authorizes $12 Billion Buyback; Employees Applaud a Bonus Reserved for People Who Do Not Work Here

Management called the repurchase a statement of confidence in the company's future, to be funded by the future's current occupants.

JS Jake Sullivan
Breakfast Anchor · Before The Open 2026-11-13 · 2 min read

Karen Oluwole has worked at Canton Consumer Products for eighteen years. She packs boxes on the line, same as when she started. The company authorized a record $12 billion buyback this week. Her manager gathered the shift to tell them. The room cheered. Karen did not cheer. She does not own stock. She owns a 401(k) with $14,000 in it that she cannot touch for thirty more years, assuming Canton Consumer Products still exists, which she is not confident about, because they stopped giving raises two years ago and started buying back stock instead.

Her manager said the buyback was the equivalent of hiring an employee who costs nothing, works forever, and never calls in sick. The employee is a share of stock. The share does not need health insurance, which the company noted was a significant savings. Karen thought about her own health insurance. Her deductible is $3,500. She had to use it this year for a root canal. The share of stock does not get root canals.

The buyback was funded by $12 billion. Karen does not know what $12 billion looks like. She knows what $12 looks like, because she spent it on lunch yesterday. She knows what $12 billion smells like, which is the same as $12, if you believe the company, which just told the shift to embrace a new culture of ownership. Karen asked her manager if the new culture of ownership came with a raise. Her manager said the raise was the stock going up. Karen asked how she was supposed to spend a stock that went up on groceries. Her manager said that was not a question he had an answer for.

“The $12 billion buyback shows management believes in the company. I've been here eighteen years. I've never seen that kind of commitment. The stock going up is the raise. That's how ownership works.”

— a packing line employee who supports shareholder value

Karen went back to packing boxes. The boxes have the company logo on them. She does not own the company. The company owns her, until the buyback is big enough, at which point it will own only itself, which Karen suspects is the whole idea.
