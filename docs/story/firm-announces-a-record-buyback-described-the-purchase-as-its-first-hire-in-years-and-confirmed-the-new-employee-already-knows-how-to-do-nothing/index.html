Firm Announces a Record Buyback, Described the Purchase as Its First Hire in Years, and Confirmed the New Employee Already Knows How To Do Nothing

Management called the repurchase a statement of confidence in the company's future, to be funded by the future's current occupants.

JS Jake Sullivan
Breakfast Anchor · Before The Open 2026-11-13 · 2 min read

A consumer giant authorized a record buyback this week, and executives framed the repurchase as a rare act of hiring at a company that has otherwise declined to add headcount this decade.

"This is the boldest staffing decision we have made in ten years," the chief financial officer said. "We have brought on a share, and the share has agreed to work for free and also to be the thing we measure our success against."

The decision comes as the company is also asking employees to contribute to a new spirit-and-flexibility initiative, which staff have been told is voluntary in the same way the buyback is voluntary, which is to say someone with our shares is doing it regardless.

Analysts praised the buyback for its efficiency, noting that a share of stock requires no benefits, no severance, and, crucially, returns the cash to the people who already own the firm, which is the point of the firm.

"Why hire a person when you can hire the claim that you don't need one." — Vance Pruitt, Portfolio Manager, Buyback & Dividend Desk
