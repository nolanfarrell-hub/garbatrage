Private Equity Acquires The Hospital, Announces The Real Patient Was The Balance Sheet All Along, Visiting Hours Begin At Revenue

The firm, having closed on the acquisition, has confirmed the strategic insight that the system was never treating people, and that the true, long-neglected patient was the margin. Services will be reorganized around the recovery of the patient.

OZ Omar Zayed
Corporate Governance Desk 2026-11-10 · 2 min read

So a fund buys a hospital, and the briefing tells you the real patient, all along, was the balance sheet, and they're right, and that's the part that should terrify you, is that the guy with the doctor's coat is now a guy with a term sheet, and the guy with the term sheet is the one who decides who gets the bed.

The firm announced visiting hours will now begin at revenue, that admittance will be scheduled around recoveries, and that the recovery, having been neglected for so long, will be given the attention the humans never got, which is to say, a great deal of attention, from a spreadsheet, at close.

They tell you the system is healing, and they mean the system that runs the system, and the people who needed the system are the ones who are, if you'll pardon the clinical term, discharged. There is not an economics degree that explains this. There is a tank. – Omar Zayed, Corporate Governance, on the acquisition

The firm noted that the hospital's capacity, on paper, remains unchanged, which is to say that the beds are still in the building, even if the pipeline to them now routes, with formidable efficiency, around the uninsured, the way a river routes around a rock, which the river regards not as an obstruction but as scenery.

Asked whether the doctors had been consulted on the reorganization, the firm confirmed the doctors had been consulted, that many had opinions, and that the opinions, having been received, had been routed, with the machinery that now runs the place, to the recovery, which is doing extremely well, and which sends its regards.
