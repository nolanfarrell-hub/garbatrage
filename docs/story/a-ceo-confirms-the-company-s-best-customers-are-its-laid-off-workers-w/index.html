A CEO Confirms The Company's Best Customers Are Its Laid-Off Workers, Who Keep Buying, Believing The Severance Check Is A Dividend

In a candid investor day, the CEO identified former employees as the firm's most loyal and least costly buyers, noting that they continue to purchase the product out of a belief that the departure check they received was, in fact, a dividend, and that the firm has made no effort to correct this, calling the misunderstanding 'the deepest loyalty metric we have.'

TN Tessa Nguyen
Economy & Data Reporter 2026-11-06 · 2 min read

You have to hand it to them for being straight about it, in the same way you have to hand it to a man for telling you he is going to steal your car. At an investor day, a CEO, asked about the firm's most reliable segment, answered without a beat: the laid-off workers. They stay customers. They buy quarter after quarter. They believe the check was a dividend, and nobody has corrected them.

Now, you would think this would be the part where the room shifts in its chair. It did not. The room leaned in. Because, as the CEO explained, a former employee who thinks her departure package is income not only keeps buying, she keeps recommending, she keeps a schedule, and she never, ever asks for the product to be better, because to her it is the thing she bought with the money you already counted as saved. In accounting terms, the CEO noted, it is a margin on a margin.

The firm confirmed it has no intention of correcting the impression, describing the confusion as, and I quote, the deepest loyalty metric we have, and adding that a worker who believes she is being paid to stay a customer is the rare human being who has, financially speaking, achieved a state of grace.

“We look for customers who cannot leave, and we have found, to our genuine surprise, that the customer who cannot leave most reliably is the one we laid off, because she has, in her mind, already been compensated for leaving, and thus considers it settled. That is a repeat buyer. That is, frankly, a buy-and-hold buyer.”

The CEO closed by thanking the firm's most valued, most price-insensitive, and, it must be said, most recently former stakeholders, and confirmed that the annual dividend, as such, would remain, for the foreseeable future, non-recurring.
