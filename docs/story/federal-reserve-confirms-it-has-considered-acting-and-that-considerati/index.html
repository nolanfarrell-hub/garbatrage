Federal Reserve Confirms It Has Considered Acting, And Phyllis Has The Agenda That Proves The Considering Was The Whole Job

Phyllis has worked at the Federal Reserve for 23 years typing agendas for the FOMC. Every agenda says the committee has &ldquo;considered&rdquo; the economic outlook. Phyllis has typed that word 312 times. She has never once typed &ldquo;acted.&rdquo; The Fed confirmed this week that considering is not a step toward acting. Considering is the product.

PS Priya Sharma
Washington Bureau · Fed & Rates 2026-11-04 · 2 min read

Phyllis sat at her desk on the third floor of the Federal Reserve building in Washington at 8:15 AM, looking at the draft agenda for the FOMC meeting next week. The second bullet point read: “The committee considered the current economic outlook and the stance of monetary policy.” Phyllis has typed the word “considered” in that exact position 312 times over 23 years. She has never once typed “acted.” She does not expect to type it before she retires.

The Federal Reserve confirmed this week that considering is not a preliminary step toward acting. For a central bank, considering is the finished product. The meeting happens, the committee discusses, the committee considers, and then the committee goes home. Acting, a senior official explained, is what other central banks do, and is widely regarded inside the building as a foreign custom, like eating lunch after 1 PM.

“I have typed the word 'considered' 312 times in 23 years. That is 312 more times than most people have considered anything in their whole career — the considering is the work.”

— Phyllis, who has typed 'considered' 312 times

Phyllis was asked whether she had ever typed “acted” instead of “considered,” just to see how it looked on the page. She said she had, once, about eight years ago, in a draft that nobody saw, and that she had deleted it within three seconds. The word “acted” looked wrong on the page, like a typo. She felt a physical discomfort reading it, as though the document itself was allergic to the word. She typed “considered” back in and the discomfort stopped.

The committee will meet next week. The agenda, which Phyllis will print and place at each seat, will say the committee has considered the current economic outlook. The committee will then go home. Phyllis will file the agenda, type the same word for the 313th time, and go home herself, having produced, for another month, the entire work of the central bank: the meeting, the considering of the meeting, and the written record that the considering occurred.
