Portfolio Is Wet. The Rain Is the Print

$240 billion in cash, and the blood is the liquidity.

MRMarcus Reid
Markets Anchor · NYSE Floor 2026-11-17 · 4 min read

Simon Cribb, 52, ran his hand over the $240 billion in cash the fund was now calling liquid, the way a man feels a soaked sleeve and names the weather lucky. The market had swung, the fund had sold into it, and the proceeds sat in the account, wet with the thing they had not been at the top of the cycle: dry powder the size of a small country's deficit.

He is the portfolio manager, which means he is the one who has to say the word seriously every quarter without being a joke. The cash is liquid, they told the investor call, and Simon thought about how the wettest thing in finance is the thing that used to be yours before you needed it to stay. Cunt, he wrote in the margin beside the word liquidity, then erased it, because the minutes get read and the hand he was filling jobs no one applauds.

“We are $240 billion liquid, which means we are wet with the cash we no longer trust.”

— Simon Cribb

Pays: Simon. The cash is not his.
