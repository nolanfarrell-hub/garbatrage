Index Fund's Tracking Error Exactly Equals Its Fee Two Years Running, Firm Upgrades From 'Coincidence' To 'Feature'

Denise from Phoenix put her 401k in the honest fund, the one that just owns everything and charges 0.23%. At the end of the year, the fund had trailed the index by exactly 0.23%. The firm called it a coincidence. Then, after a pause, called it engineering.

DW Dana Whitfield
Portfolio Strategy 2026-11-10 · 2 min read

Denise from Phoenix put her 401k in the fund that tells you it is the honest one. The one that just owns everything. The one that charges you twenty-three basis points and says that is all it takes, that is the entire business model, the index is the thing and the fund is the index and the difference between them is nothing but a rounding error that the fund, being honest, will return to you. At the end of the year, the difference between Denise's fund and the index was twenty-three basis points. Exactly twenty-three. Not twenty-two. Not twenty-four. The fee.

Denise called the fund. A man named Phillip told her the gap was a coincidence. Denise waited. Phillip said the word again, slower, as if Denise had not heard it the first time. Coincidence. Denise asked what the odds were of an index fund trailing the index by exactly the fee. Phillip said he would need to check with the product team. Phillip called back. The product team said the gap was not a coincidence. The gap was engineering. Phillip used the word design. Phillip said the fund, by design, tracks the index minus the fee, and that the fee, by design, is the cost of the design, and that the design, now that Denise understood it, was the very honesty the fund had promised.

“Denise told Phillip the fee is the design and the design is the honesty. My 401k trails the index by 0.23% and I call that transparency, because I know exactly where every penny of the error went.”

— Denise, 0.23% richer in understanding

Analysts praised the alignment. They said the fee and the error had achieved a harmony rare in finance, and that the two figures, having met, would now travel together as one line on the chart, which is the kind of chart you look at when you do not want to see the line underneath. Denise looked at the chart. The chart showed one line. The other line, the index, was not there. Denise said it looks like the fund is just taking the fee off the top. Phillip said that is exactly what it is. Denise asked why nobody said that. Phillip said nobody asked.
