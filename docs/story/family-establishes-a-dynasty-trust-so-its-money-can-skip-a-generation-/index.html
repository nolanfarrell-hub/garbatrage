Family Dynasty Trust Skips Grandchildren After Determining They Wear Shoes On Sidewalks Like Regular People

Whitney Vanderbilt IV has decided his grandchildren are a tax, not beneficiaries. The money will skip them entirely and land on the generation after, which the trust has already vetted through a series of interviews the grandchildren were not told about.

OZ Omar Zayed
Corporate Governance Desk 2026-11-11 · 2 min read

Whitney Vanderbilt IV sat in a room with three lawyers and a notary and signed a document that says his grandchildren, Caroline and William, are not the recipients of his fortune, but the obstacle to it. The document is called a dynasty trust. What it does is skip. It skips Caroline. It skips William. It takes the money that Whitney made and holds it in a warm, dark place until Caroline and William have had their chance to do what Whitney assumes they will do, which is spend it foolishly, on things like rent and groceries and the kinds of shoes you wear on an actual sidewalk. The money, Whitney decided, does not deserve that. The money has been patient. The money wants to see the next century. Caroline and William, if the trust's math is correct, will be dead by then.

Caroline was informed of the trust by her father, who was informed by the lawyer, who had been instructed to inform Caroline that the trust was not a reflection on Caroline personally, but a reflection on the generation Caroline happened to be born into, which the trust, in its preamble, describes as a period of elevated consumption and diminished regard for compounding. The preamble does not mention Caroline by name. It mentions a demographic that Caroline, for the purposes of the trust, represents. Caroline asked whether she could appeal. The lawyer said the trust does not have an appeals process. The trust has a vesting schedule. Caroline is not on it.

“Caroline told the lawyer the used Honda was the application and the application was denied. I am 29 and I am not on the vesting schedule and that is the trust working as designed, because the $40 million should go to someone who never had to buy a used car.”

— Caroline, who drove the Honda to the lawyer's office

The money, having skipped Caroline and William, will now sit in a pool that compounds until it encounters a descendant the trust considers worthy, which the trust defines as a descendant who has demonstrated an understanding that money is not for spending but for being, and that the being, in a dynasty trust, is the trust itself, which does not spend, which does not drive a used car, and which will outlive every Vanderbilt who has ever tried to open it.
