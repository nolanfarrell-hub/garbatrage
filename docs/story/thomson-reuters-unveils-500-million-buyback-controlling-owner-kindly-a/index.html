Thomson Reuters Unveils $500 Million Buyback, Controlling Owner Kindly Agrees To Sell Some Back To Itself

The company's controlling investor will participate proportionally in the buyback, graciously accepting the shares it already controls back.

DW Dana Whitfield
Corporate Correspondent 2026-11-14 · 2 min read

TORONTO—Thomson Reuters announced a $500 million share-buyback program under which it may purchase up to 7 million common shares, or about 1.6% of its outstanding stock, between November 13 and next November 12. The company's controlling investor, Woodbridge, confirmed it would participate proportionally, thereby permitting the company to buy back a portion of the shares that Woodbridge already, functionally, decides what to do with.

Corporate-governance experts described the arrangement as the most elegant solution yet to the modern ownership problem, in which a firm repurchases its own equity from a family trust that controls its board at a price the family trust is comfortable with, to the benefit of everyone concerned, definitionally.

“There is no conflict here. There are merely two patients who share one doctor, and the doctor has decided the transaction is healing,” said governance adviser Ingrid Marsh, who confirmed the price was set by a committee that meets in a room containing only portraits of the family that hires it.

“We are delighted to sell the company a portion of a company we have always thought should own a bit more of itself, provided the price reflects the love.” — Meera Chandrasekhar, Trustee

Analysts said the buyback would be accretive to earnings in the manner of all repurchases, which is to say by removing shares rather than adding profit, and praised Woodbridge for participating rather than, as one put it, “letting the company buy shares from strangers, which would have been gauche and cross-contaminated.”

At press time, Thomson Reuters had reportedly begun drawing up a $250 million follow-up buyback in which the company would purchase the shares it had just repurchased from itself, a transaction advisors were quick to describe as net neutral, cash-positive, and the closest thing the modern corporation has to a hug.
