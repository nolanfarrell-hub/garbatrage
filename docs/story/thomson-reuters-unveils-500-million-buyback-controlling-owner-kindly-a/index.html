Thomson Reuters Announces $500M Buyback, Controlling Owner Graciously Sells Shares to the One Person Who Already Owned Them

The company's controlling investor will participate proportionally in the buyback, graciously accepting the shares it already controls back.

DWDana Whitfield
Portfolio Strategy 2026-11-14 · 2 min read

Marcus Webb owns twelve shares of Thomson Reuters. He knows this because his 401k statement lists them under a column called "holdings" and a sub-column called "not enough to matter." When he heard the company announced a $500 million buyback, he called investor relations to ask if his twelve shares could be included in the program.

The investor relations line put him on hold, then transferred him to a voicemail box that was full. When he finally reached a human, the human asked if he was a member of the Woodbridge family trust. Marcus said he was not. The human asked if he controlled a majority of the voting shares. Marcus said he did not. The human told him the buyback was for people who already owned most of the company, and that the shares being bought back would be sold by the people who already controlled them, and that after the transaction the shares would belong to the company, which was also controlled by those people, and that Marcus's twelve shares would remain exactly where they were, which was nowhere.

Corporate governance experts described the arrangement as the most elegant solution yet to the modern ownership problem: the people who own the company sell some of it back to the company, which is the same people, at a price the same people set, to the benefit of the same people.

“I asked the man why I could not sell my shares back like the family office could, and he said the program was full, and I told him I understood because a program can only hold so many people, and the family office had gotten there first, which was fair.”

— a shipping supervisor who respects program limits

At press time, Marcus had looked up the Woodbridge family trust online and found a photo of a building in Toronto that was larger than his entire block. He stared at it for a while, then closed the tab and went back to work.
