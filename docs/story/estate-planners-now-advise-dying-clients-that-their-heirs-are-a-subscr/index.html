Estate Planners Now Advise Dying Clients That Their Heirs Are A Subscription That Must Be Renewed Annually

A revised succession strategy, circulating among the oldest families, treats the beneficiaries not as loved ones but as licenses, to be reviewed, redacted, or revoked at the top of each fiscal year, with the grieving encouraged to think of themselves first as accountants and only later as relatives.

OZ Omar Zayed
Estate & Governance Desk · Trusts 2026-11-08 · 2 min read

There was a time when a family fortune was passed down like a recipe, from one set of hands to the next, on the assumption that the hands were attached to someone you had perhaps met. That assumption, the estate planners have concluded, was sentimental, and sentiment is a line item that must be trimmed. The new model treats the heir as a renewable entitlement, and the annual review as the holiest day on the family calendar.

The strategy, outlined in a memo titled Blood Is Thick But Cash Is Colder, instructs that each beneficiary be evaluated on contributions to the dynasty, attendance at the dynasty's events, and proximity to the dynasty's lawyers. A source familiar with the reviews described one heir as barely maintaining an active subscription, a status the heir did not dispute on the grounds that he had only recently learned he had one.

You do not keep a person for life, you keep them for as long as they justify the rate. The mistake the old families make is confusing the grief of the moment with the balance sheet of forever. We are simply asking everyone to be honest, before the next generation arrives, that it is a deal, and deals expire. — Dana Whitfield

Elder-law attorneys describe the shift as inevitable and quietly efficient, noting that it converts the awkward matter of a family falling out into a straightforward matter of a contract not being renewed. The planners have declined to say whether the dying clients themselves are also on a subscription, citing only that everyone is, technically, and that the terms have never been fully disclosed.
