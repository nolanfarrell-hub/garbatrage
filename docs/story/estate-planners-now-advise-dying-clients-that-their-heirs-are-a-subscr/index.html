Inheritance Is Now A Subscription; An Heir Named Edwin Was Notified His Share Lapsed

A memo titled 'Blood Is Thick But Cash Is Colder' advises rich families to treat their children like streaming subscriptions. One heir was informed his share has lapsed. He did not dispute it. He did not know he had one.

OZ Omar Zayed
Estate & Governance Desk · Trusts 2026-11-08 · 2 min read

There was a time when a family fortune was passed down like a casserole recipe. From one set of hands to the next. On the assumption that the hands belonged to someone you actually liked. That is over.

Estate planners have a new model. They call the heir a subscription. Like HBO. Like a gym membership you forgot you had. The heir must be reviewed annually. Did the heir show up to the family events? Was the heir nice to the lawyers? Did the heir contribute to the dynasty? If not, the subscription is not renewed. No hard feelings. Just a letter saying your access has been terminated.

Edwin Van Horn III was informed at brunch that his inheritance has been paused. He asked what that meant. The lawyer explained that his status was 'in review.' Edwin asked review for what. The lawyer said for value. Edwin asked what value. The lawyer said the value of Edwin. Edwin, who has never held a job, did not have an answer. The subscription was not renewed.

You do not keep a person for life, you keep them for as long as they justify the rate. The mistake the old families make is confusing the grief of the moment with the balance sheet of forever. We are simply asking everyone to be honest, before the next generation arrives, that it is a deal, and deals expire. — Dana Whitfield

Edwin Van Horn III now gets a monthly statement showing what he would have inherited if he had been a better grandson. He looks at it every day. He does not know how to appeal. There is no appeal. The subscription just ends.
