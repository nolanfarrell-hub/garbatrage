Fund Manager Buys Exactly What He Bought Yesterday at the Same Time as Everyone Else, Calls It Strategic Discipline

Index vehicles now do nearly all their buying at the day's final auction, a practice regulators call elegant and investors call 4:00 PM.

DWDana Whitfield
Portfolio Strategy 2026-11-12 · 2 min read

Avery Loomis manages $27 billion in index strategies and has not made a discretionary trade since 2023. He describes this as the most sophisticated period of his career. Every afternoon at 3:55, he opens a window on his terminal that shows the closing auction. At 4:00, his algorithm buys exactly what it bought the day before, at exactly the same time as every other algorithm that is also buying exactly what it bought the day before. The closing price is the price. Avery’s job is to arrive at it.

“I used to trade in the morning,” Avery said. “I would watch the open. I would read the news. I would form a view. The view was always wrong. Not wrong in an interesting way. Wrong in the way that a man who has an opinion about a thing he does not control is always wrong. The closing auction does not require a view. The closing auction is a fact. The fact is $147.23. I do not need to believe anything about $147.23. I need to own it.”

The industry calls this “liquidity consolidation.” Avery calls it “giving up.” He says it with pride. “Giving up is the correct answer,” he said. “The stock market is a machine that produces a number every day. The number is the correct number because the machine says it is the correct number. Fighting the machine is a hobby. I do not have hobbies. I have index funds.”

Avery’s day now ends at 4:01. He makes more money than he did when his day ended at 4:00 and he spent the other 23 hours and 59 minutes worrying about what happened between 4:01 and 4:00 the next day. “My wife asked if I miss the thrill of the trade,” Avery said. “I said the trade was never thrilling. The trade was expensive and I was bad at it. The closing auction is free and I am perfect at it. I do nothing and the market hands me the average. Every single day. The average is up. I do not deserve the average. But the average does not know that.”

“I set my limit order for 3:59 every single day. The closing auction is the only time I know I'm getting the same price as the institutions. That's discipline. That's how you beat the market without trying.”

— a retail investor who sets his alarm for the close
