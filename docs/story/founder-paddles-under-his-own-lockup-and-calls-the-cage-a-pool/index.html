Founder Paddles Under His Own Lockup and Calls the Cage a Pool

Years of underwater shares and the man who rang the bell calls the cage a pool.

MRMarcus Reid
Markets Anchor · NYSE Floor 2026-11-19 · 4 min read

Rafferty ODell, 44, watched his company list, held the ring, and then spent five years dog-paddling under a lockup he calls a cold pool, which is the only pool in America with an ice maker and a vesting schedule. The stock listed at $38 and is now $14, and Rafferty owns 11 percent of a company worth a third of the number on the day he rang the bell. He rang the bell. The bell is the only thing that has gone up.

The lockup was five years to match the earnout, and the earnout was the part of the deal Rafferty said he would never surrender, and then he surrendered it for the pool that now holds him. He tells the analysts the pool is cold, the way a man insists a loan is a swim, and the analysts write down cold, because analysts write down whatever a founder says and the market is a room that believes its nouns.

“You stay in the $14 water long enough and you stop feeling it,' Rafferty told a conference, and the water is down 63 percent, and the feeling he has stopped is the one that kept the staff from quitting during the window the shares still meant something.”

— Marcus Reid

The lockup expires at the end of the quarter. Rafferty has 30 days to decide whether the company is a boat or a battery, and he has spent the year asking the price of the pool rather than whether the pool overvalues the swim. He calls it a cold pool like it is a hardship and not a holding cell he built himself, with the door on the inside, and a bell the bastard rang at the top that has not been right about a single number since.

Rafferty ODell bought the pool.
