Fed Holds Rates, Explains It Depends on the Data, and the Data Has Started Depending on the Fed

The committee left policy unchanged and noted, for the forty-second straight meeting, that it remains data dependent, which sources confirm is the point.

PS Priya Sharma
Washington Bureau · Fed & Rates 2026-11-12 · 2 min read

The Federal Open Market Committee voted to hold the target range at 3-3/4 to 4 percent, then spent the press conference explaining that every decision remains data dependent, a phrase that has now outlived three recessions, one yield curve, and everyone who asked a follow-up.

Economists noted that the data, in turn, has grown dependent on the Fed, producing a closed loop in which the policy depends on the numbers and the numbers depend on the policy, and nobody is willing to break the phone call first.

Committee members cited the need to watch labor, watch prices, and watch the market watch them back. "We are watching," one member said, "and we have reports that we are being watched, and we are prepared to be watched watching."

Households were nonetheless advised to keep their plans flexible, on the understanding that the data, which depends on the Fed, will be released at the Fed's convenience, which depends on the calendar.

"They said the decision depends on the data, and I asked if I was in the data, and they said I was in a category of data they were still watching." — Ray Okafor, Warehouse Picker
