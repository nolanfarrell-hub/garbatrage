Bank Confirms The $34 Charge On A $7 Coffee Is Not A Fee, It Is A Statement Somebody Should Be Making

Regulators have been asking how a six-dollar cappuccino becomes forty-one dollars, and the bank, whose answer it is, is prepared to explain it as punctuation.

CR Claire Reyes
Debt & Fixed Income 2026-11-11 · 2 min read

We obtained an overdraft explanation in which the bank clarified that the difference between what you had and what you spent is not the fee's fault, and that the fee is simply the bank's way of being heard.

The regulator described certain consents as phantom. The bank described the fee as a courtesy. The customer, described by the bank's own records as now carrying negative nine dollars, described nothing, on account of the statement.

We are not charging you for being poor. We are charging you for having kept it from us until the very last possible moment. – Sheila Vance, Retail Banking Executive

The bank reiterated that nobody is ever charged without written, affirmative, documented goodwill, which the customer had, at some point, acknowledged, probably while signing something else.
