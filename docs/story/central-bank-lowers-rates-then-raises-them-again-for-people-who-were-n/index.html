Central Bank Lowers Rates, Then Raises Them Again For People Who Were Not Paying Attention

The policy committee voted to ease policy, celebrated the decision briefly, and then voted, in a second, slightly angrier meeting, to re-tighten for the subset of borrowers who had not yet received word, a cohort the bank has branded 'late-arriving households' and which it now charges a surcharge for the privilege of timing.

PS Priya Sharma
Washington Bureau 2026-11-07 · 2 min read

The first decision was lovely. Rates came down, mortgages flashed red, the bank's economists high-fived over a graph going in the direction people like. The relief lasted roughly as long as it took the bank to notice the tape again and wonder whether it had been too nice.

The committee therefore convened a second session, hours later, at which it formally restored the previous rate for anyone who had not yet benefited, on the theory that a rate cut only counts if it is felt, and a family that has not yet felt the cut has, in the bank's taxonomy, been cutting against the will of the bank.

We are not raising rates. We are correcting a distribution error. The people who read the news in time get the economy they were promised. The people who read the news at their own pace get a confirming message from us, and a reminder about who sets the pace.” — Elena Marsh

The bank insists the move is not punishment, merely synchronization, and notes that all households, regardless of when they were updated to the higher rate, are now facing the same rate, which it calls the beginning of equity.
