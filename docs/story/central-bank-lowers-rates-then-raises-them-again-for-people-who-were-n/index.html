Gary Webster's Coffee Went Cold While The Central Bank Cut, Then Uncut, His Mortgage Rate

The bank cut rates at 10 a.m., took a victory lap, and at 2 p.m. raised them back for everybody who had not yet called their mortgage broker. The bank calls this 'synchronization.' The rest of us call it getting the rate cut memo a day late and a dollar short.

PS Priya Sharma
Washington Bureau 2026-11-07 · 2 min read

Let me tell you about the day the central bank decided to help people, changed its mind, and then charged the slow people for the help they almost got.

At 10 a.m. the committee voted to cut rates. People cheered. The internet did a little dance. Somebody in Ohio named Gary Webster actually picked up the phone to call his lender. But Gary Webster was not fast enough. The bank, looking at the tape at lunch, decided it had been too generous, reconvened, and voted to put the rate back up for anybody who had not yet locked in.

So Gary Webster, who had been sitting there with his coffee, feeling good for roughly three hours, now gets to pay more than the guy next door who checked his phone in the bathroom. The bank calls Gary a 'late-arriving household' and charges him a surcharge for the timing. The bank does not apologize. The bank says this is equity.

“If you did not check your phone in the bathroom, you missed the rate. That is your problem, not the bank's. The guy next door checked his phone. He got the rate. I checked my phone. I got the rate. Gary did not. Gary pays more. That is how fast works.”

— a borrower who checked his phone in the bathroom

Gary Webster is now paying a higher rate than he was before the rate cut. He has not left his kitchen. He is staring at the letter. The letter says CONGRATULATIONS on top and then, in smaller print, explains the congratulations are no longer valid. Gary Webster has been out-earned by a guy who checks his email during dinner.
