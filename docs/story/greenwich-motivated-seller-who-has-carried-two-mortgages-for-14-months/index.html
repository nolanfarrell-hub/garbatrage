Greenwich Motivated Seller Who Has Carried Two Mortgages for 14 Months Begins Accepting Applications for the Role of Buyer

A Greenwich Colonial that has sat on the market for 14 months now treats prospective buyers as job applicants for the privilege of assuming the seller's debt, as existing-home sales hit a 16-year low across all price tiers.

PS Priya Sharma
Estate & Governance Desk · Trusts 2026-11-16 · 2 min read

Existing-home sales fell to a 16-year low of 3.75 million units annualized in October, with declines recorded across every price tier including the million-dollar-plus segment, according to National Association of Realtors data — news that reached Gerald Masterson as he stood in the foyer of his Greenwich Colonial, a house that has been on the market for 14 months.

Masterson greets open-house visitors with a laminated FAQ titled "Yes, I Know It's Been 14 Months." The document explains that prospective buyers are not merely purchasing a five-bedroom Colonial on a half-acre lot; they are "applying for the role of buyer," a position that requires a non-contingent offer and a willingness to assume both Masterson's primary mortgage and the bridge loan on the new property he purchased before the old one sold. "I am not selling a house," Masterson told a couple who arrived Saturday. "I am interviewing candidates for the privilege of carrying my debt."

The listing has become a local attraction. Teenagers from the neighborhood photograph the sign as it evolves: "PRICE REDUCED" gave way to "WILL CONSIDER OFFERS THAT ARE NOT ACTIVELY INSULTING," and most recently to "OPEN HOUSE — SERIOUS INQUIRIES ONLY, DEFINITION OF SERIOUS NEGOTIABLE." Masterson has begun exploring landmark designation for the property, reasoning that a preservation society would be obligated to maintain a structure deemed historically significant.

"At this point I am not a seller. I am a museum of my own poor timing, and the admission fee is a non-contingent offer at 85 percent of asking." — Gerald Masterson, Greenwich homeowner

At press time, Masterson was drafting a historic-district application that would, if approved, transfer the lawn-mowing obligation to the Greenwich Preservation Trust, freeing him to focus on the interior mold that has spread across the master bathroom since he stopped paying the house's electric bill.
