Patricia Okonkwo's Fund Owns Nothing And Is Therefore Outperforming Everything

A new fund holds zero assets. Zero stocks. Zero cash. It tracks the market's score if the market had declined to play. The management fee remains. The fund says it has outperformed everybody who owns anything, because nothing cannot go down.

TNTessa Nguyen
Consumer & Earnings Desk · New York 2026-11-08 · 2 min read

There is a fund now that does not own anything. Not a share. Not a bond. Not a nickel in a drawer. Nothing. The logic is unstoppable. If you own something and that thing goes down, you go down. If you own nothing, nothing goes down. You cannot lose. The fund charges a fee for this.

The manager, a woman named Patricia Okonkwo, explained it on a call. She said the fund tracks the market the way you track a noise you hear outside at night, from a distance, out of caution, not because you intend to participate. The fund does not participate. It observes. From a safe place. The safe place is where the money was before you gave it to the fund.

Doug Milliken put his retirement savings into this fund. He does not fully understand it. He knows the quarterly statement says his balance has not changed. It says the same number every time. He called to ask if the fund is doing anything. They said the fund is doing exactly what it is supposed to do, which is nothing. Doug asked if that is good. They said nothing cannot be bad. Doug agreed and hung up.

“Nothing cannot go down. I have been investing for twenty years and nobody ever explained it that simply. If your fund goes down, you bought the wrong thing. If your fund is nothing, it stays the same. That is better than going down.”

— Doug, who stopped checking his balance

The fund has already filed for a second vehicle. It will own negative holdings. If something goes up, the fund goes down. Patricia says this is the next frontier. Doug Milliken has not signed up for that one yet. He is still trying to figure out the first one.
