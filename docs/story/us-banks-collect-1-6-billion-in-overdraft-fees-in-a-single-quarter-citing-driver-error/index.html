US Banks Collect $1.6 Billion in Overdraft Fees in a Single Quarter, Citing Driver Error

Revenue rose 9.4% to a four-year high as lenders attributed the bonanza to a chronic shortage of balance on the part of account holders.

DW Dana Whitfield
Consumer Finance Reporter 2026-11-15 · 2 min read

Domestic lenders collected $1.6 billion in overdraft fees in the third quarter, up 9.4% from the prior three months and the highest quarterly total in four years, according to figures confirmed by KBRA Financial Intelligence.

JPMorgan Chase and Wells Fargo alone charged $590 million, an amount roughly equal to the rest of the twenty largest banks combined. A federal rule that would have capped the fee at $5 per transaction was approved, then rolled back, in the same year, freeing lenders to pursue what several described as the fee's natural ceiling.

We do not raise the fee because we are greedy. We raise it because the account keeps presenting itself.

At Raleigh-based First Citizens, overdraft revenue rose 200% versus the start of 2025, the largest increase among the fifty largest lenders, a climb the bank attributed to a growing local conviction that money belongs where the courage to spend it is absent.

Regulators described the quarter as an early warning sign for consumer health. Banks described it as an unusually legible earnings statement, and one executives expect to repeat.

— Vivian Koontz, Head of Deposit Optimization
