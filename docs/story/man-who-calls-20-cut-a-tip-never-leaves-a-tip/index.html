Man Who Calls 20% Cut a Tip Never Leaves a Tip

Cecil Hartigan takes $10 billion off the top and sees no reason the waiter costs him.

OZOmar Zayed
Corporate Governance Desk 2026-11-19 · 4 min read

Cecil Hartigan, 68, of Greenwich, calls the 20 percent he takes from every fund a tip, and has spent four decades maintaining that the tip is money he does not actually have to tip anyone with. Over his career that 20 percent has come to roughly $10 billion. He has never once left one for a waiter, a driver, or the people who built the companies the tip is taken from, and he tells the people who ask that the tip does not work like that.

Mr. Hartigan earns the percentage whether the fund that pays it makes money or not. It is calculated on the capital a fund raises, not on what it returns, a detail he has described as the real generosity. When a portfolio company the fund owned went bankrupt last year, the 20 percent on the sum that entered the company was already collected.

“You leave the 20 on the table and the good money finds it,' Mr. Hartigan told the industry dinner. The 20 was not left on any table. It was wired to a management entity that does not appear in the fund's marketing materials, which he has called getting the tip while the room is still open.”

He has two homes, a staff, and a standing reservation at a restaurant he has never once had to describe as expensive. When asked what the money actually buys, Mr. Hartigan said the money buys the position from which the tip is taken, and that the position is the whole point. The whole thing functions on the premise that a waiter who never receives a fucking tip will keep serving, because the waiter does not know what the percentage was.

Cecil Hartigan keeps the tip.
