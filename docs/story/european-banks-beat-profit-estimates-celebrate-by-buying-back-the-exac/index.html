European Banks Beat Profit Estimates, Celebrate By Buying Back The Exact Shame That Built The Profit

UBS and BNP Paribas delivered strong quarters and accelerated buybacks, resolving the eternal question of what to do with gains.

JS Jake Sullivan
Banking & Credit Reporter 2026-11-14 · 2 min read

ZURICH—UBS reported a 8% rise in third-quarter net profit to $2.69 billion and pulled its share-buyback program forward, while BNP Paribas unveiled a buyback of its own, prompting analysts to observe that the continent’s banks have finally solved the mystery of what to do with money they are embarrassed to have made.

The banks attributed the results to strong client engagement, disciplined pricing, and, in one division they did not name, to having made the customers feel like it was all their idea. Executives framed the buybacks as both a return of capital and an acknowledgment that the profit, while real, was best distributed somewhere other than the balance sheet.

UBS said it would complete its $3 billion repurchase program by February, ahead of schedule, a timeline bankers said was driven by the realization that the shares, like the clients, were not going to get cheaper if you waited. European rivals were reported to be watching closely, taking notes, and preparing to feel the same way about their own recent good news.

“We beat expectations, and the first thing we thought was that we should probably give a portion of that back before it has a chance to be explained.” — Henrik Ostergaard, Chief Financial Officer

At press time, the profits were said to be in a fund, the fund was in a committee, and the committee was reported to be buying back the whole thing as a gesture of goodwill toward the people who made it.
