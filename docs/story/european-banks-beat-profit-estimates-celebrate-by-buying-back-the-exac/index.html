European Banks Beat Profit Estimates, Celebrate By Buying Back Exactly As Much Of Themselves As They Can Before Anyone Asks Where It Came From

UBS and BNP Paribas delivered strong quarters and accelerated buybacks, resolving the eternal question of what to do with gains.

JSJake Sullivan
Banking & Credit Reporter 2026-11-14 · 2 min read

ZURICH—UBS reported an 8 percent rise in third-quarter net profit to $2.69 billion and pulled its share-buyback program forward, while BNP Paribas unveiled a buyback of its own, prompting analysts to observe that the continent's banks have finally solved the mystery of what to do with money they are embarrassed to have made.

"We beat expectations, and the first thing we thought was that they should probably give a portion of that back before it has a chance to be explained," said a customer of one of the banks for 31 years, repeating the CFO's line back to a reporter. "The word bonus was not used, but you could smell what it was about to become."

Lena Gottlieb, 52, a customer of one of the banks for 31 years, said she received a notice that her account fees were going up the same week the bank announced its buyback. "The letter said the fee adjustment was necessary to maintain the quality of service I deserve," Gottlieb said. "I called and asked if any of the buyback money could maintain the quality of service I deserve. The woman on the phone said the buyback and the fees were in different departments. I said they were in different parts of my bank account."

“I called the bank and asked if any of the buyback money could lower my twelve-dollar monthly fee, and the woman said the buyback and the fee were in different departments, and I told her that made sense because a department that buys things should not have to know about a department that charges things.”

— a customer who understands departmental separation

At press time, the profits were said to be in a fund, the fund was in a committee, and the committee was reported to be buying back the whole thing as a gesture of goodwill toward the people who made it, none of whom were in the room.
