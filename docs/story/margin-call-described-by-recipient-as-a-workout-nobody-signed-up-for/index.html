Margin Call Described by Recipient as 'A Workout Nobody Signed Up For'

Phineas Grant sold a quarter of his art to cover margin, says market made him fitter.

CRClaire Reyes
Debt & Fixed Income, plus consumer credit 2026-11-19 · 4 min read

Phineas Grant, 58, was margin called on Wednesday and has described the call as a workout he did not sign up for, before selling a quarter of his art collection to complete it. The call was for $12 million in collateral on a mortgage-style loan against his portfolio, which the bank said had fallen below the loan's coverage ratio for the second time this year.

Mr. Grant transferred the $12 million in cash by lunch. To do so he sold a Rothko he had owned since 1998, and has described the painting's departure as the market forcing him to make room. He has confirmed the room is a smaller office he was going to rent anyway.

“It is a good lesson, that the money has to move when the number moves,' Mr. Grant told the dinner party. The dinner party was in the smaller office. He described the reduced square footage as the market helping him decide what mattered, and the bank confirmed it did not intend to help him decide anything.”

He has asked whether the margin call could happen again next month. The bank said it could. Mr. Grant said that was fine, that a workout a man can schedule is a workout a man can train for, and that he already had the trainers, which he said was the shit payment he was making for the privilege of being margin called on his own terms.

Phineas Grant foots the bill.
