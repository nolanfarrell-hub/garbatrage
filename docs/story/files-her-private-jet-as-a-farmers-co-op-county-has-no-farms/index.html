Files Her Private Jet as a Farmers' Co-op, County Has No Farms

Jet qualifies for ag fuel credit; no crop is grown, and it costs $6 million.

EMElena Marsh
Head of Research 2026-11-19 · 4 min read

Cornelia Vance, 63, has registered her Gulfstream as the Tri-County Grain Co-op, Inc., a farm entity that owns no grain, no silos, and no co-op land, and that has just saved her $6 million in fuel tax, which she calls the harvest. The county the co-op is registered in has not grown a crop since 1961. The co-op's registered address is the hangar.

Farming is a pussy and I am pounding it. The state opened a hole in the tax code big enough to fly a jet through, and I pushed my whole plane through it and did not once look back. I am a grain co-op the way a bank robber is a neighborhood watchman. Do you think that clerk gives a shit what I grow? The clerk wants his checkbox, I want my $6 million, and we just tenanted each other's fantasy. Farmer my ass. I farm the taxman's dumb cock.

The co-op's only shareholder is Ms. Vance. Its only asset is the jet. It has submitted fuel-purchase logs to the state in the form of a flight manifest showing six round trips to a county that contains, according to the same state, no registered farms, which the co-op has called a data-entry gap on the state's part.

Ms. Vance described the $6 million as the harvest and the jet as the tractor, and when asked how much grain the co-op grows she said the co-op grows margins. She said it without smiling, which made it worse, because she meant it, and the county clerk who approved the registration has confirmed she checked the box marked farm vehicle and does not currently see a reason to uncheck it. The co-op farms the one crop that grows when nobody is looking, and Ms. Vance has never once been confused about what that crop is, which is fucking money.
