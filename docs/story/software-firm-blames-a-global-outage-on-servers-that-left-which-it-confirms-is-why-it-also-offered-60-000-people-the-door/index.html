Software Firm Blames a Global Outage on Servers That Left, Which It Confirms Is Why It Also Offered 60,000 People the Door

The email, the teams, the meetings all down at once. The company said no layoffs were involved, only upgrades.

PS Priya Sharma
Technology Correspondent 2026-11-13 · 2 min read

A major software firm's products went dark for much of the world this week, an outage the company initially attributed to demand before revising it to the more honest explanation that servers had become unexpectedly unavailable.

The revision was praised for its candor, coming as it did from a company that had just offered voluntary buyouts to 60,000 employees, a number it described not as a reduction but as an upgrade in the direction of the machines.

Users who could not reach their own files were told the firm's priorities were clear, and that a service that cannot be reached during the fiscal period is still performing its most important function, which is generating the meeting about why it cannot be reached.

No connection between the outages and the exits was made, because, the company noted, establishing one would require the very systems that were, at that moment, unavailable.

We did not fire anyone. We automated the leaving, and the leaving, like the email, is temporarily out of order. It will be back, smaller. — Nia Colter, Chief Operating Officer, Cascade Software Outfitters
