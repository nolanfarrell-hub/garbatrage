Berkshire Hathaway Spends $9.6 Billion Buying Stock From The Only Person Willing To Sell It: Itself

The conglomerate more than doubled repurchases while insurance underwriting tumbled 43%, describing the buyback as an expression of long-term conviction in a security whose only shareholders are, increasingly, the same company.

MRMarcus Reid
Markets Anchor · Industrials 2026-11-09 · 7 min read

Gary lives in Omaha, and his father told him in 1997 that you buy Berkshire Hathaway and then you die, and that is it, that is the plan. Gary did that. Gary is now sixty-one years old, and his 401‑k is, by his own estimate, me and that one company. He says this proudly, like he has discovered the only safe chair in a burning building.

What Gary does not know, and what nobody in Omaha is going to tell him, is that the company has spent $9.6 billion buying itself back, because the only person who wants to buy the stock is the stock. The operating profits fell. The underwriting fell. The insurance thing everybody counts on fell. And the company bought $9.6 billion of itself, because when you are Berkshire, your own shares are the only supplier that never says no.

Gary is the buyback. Gary, with his 401‑k statement and his faith in the man from the newspaper, is the counterparty. The company buys, and Gary holds, and the price stays up, and Gary does not know that he is the inventory. He thinks he is the customer.

A man who worked in the same building as Gary for fourteen years, named Phil, sold his Berkshire shares in March to put a down payment on a townhouse. Phil now checks the price every day, because the townhouse is the same price it was in March, and the stock is up, and Phil has to live with that, which he knew he would. He told me he knew he would. He says it again at the mailbox every time he sees Gary.

“I told Phil the buyback is the only thing keeping my townhouse worth anything. The stock is up 14% and the house is flat, and I call that a fair trade — the company knows what it is worth better than the realtor does.”

— Gary, who checks Zillow and the ticker from the same bench
