Sanitation Firm Posts Record Margins, Credits A Material It Calls, Affectionately, The Load

In a quarter of logistics excellence, the hauler reported its most profitable run ever, a margin it attributes to a consistency of collection that has, for some of its customers, become their entire curbside biography.

EM Elena Marsh
Head of Research 2026-11-11 · 2 min read

Marcus Blythe’s garbage has not been picked up in 17 days. The hauler keeps driving past. Marcus keeps putting the can out. The can is now a neighborhood landmark. Kids draw on it. A possum lives behind it. The possum understands the situation better than the hauler does, because the possum does not have an account number.

“I called customer service and they said my route is operating at record efficiency,” Marcus said. “I said my can is full. They said the route is the most profitable in the region. I said my can has a possum. They said the possum is not in the system. I said the possum is on my can. They said the can is at the curb, the curb is the collection point, and the collection point is operating at a 97 percent service rate. I said which 97 percent am I in. They said the 3 percent. I said the 3 percent of what. They said the 3 percent we are not collecting.”

CEO Gordon Heath attributes the record margins to “load consistency,” which is the term the company uses to describe customers who generate the same volume of the same material on the same schedule, so the truck never has to guess. Marcus Blythe’s garbage, by contrast, is what the industry calls “a variable.” The company has no interest in variables. Variables cost money. Variables are why the margin was not higher.

“I asked them to at least take the possum,” Marcus said. “They said the possum is not in the scope of collection services. The possum is a wildlife issue. I said the possum is on top of my garbage. They said the garbage is a collection issue. The possum is a municipality issue. I said what about the raccoon. They said I have a raccoon now too. I said yes. They said the raccoon is also a municipality issue. I hung up and looked at the can. The possum and the raccoon were looking at me. We all understood the same thing: the hauler was not coming.”

“The possum has a name now. His name is Gordon, after the CEO. Gordon the possum lives on my garbage and pays no fee. Gordon the possum does not have an account number. Gordon the possum is the most successful customer this hauler has ever had.” – Marcus Blythe, Accounts Receivable, Three Weeks Uncollected
