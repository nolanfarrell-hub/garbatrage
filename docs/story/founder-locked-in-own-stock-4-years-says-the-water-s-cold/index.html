Founder Locked in Own Stock 4 Years, Says the Water's Cold

He proposed the lockup to show confidence. He has spent three years inside.

MRMarcus Reid
Markets Anchor · NYSE Floor 2026-11-19 · 4 min read

Rafferty O'Dell, 44, proposed the four-year lockup on his own company's shares as a sign of confidence, and has since spent three of those years inside it, unable to sell a single share. The stock listed at $38 and trades at $14. His stake is worth $41 million on paper and cannot be touched until the fourth quarter of next year.

The lockup is my pool. I built it. Do you understand how rich you have to be to have your own pool? You have to be rich enough that the water wants you in it. The market said it wanted me, so I got in. Now the market is saying it wants me out, and turns out the lock was my idea. That is not a loss. That is me trusting the market for four years and the market showing me who built the damn pool.

The board has declined twice to let him sell early. Mr. O'Dell has described the board as the lifeguard, which he hired. He does not see the irony. He has asked whether the company can raise its own cash by issuing new shares, which would dilute his stake further, and he described that as heating the pool with the water in the pool.

When it was pointed out that this would make the pool colder, he said that was the point, that he had spent three years in the cold and that the only way to get warm was to trust the market to make it colder, because the market never gives you the warm thing you are already holding. He said it the way a man says it who has said it before and will say it again, at every conference, into every microphone, until the lock he built empties and he finds out what was in it, the asshole.

Rafferty ODell pays to swim in it.
