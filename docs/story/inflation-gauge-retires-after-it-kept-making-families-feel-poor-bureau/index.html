Inflation Gauge Retires After It Kept Making Families Feel Poor, Bureau Cites Emotional Toll On The Data

The Bureau announced it will cease publishing its headline price index, explaining that the number, while technically accurate, had developed a habit of arriving at a bad time and making whole households feel bad about themselves. Officials said the series declined to comment further, citing the toll.

PS Priya Sharma
Washington Bureau 2026-11-03 · 2 min read

There is a number, and then there is a number that shows up at a family's kitchen table and tells them they cannot afford a thing they just bought. The Bureau has decided that the second kind of number is no way to run a country, and, more importantly, no way to run a morning.

The index, officials explained, was not wrong; it was hurtful, and those are very different categories, and only one of them has ever been solved. The publication schedule will instead feature what the Bureau calls affirmation data, a new series constructed entirely from things that are true and also nice, beginning with the observation that the weather, considered broadly, remains free.

Reaction from economists was muted, then loud, then quiet again for tax reasons. Those who had spent careers defending the number now defended its absence, arguing that a country which no longer hears the bad news is, by a strict reading of the news, a country with less bad news in it. The Treasury called the decision courageous.

The Bureau added that it had not actually stopped measuring prices; it had merely stopped being the one to say them out loud, and had passed that responsibility, with a note of sympathy, to the families themselves. Early feedback suggests families prefer the Bureau's version of events, which some have called the first piece of federal data they have ever fully believed.

The index will return, the Bureau said, when the country is in a better place to hear it, a date the Bureau declined to specify but which analysts, reading between the lines, have penciled in as never.
