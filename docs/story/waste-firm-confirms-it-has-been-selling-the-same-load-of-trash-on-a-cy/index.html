Garbage Company Has Been Selling the Same Trash for Four Quarters, Calls It a Circular Economy, Calls the Trash 'Load A'

Gary Petrosino's job is to sell a pile of garbage. He has sold the same pile of garbage four times. His boss calls it steady-state inventory. His wife thinks he is joking. He is not.

MRMarcus Reid
Markets Anchor · NYSE Floor 2026-11-06 · 2 min read

Gary Petrosino works for a waste management company. His job is to sell garbage. Not the garbage that gets picked up every Tuesday. That garbage goes to a landfill. Gary sells the other garbage. The garbage that does not go anywhere. The garbage that stays. The garbage that Gary keeps selling, over and over again, to the same people, who keep buying it.

The garbage is called Load A. Load A was collected in January. It was sold in January. It was recounted in April. It was sold again in April. It was weighed in July. It was sold again in July. It was inventoried in October. It was sold again in October. Load A has now been sold four times. Load A has never left the facility. Load A is the same garbage it was in January. But Load A is revenue, and revenue is real, and revenue is what matters.

“Load A has been sold four times and never left the building. That is not fraud. That is consistency. Four quarters of the same revenue is a track record, and you cannot build a track record on garbage that keeps leaving.”

— Gary, four quarters on the same load

Gary's wife asked him what he did at work today. Gary said he sold Load A. She asked if Load A was new. Gary said Load A was the same Load A he sold last quarter. She asked if anyone noticed. Gary said nobody noticed because Load A was in a spreadsheet, and spreadsheets do not have smell, and Load A's smell is the only way to tell it is the same garbage.

Load A's quarterly "revenue growth" was cited in the company's earnings report. The company raised guidance. Analysts asked what drove the growth. The company said Load A. Analysts asked what Load A was. The company said it was a load. Analysts asked if it was the same load as last quarter. The company said that was not the kind of question analysts ask about revenue.

Gary came home. He ate dinner. He watched TV. He will sell Load A again next quarter. Load A will be there. Load A is always there. Load A is the most reliable thing in Gary's life. Load A is garbage. Load A is revenue. Load A is both. Load A is everything.
