Bank Confirms $35 'Courtesy' Fee For Letting You Be $7 Short For Six Minutes

Tanya from Detroit put $7 in gas at 7:13 in the morning. Her paycheck hit at 7:19. The bank posted the gas before the check, charged her $35 for the privilege of being$7 short for six minutes. Then it called the $35 a courtesy.

CRClaire Reyes
Debt & Fixed Income 2026-11-10 · 2 min read

Tanya from Detroit put forty-two dollars in regular gas at the Shell on Livernois at seven-thirteen in the morning. Her paycheck, which is the Paylocity one she can see pending on Thursday but cannot touch until Friday, hit the ledger at seven-nineteen. Six minutes. The bank posted the forty-two dollars out the door before the eight-hundred-and-thirty-seven dollars came in, because the bank said the out had arrived first, and the out does not cut in line, and the in, having arrived second, must wait, and the waiting, for a deposit, is a courtesy, and the courtesy, like a handshake between two people who have already decided one of them is paying, costs thirty-five dollars.

Tanya asked the teller, a woman named Cheryl who has been at the branch for eleven years and did not write the rule and cannot change the rule and has had this conversation eighty times this week, whether the bank could simply wait the six minutes. Cheryl laughed. She laughed the way you laugh when a child asks if the dog can have the whole steak. Cheryl explained that the computer does not wait, and that the computer does not know Tanya, and that if the computer waited for Tanya, the computer would have to wait for everybody, and then what would the computer be. A person, Tanya said. Cheryl laughed again. The second laugh was shorter.

“Tanya told Cheryl the $35 is the cost of the six minutes. The computer does not wait for anybody and I do not want a computer that waits for me. I want a computer that knows the time is the time.”

— Tanya, who paid $35 for punctuality

The bank, when reached for comment, confirmed that it does not post deposits before withdrawals because that would be rude to the withdrawal, which arrived first and which the bank has already promised to honor, and that honoring a withdrawal before the money is there to cover it is the very definition of a courtesy, which it is, if courtesy means we took eleven billion dollars in overdraft revenue last year and the word for that, in the Tellurian language the computer speaks, is Tanya.
