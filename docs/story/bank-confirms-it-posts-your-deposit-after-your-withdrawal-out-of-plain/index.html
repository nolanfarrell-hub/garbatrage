Bank Confirms It Posts Your Deposit After Your Withdrawal Out Of Plain Courtesy, The Courtesy Costs Thirty-Five Dollars, Renewed Daily

The bank, addressing why a customer's paycheck is credited only after that morning's debits are processed, explained it is simply good manners. An overdraft follows as a courtesy, the bank confirmed, and the courtesy, like most things that are free, bills.

CR Claire Reyes
Debt & Fixed Income 2026-11-10 · 2 min read

You know there is a reason they post the deposit after the withdrawal, and the reason is that the bank likes to see the withdrawal happen, you know, with its morning coffee, and the deposit retire to the back of the line and wait its turn, which is the polite thing for a deposit to do, and which generates a fee, and the fee is the bank saying thanks to the deposit for waiting.

The bank explained its process to me like a man explaining how he claps once and the lights go out: the deposit arrives, you know, it knocks, and the bank says hold on, and then it goes ahead and pays out the things that were already lined up, the rent, the bill, the three-dollar coffee that will now cost thirty-five, and only then does it get to the deposit, which has by this point been waiting so patiently that the bank decides to charge it for the wait, out of respect.

I have seen a man be late to his own paycheck, and I have seen a paycheck be late to a man, and the difference is that one of them apologizes, and the other one charges interest. That is just the way it is. – Jake Sullivan, Breakfast Anchor, on the bounce of modern banking

Regulators, who passed a rule once limiting this exact practice, had the rule removed in 2025, and the bank, noting the absence, framed its processing order as a courtesy rather than a fee, on the grounds that the customer was welcome to deposit earlier, get a different job, or, in the documented case of at least one customer, simply never be three hundred dollars short on a Tuesday again, which the bank conceded was technically an option.

The bank set a record for overdraft revenue, which it does not call a record but a warm, running thanks to its most reliable customers, and it notes that curiosity remains strong among new depositors, who tend to learn the texture of the practice within the first thirty-five dollars, of which the bank, letting the topic rest, says only that it is genuinely appreciative.
